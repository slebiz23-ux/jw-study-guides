¶1. The Bible is an ancient book. Its writing was started some 3,500 years ago, and it was completed nearly 2,000 years ago. And yet "the word of God" found within its pages is alive and powerful today. (Heb. 4:12; 2 Tim. 3:16, 17) Millions of people testify that the Bible has helped them to cope with life in this modern world.

(1 Tim. 4:10; Rom. 16:26, 27) Second, the Bible is written in a way that reveals timeless principles. These principles remain beneficial no matter what era a person lives in or what challenges a person faces.

Paragraphs 4–5 · What Are Bible Principles?
Question 4
What are Bible principles?

Suggested Answer

Bible principles are basic truths on which God's laws are based. They could be described as the reason behind the law. While specific laws may be limited to a particular time or situation, principles reflect Jehovah's thinking more broadly. Principles are timeless—they do not expire or become outdated.

Key distinctions:

  • Laws = specific commands, sometimes for a particular era or situation
  • Principles = the why behind the law; timeless; broadly applicable

Supporting scriptures: Matt. 22:37 Ps. 119:111 Isa. 40:8

💬 Comment: "I found it helpful to think of principles as the 'why' behind the law. Once you know the why, you can apply the same thinking to hundreds of situations the law never directly addresses."
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah doesn't just issue commands—He explains His thinking. That reveals a God who wants relationship and understanding, not just compliance.
Paragraph 4

¶4. Bible principles are basic truths on which God's laws are based. At times, a principle may be clearly stated within the law. (Matt. 22:37) Principles are, however, superior to specific laws. Specific laws may be limited in application—given for a particular time or situation. But Bible principles reflect Jehovah's thinking in a broader sense. Principles could be described as the reason behind the law. The principles on which they are based can be applied in a number of different circumstances and are timeless. (Ps. 119:111) Laws may need to change, but principles do not expire or become outdated.—Isa. 40:8.

Question 5
How can we illustrate the difference between laws and principles?

Suggested Answer

The article uses the illustration of a mother telling a child: "Don't touch the stove." The law is specific—don't touch the stove. But the principle is: don't touch anything that could burn you. That principle extends to an iron, a heater, a campfire—anywhere, anytime. As the child grows, the narrow rule fades, but the principle never does.

Similarly, Bible laws are like specific applications. The principles behind them apply across cultures, time periods, and technologies that didn't exist when the Bible was written.

💬 Comment: "The stove illustration really clicked for me. A specific rule ages out—but the principle behind it stays forever. That's why the Bible speaks to smartphones and social media even though those didn't exist."
📌 Ministry connection: This is a great point for people who feel the Bible is too old to be practical. It doesn't need to address every specific technology—its principles cover all of them.
Paragraph 5

¶5. To help us better understand the difference between laws and principles, consider this illustration. A mother may say to a small child, 'Don't touch the stove.' That is a command or a law. But the principle, or thinking, behind that law is: Don't touch something that is hot; otherwise, you will get hurt. That principle is not just limited to a stove—it could be applied to a clothing iron, a heater, or any other hot item. That principle does not just apply in the family's home but applies elsewhere too. Of course, as the child grows older, he will probably use the stove. However, he will still need to be careful. So the law may change, but the basic principle behind it remains.

A law may change, but the basic principle behind it remains (See paragraph 5)
💡 Application: Think about rules your parents gave you as a child. Many of those specific rules no longer apply—but the principles behind them (safety, respect, kindness) still guide you daily. Bible principles work the same way.
Paragraphs 6–9 · Why Are Bible Principles Important?
Question 6
(a) What does Jehovah provide for us in his Word? (b) How does Jehovah dignify us?

Suggested Answer

(a) Jehovah provides both specific laws (clear commands to protect us from danger) Jas. 2:11 and the understanding of the principles behind those laws—covering the many life areas where no specific law exists.
(b) By providing principles rather than an exhaustive list of rules, Jehovah treats us as mature adults. He gives us the freedom—and the responsibility—to make decisions that prove our love for him. This is true dignity. Gal. 5:13
💬 Comment: "I appreciate that Jehovah doesn't treat us like little children who need a rule for every situation. By giving us principles, he's saying he trusts us to think—and that trust is a form of love."
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah is a God who dignifies us. He could have issued thousands of specific rules, but instead He gives us principles and trusts our judgment—that's a respectful, loving God.
Paragraph 6

¶6. Out of love for us, Jehovah gives us specific direction in the form of laws so that we can avoid danger. (Jas. 2:11) Additionally, he helps us to understand the principles, or the thinking, behind those laws. By means of his Word, he provides us with his guidance in many areas of life that may not be governed by specific laws. Bible principles help us to make good decisions in life and to do what is right. By providing these principles, Jehovah dignifies us and allows us the freedom to make decisions that prove our love for him and his standards.—Gal. 5:13.

Question 7
How can we illustrate the value of Bible principles?

Suggested Answer

The article uses road signs as an illustration. Some signs are laws—speed limits, stop signs—with penalties for breaking them. But other signs are warnings: slippery road ahead, animals crossing. An experienced driver uses good judgment when seeing a warning sign and adjusts behavior proactively—even if no law specifically covers that exact situation.

Similarly, Christians strictly avoid what God's Word specifically condemns, but Bible principles also help us avoid the path that leads toward wrongdoing—before we arrive at a specific sin. This requires using our "power of reason." Rom. 12:1, 2

💬 Comment: "The road sign illustration helped me see that Bible principles are like warning signs—they don't just tell us what's illegal; they help us avoid danger in the first place."
📌 Ministry connection: Many people want permission slips for borderline behavior. Understanding principles helps us see why certain things are "warning signs," even if not strictly condemned in a single verse.
Paragraph 7

¶7. Think of road signs that drivers see along highways. Many of these signs inform us of laws that carry penalties for those who disregard them—speed limits, stop signs. However, many other signs alert drivers to possible dangers—slippery road, animals crossing. An experienced driver uses good judgment and drives cautiously when he sees these warning signs. Similarly, while Christians strictly avoid any conduct that is specifically condemned in God's Word, they also avoid any thoughts or actions that could lead them to break God's laws. This calls for good judgment on their part.

In this way, we learn how to use our "power of reason" to serve Jehovah. We prove to ourselves "the good and acceptable and perfect will of God."—Read Romans 12:1, 2.

Paragraphs 10–17 · How Can We Identify Bible Principles?
Question 10
How can we identify Bible principles?

Suggested Answer

We identify Bible principles by searching the Bible for:

  • Lessons that show how Jehovah thinks and feels about matters
  • The reasons behind specific laws ("Why did God give this law?")
  • What a Bible account reveals about Jehovah's character

This requires prayer for wisdom and developing our own thinking ability. Prov. 2:10–12

Good questions to ask: "Why did God give this law? If Jehovah doesn't approve of this conduct, how would He feel about similar conduct? What lesson do I learn from this account, and how can I apply it in my life?"

💬 Comment: "The three questions in paragraph 10 are like a formula I can actually use in personal study. Instead of just reading a passage, I ask: What is the principle? How does it reveal Jehovah's thinking? How can I apply it?"
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Identifying principles means we're learning to see the world through Jehovah's eyes—understanding not just what He commands, but why. That's a deep form of knowing Him.
Paragraph 10

¶10. When we search the Bible for lessons, we find principles that help us to understand how Jehovah thinks and feels about matters. We can also identify principles when we look for the reasons behind certain laws. The more we understand the reasons, the more we understand Jehovah. But in order to understand God's thinking, we must pray to him for help and we must develop our own thinking ability. (Prov. 2:10–12) We can ask ourselves: 'Why did God give this law? If Jehovah does not approve of this conduct, how would he feel about similar conduct? What lesson do I learn from this Bible account, and how can I apply it in my life?'

This principle was stated centuries before films, photographs, or digital devices existed, yet it addresses them all perfectly. The principle endures; the technologies come and go.

Review Question 1
What is a Bible principle?

A Bible principle is a basic truth on which God's laws are based—it is the thinking or reason behind the law. While specific laws may be limited to a particular time, situation, or circumstance, principles reflect Jehovah's broader values and remain applicable in any era. They do not expire or become outdated. (¶4; Isa. 40:8)

💬 Personal comment: "A Bible principle is like the DNA of a law—the core truth that gives it life and makes it applicable far beyond its original stated context."
Review Question 2
How do principles differ from laws?

Laws are specific commands that may be tailored to a particular era, situation, or people. They can change or become obsolete. Principles are the timeless truths behind those laws—broader, deeper, and universally applicable. A law tells us what to do in a specific case; a principle tells us why and enables us to handle countless situations no specific law ever addresses. Example: The law "don't touch the stove" → the principle "don't touch what will burn you." The law may not apply when you're an adult, but the principle still does—everywhere. (¶4–5)

Review Question 3
What are some of the principles Jesus taught in the Sermon on the Mount?

Jesus taught three key principles through laws:

  1. "Do not murder" → Don't hate. Even anger, contempt, and hateful speech violate this principle. Today it applies to gossip, slander, and online harassment. (¶12–13; Matt. 5:21, 22)
  2. "Do not commit adultery" → Don't dwell on immoral thoughts. Jehovah cares about what we let our minds linger on. Today it covers pornography, sexting, and virtual immorality. (¶14–15; Matt. 5:27, 28)
  3. "Love your neighbor" → Love everyone, including enemies. Jehovah's love is universal—no exclusions based on race, nationality, or religion. Today it guides our neutrality, kindness, and forgiveness. (¶16–17; Matt. 5:43, 44)
