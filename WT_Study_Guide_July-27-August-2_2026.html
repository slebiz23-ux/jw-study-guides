🎵 Opening Song 56 — Make the Truth Your Own
FOCUS

Four Bible principles that will help you stay spiritually strong if you decide to get additional education.

“To the extent we have made progress, let us go on walking orderly in this same course.”
Philippians 3:16
Introduction▸
QUESTIONS 1–2

(a) If you choose to get additional education, what do you need to keep doing? (b) What does it mean to “go on walking orderly in this same course”?

(a)

Some in the congregation choose education beyond what's legally required, often hoping it will help them support themselves while continuing to serve Jehovah. Whatever the reason, the real question is how to stay spiritually strong while pursuing it — by continuing to do the same things that have kept you close to Jehovah all along.

(b)

The phrase pictures soldiers marching in an orderly, unified line. It means continuing to serve Jehovah faithfully — the same course you were already on — without slowing to a stop or falling back.

💬 Comment you could share: "What struck me about this illustration is that Paul compares faithful service to marching — you're either moving forward or falling behind, there's no just standing still."
📌 Ministry connection: This works well with someone weighing a big life change — you can point out that Bible principles aren't meant to be paused for a season, they're meant to travel with you.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah doesn't ask for perfection, just steady forward movement — that says a lot about how patient and realistic he is with us.
¶1-2 Some of Jehovah's servants have decided to get additional education that goes beyond the basic schooling mandated by the state, often hoping it will help them find work that supports their service to Jehovah. James 4:8 No doubt you faced tests of faith during your previous years in school. Now you will need to keep walking orderly in this same course — continuing to serve Jehovah faithfully, just as you have already. The Greek verb translated "walking orderly" was often used in a military sense to describe the unified marching of soldiers. If you decide to get additional education, you will need to keep marching forward in Jehovah's service, neither standing still nor retreating.
Maintain a Healthy Spiritual Routine▸
QUESTION 3

As you are getting additional education, what challenge might you face with regard to your daily routine?

Additional education can place new demands on your daily routine. Because doing well matters to you, there's a real risk of quietly treating your service to Jehovah as less important — even neglecting meetings, the ministry, personal study, and prayer.

💬 Comment you could share: "I found it interesting that the article doesn't frame this as an obvious, dramatic slide away from the truth — it's described as something that happens quietly, one skipped habit at a time."
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Revelation 2:4 shows Jehovah notices even a gradual cooling of love, not just outright abandonment — he pays attention to the heart, not just the outward record.
¶3 The challenge. Additional education might place new demands on you that will affect your daily routine. Because you naturally want to do well in your schooling, you could begin to treat your service to Jehovah as of lesser importance. You could even start to neglect Christian meetings, participation in the ministry, personal Bible study, and prayer. Revelation 2:4
QUESTION 4

What can help you to have "plenty to do in the work of the Lord"? 1 Corinthians 15:58

Staying "in motion" spiritually — like a bicycle that only stays upright while moving — is what keeps you steady. That's not just about clocking hours in theocratic activity; it's about priorities. See yourself as Jehovah's servant first and a student second.

💬 Comment you could share: "The bicycle illustration really stood out to me — you don't stay upright by standing still, you stay upright by moving forward."
📌 Ministry connection: Good angle for a student or young professional: ask what keeps them motivated and steady when life gets busy — then relate it to having a clear priority.
¶4 Just as a bicycle stays upright when it is in motion, you will stay upright spiritually if you are in motion, having "plenty to do in the work of the Lord," in Jehovah's service. This is not simply a matter of spending some time in theocratic activities — it is a matter of priorities. View yourself as a servant of Jehovah first and as a school student second. Matthew 22:37 A young sister named Samantha says, "I promised myself that if my education were ever to interfere with my service to Jehovah, I would quit school."
QUESTION 5

What can you do to stay active in Jehovah's service?

Before beginning additional education, get your priorities clearly in focus. Think honestly about how well you managed things in previous years of school, and decide in advance what you'll do differently. Give real attention to your education — but never at the expense of your worship.

💬 Comment you could share: "What I appreciated was that this isn't about avoiding education — it's about deciding your plan before the pressure hits, not after."
📌 Ministry connection: Useful with a student householder — ask what their plan is for balancing school and other priorities, then offer this as a framework that's worked for others.
¶5 What you can do before you begin your additional education. Have your priorities clearly in focus. Ask yourself, "What plan do I have in place to stay regular in my meeting attendance, ministry, and personal study?" Joshua 1:8 Matthew 28:19, 20 Hebrews 10:25 Also, think back: How did you keep your priorities in focus during your previous years in school? Be determined to stay active in Jehovah's service as you get additional education. Give adequate attention to your education — but not at the expense of your worship. Matthew 6:24
QUESTION 6

What can you consider while making a self-examination? (See also picture.)

Build in a regular check-in — monthly is a good rhythm — and watch for warning signs: skipping meetings, arriving late, thinking about assignments during the meeting, attending only virtually, or letting prayer, Bible reading, and the ministry become a routine chore.

💬 Comment you could share: "The specific warning signs listed here felt very practical — things like attending only virtually or thinking about homework during the meeting are easy to miss in yourself."
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah doesn't wait for a full collapse before caring — he wants us to notice the small warning signs early, which shows how closely he watches over our spiritual health.
¶6 As you get your additional education, regularly make a self-examination, perhaps every month or so. Ask yourself, "Are my priorities still in focus?" Be alert to warning signs — for example, a habit of skipping meetings or arriving late, of thinking about your school assignments while the meeting is in progress, or of attending meetings only virtually. Note, too, whether your habit of prayer and Bible reading begins to slack off or whether your ministry becomes superficial. If you see such traits developing, act quickly.
Do not let your education keep you from giving priority to Jehovah's service (see paragraph 6)
Application: a set weekly time for personal study — protected the same way you'd protect a class or exam — helps keep this priority visible on the calendar, not just in your head.
“Guard Your Thinking Abilities”▸
QUESTION 7

As you are getting additional education, what challenges might you face with regard to your thinking?

Depending on your field of study, additional education can expose you to ideas that directly conflict with Bible teaching — evolution, atheism, or a self-reliant mindset that quietly leaves Jehovah out of the picture.

💬 Comment you could share: "The quote from the young brother stood out — he said it wasn't just a skill being taught, but a whole way of thinking that left Jehovah out of the equation."
¶7 The challenge. Depending on the courses you take, your additional education might expose you to the world's "philosophy and empty deception," including atheism and evolution. Colossians 2:8 It could also promote a spirit of self-reliance. One young brother recalls: "We weren't just taught a skill; we were taught a way of thinking, one that often wasn't in harmony with Jehovah's thinking... This kind of thinking left Jehovah out of the equation."
QUESTION 8

Why is it important to "guard your thinking abilities"? Proverbs 5:1, 2

Your thinking abilities need active protection because they're genuinely under attack. Ideas that sound like "higher" thinking about morals, life's origin, or God's existence can actually be foolishness in Jehovah's eyes.

💬 Comment you could share: "What convinced me is the point that what the world calls 'higher' thinking, the Bible calls 'foolishness with God' — it flips the whole framing around."
📌 Ministry connection: Works well with someone who values education highly — a respectful conversation about where true wisdom comes from can open the door.
¶8 The Bible warns you to "guard your thinking abilities." To guard something implies that it is under threat of attack. 1 Peter 5:8 The world's spirit can undermine your faith with regard to morals, the origin of life, or even the existence of God. People may present their view as "higher" thinking, but it is actually lower thinking. In fact, it is "foolishness with God." 1 Corinthians 3:18-20
QUESTION 9

What can you do to guard your thinking abilities?

Make sure your Bible-based convictions are strong before you even begin. Ask why you believe in God, how you know the Bible is his Word, and what convinces you Bible morals are superior — then look honestly at how those convictions held up in the past.

💬 Comment you could share: "I liked that this puts the preparation before the pressure — building convictions ahead of time rather than scrambling to defend them mid-course."
¶9 What you can do before you begin your additional education. Make sure your Bible-based convictions are strong before you begin. Ask yourself such questions as: "Why do I believe in God? How do I know that the Bible is his Word?" Be determined to guard your thinking abilities as you get additional education. 2 Timothy 2:16-18
QUESTION 10

How can you continue to protect your thinking abilities?

Keep examining yourself: are lifestyles around you shaping your thinking? Can you see through popular philosophies? Staying a diligent student of God's Word — through regular study and meditation — is what keeps those convictions sharp.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah doesn't just ask us to avoid bad influences — he actively equips us through his Word to be able to see through them, which shows he wants us capable, not just sheltered.
¶10 As you get additional education, keep making a regular self-examination. To guard your thinking abilities, continue to be a good student of God's Word. Bible study and meditation will help you to strengthen your conviction that you have the truth. 1 Timothy 4:15
Manage Your Time Wisely▸
QUESTION 11

As you are getting additional education, what challenge might you face with regard to your use of time?

Coursework can place heavy demands on your time, especially around exams and deadlines. Poor time management can lead to real stress and even burnout — which is why caring for your physical and emotional health matters too.

💬 Comment you could share: "It was interesting that the article links poor time management directly to burnout, not just a busy season — it's framed as a health issue, not just a scheduling one."
¶11 The challenge. Additional education may place heavy demands on your time, especially when you are studying for exams or when you have a tight deadline to complete a project. If you do not manage your time wisely, you could suffer from undue stress and even set yourself up for burnout.
QUESTION 12

What activities will you need to include as you make "the best use of your time"? Ephesians 5:15, 16

It takes real balance: association with fellow believers and family, spiritual needs as the top priority, plus any job or family responsibilities, and enough rest and exercise to protect your health.

📌 Ministry connection: Good discussion starter with a busy professional or student — ask what "balance" looks like for them, then share how Bible principles define it.
¶12 You need balance. Even more than giving attention to your classes, you need to have time for wholesome association with fellow believers and family members. Psalm 133:1 Proverbs 18:1 Your spiritual needs are the top priority. Matthew 6:33 You might also have family responsibilities and a job to care for. In addition, you need to take care of your health by getting adequate rest and moderate exercise. Ecclesiastes 4:6 1 Timothy 4:8
QUESTION 13

What can you do in order to make the best use of your time?

Build a realistic schedule before you start, based on an honest look at how well you handled time in the past — and be determined to control your time rather than let it control you.

💬 Comment you could share: "The line that stuck with me was 'control your time rather than allow time to control you' — it's a simple test to apply to any week."
¶13 What you can do before you begin your additional education. The Bible says that planning leads to success. Proverbs 21:5 With that in mind, have a realistic schedule in place before you begin your additional education. Be determined to control your time rather than allow time to control you.
QUESTION 14

What questions might help you to examine yourself?

Ask whether your schedule allows enough rest and time with fellow believers, whether homework gets done on time, and if not, whether that's from having too much to do or from procrastinating. It can help to ask family or others with strong time-management skills for their honest input.

📌 Ministry connection: The "too much to do vs. procrastination" distinction is a very relatable, non-judgmental way to start a conversation with a stressed student or parent.
¶14 As you get additional education, regularly make a self-examination. Would others, including family members, agree with my assessment? Whether you need to adjust your schedule or simply develop better self-discipline, act quickly. You might even talk to others who have good time-management skills. Proverbs 11:14
Keep “Walking With the Wise”▸
QUESTION 15

As you are getting additional education, what challenge might you face?

Classmates can become a strong social pull, especially when you're spending many hours a week together. It's easy to feel more in common with them than with some in the congregation — but sharing a classroom isn't the same as sharing values.

💬 Comment you could share: "Michael's experience really illustrated it well — 40+ hours a week with coworkers whose views on entertainment and language gradually started affecting his own thinking, without him necessarily choosing that."
¶15 The challenge. You might even feel that you have more in common with your classmates than you do with some of your fellow believers. However, you need to be cautious. Although you share a classroom with your fellow students, you do not share their values. 1 Corinthians 15:33 Michael, who completed a four-year electrical apprenticeship, says, "I spent more than 40 hours a week with coworkers whose view of the opposite sex, choice of music, and use of vulgar language started to affect my thinking."
QUESTION 16

What does "walking with the wise" mean? Proverbs 13:20

We're influenced by those we spend time with, for better or worse. The friendships worth building are with those who, like you, wholeheartedly serve Jehovah.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah cares enough about our friendships to give direct counsel on them — that shows he sees our social circle as part of our spiritual protection, not a side issue.
¶16 The Bible warns us about the dangers of harmful association. On the other hand, "the one walking with the wise will become wise." We are influenced by those we choose to spend time with, either for good or for bad. Maintain friendships with those who have your best interests at heart. Psalm 101:6, 7 Psalm 119:63
QUESTION 17

What can you do to avoid harmful association?

Decide ahead of time what limits you'll set with classmates. One Witness described being cordial but not spending time with classmates after school — viewing them as classmates, not close friends. Think back on what limits you set before, and what you'd adjust now.

💬 Comment you could share: "Trenton's approach was helpful — 'cordial but not close friends' gives a clear, practical line without being unfriendly."
¶17 What you can do before you begin your additional education. Decide which limits to set with regard to your association. A Witness named Trenton says: "I'm cordial with my fellow students, but I don't spend time with them after school. I view them as my classmates, not as my close friends." What limits will you set?
QUESTION 18

What self-examination would be beneficial? (See also picture.)

Periodically ask whether you're drifting too close to classmates and whether their habits or attitudes are starting to shape your own speech and thinking — and don't overlook that you're in a unique position to witness to them.

📌 Ministry connection: Classmates already know you and trust you in a way a stranger wouldn't — that familiarity is itself an opening for a witness.
¶18 At regular intervals, make a self-examination. Ask yourself: "Am I getting too close to my classmates? Are their habits, their speech, and their attitudes beginning to affect my speech and thinking?" Psalm 1:1 Also, don't hesitate to witness to your classmates. You are in a unique position to help them learn about the truth.
Do not hesitate to witness to your classmates (see paragraph 18) — a sister shares a tract with a classmate after their cosmetology class
Application: a tract or a brief comment tied to a class topic can be a natural, low-pressure way to open a conversation with someone you already see every week.
Be Prepared▸
QUESTION 19

How can you prepare yourself to overcome spiritual challenges as you get additional education? Illustrate.

Like a hiker who prepares before setting out on a trail — staying fit, dressed properly, and knowing the destination — you can prepare spiritually before additional education begins, aiming not at the world's idea of "success" but at bringing glory to Jehovah in everything you do.

💬 Comment you could share: "The hiker illustration really works — he can't predict every challenge on the trail, but he's still prepared for the ones he can anticipate."
¶19 A wise hiker prepares himself before he sets foot on the trail. Although he cannot predict every challenge that he will face, he is in shape, he is properly dressed, and he has a goal. Keep strong in the faith, wear "the complete suit of armor from God," and have a goal — not to achieve what the world considers "success," but to bring glory to Jehovah in everything you do. Ephesians 6:11-13 1 Corinthians 9:26, 27 1 Corinthians 10:31
QUESTION 20

How can you "keep testing whether you are in the faith"?

Regularly examine yourself in each area discussed: a healthy spiritual routine, guarded thinking, wise use of time, and cultivating good friendships while avoiding harmful association — whether you're at school, at work, or anywhere else. Jehovah will bless the effort.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: The promise that Jehovah will bless these efforts "in whatever circumstance" reflects how personally invested he is in our staying close to him, regardless of the setting we're in.
¶20 The Bible urges us: "Keep testing whether you are in the faith; keep proving what you yourselves are." 2 Corinthians 13:5 Are you maintaining a healthy spiritual routine? Are you guarding your thinking from the so-called wisdom of this world? Are you managing your time wisely? Are you cultivating friendships among fellow Christians while avoiding harmful association? Be determined to stay strong in the faith in whatever circumstance you face. You can be certain that Jehovah will bless your efforts to do so. Proverbs 3:5, 6
