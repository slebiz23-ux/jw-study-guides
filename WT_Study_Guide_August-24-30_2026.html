Benefit From the Field Service Group Arrangement

The Watchtower—Study Edition, June 2026, pp. 14-19 · Studied August 24-30, 2026
🎵 Opening Song: SONG 65 — Move Ahead!
Focus

How we benefit from the field service group arrangement.

“Praise Jah! I will praise Jehovah with all my heart In the assembled group of upright ones and in the congregation.”
— Ps. 111:1
1-2. How do many feel about the field service group arrangement?

Many publishers describe the field service group as a source of practical training, spiritual goals reached with help from others, and lifelong friendships. Group overseers who serve well find it an outstanding privilege to help others make spiritual progress for years to come.

💬 Comment you could share: “What struck me was how Wendy, Rich, and Dinah all describe the same arrangement so differently—skills, privilege, family. It shows how much this small group shapes our whole ministry.”

📌 Ministry connection: Mentioning that I regularly go door to door (or informally witness) with the same small group of brothers and sisters can open a natural conversation about why congregations are organized this way.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah doesn’t leave us to serve alone—he organizes his worshippers into small, personal groups so that no one is overlooked. That reflects his personal, caring nature.

¶1 DO YOU remember the day you were approved to be a publisher of the good news? On that happy day, you identified yourself with a vast group of worshippers who are praising Jehovah’s name. (Ps. 148:1, 2, 12, 13) However, on that day, you may also have become part of a smaller group, one that may have included only a handful of fellow worshippers—a field service group. Field service groups play a key role in helping us to fulfill our commission to preach the good news of the Kingdom “in all the inhabited earth.”—Matt. 24:14.
¶2 How do you feel about the field service group arrangement? A sister named Wendya says: “So much of my ministry has been shaped by this arrangement. I have learned new preaching and teaching skills from others, have been helped to set and reach spiritual goals, and have made lifelong friends—all as a result of this loving arrangement.” A brother named Rich adds: “I view serving as a group overseer as an outstanding privilege. While some publishers get to assist a person to progress to baptism, a group overseer has unique opportunities to help that person to make spiritual progress for years to come.” A sister named Dinah says: “The field service group arrangement is wonderful! I view my group as my family.”
3. What will we discuss in this article?

The article covers three areas: (1) the role of the group overseer and his assistant, (2) how we personally can contribute to our field service group’s success, and (3) the spiritual, emotional, and practical benefits we receive from the arrangement.

💬 Comment you could share: “I appreciate that this article isn’t just for group overseers—it gives all of us, whether new publishers or experienced ones, practical ways to contribute.”

📌 Ministry connection: I could use this outline to think through my own week: am I being present, hospitable, and taking initiative in my ministry group?

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah organizes matters so that every one of us has a role to play, not just the overseer.

¶3 In this article, we will first discuss the role of the group overseer and his assistant and see how they care for those assigned to their group. Then, we will consider how we personally can contribute to the success of our field service group. Finally, we will see how we can benefit from this loving arrangement.
The Group Overseer and His Assistant
4. (a) What is a field service group overseer’s main responsibility? (b) How does a group overseer care for those in his group? (See the box “Tips for the Group Overseer.”)

(a) A group overseer is an elder assigned by the body of elders to help a group of publishers make spiritual advancement.

(b) He does this in three ways: he takes an active interest in each person in his group by keeping in contact and providing spiritual encouragement (Prov. 27:23; Prov. 12:25); he helps each publisher have a full share in the ministry by taking the lead and training regularly; and he helps baptized brothers qualify for congregation responsibilities through commendation, counsel, and training (1 Tim. 3:1).

Tips for the Group Overseer

  • Keep meetings for service brief, no longer than seven minutes, so that publishers can promptly get out in the ministry.
  • Work with as many of the publishers assigned to the group as possible, including those whose circumstances do not allow them to support meetings for service regularly. It may be possible to work with such publishers on a different day or evening.
  • Shepherd all in the group regularly. Effective shepherding calls may be made at the homes of the publishers, at the Kingdom Hall, or at other appropriate locations. Shepherding may also be provided by telephone or by letter.
  • Train brothers to reach their full potential and to help them qualify for increased responsibility.
  • Take the lead in your service group even when your group needs to meet together with other service groups.

💬 Comment you could share: “I found it interesting that the article lists three distinct roles for a group overseer—shepherd, trainer, and mentor—rather than just ‘the one who leads the meeting for service.’”

📌 Ministry connection: The reminder that group overseers ‘take a zealous lead in the ministry’ encourages me to be ready and prepared when it’s my turn to work the territory.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah cares for his flock through appointed shepherds who reflect his own compassion and patience toward each individual, not just the group as a whole.

¶4 A field service group overseer is an elderb who has been assigned by the body of elders to assist a group of publishers to make spiritual advancement. How does he do this? First, he takes an active interest in each person assigned to his group. (Prov. 27:23) Being an alert, caring shepherd, he keeps in contact with them. Doing so helps him to provide spiritual encouragement, emotional support, and practical help. (Prov. 12:25; Isa. 32:2; Jas. 2:15-17) Second, he helps each publisher to have a full share in the ministry. He takes a zealous lead in the ministry, especially on weekends. He also works regularly with each publisher to provide personal encouragement and training. If on the rare occasion he cannot be in service, he makes sure that the group is cared for by his assistant or another qualified publisher. Third, he helps baptized brothers to qualify for congregation responsibilities. (1 Tim. 3:1) He provides sincere commendation, Scriptural counsel, and regular training to help brothers qualify as ministerial servants and as elders. In view of the importance of the group overseer’s role, the body of elders selects those elders who are most qualified to fulfill this assignment.
5. What challenges do some group overseers face?

Group overseers often juggle multiple congregation assignments (like Jörg), care for unusually large groups where elders are few (like Abel), or find that training gets neglected because it takes time and the results aren’t always immediate (like Obed).

💬 Comment you could share: “I found it interesting that even experienced elders from different parts of the world—Germany, Uganda, Suriname—face the very same struggle: not enough time to contact everyone personally.”

📌 Ministry connection: This reminds me to be patient and cooperative when my group overseer reaches out, since I know his time and attention are genuinely stretched.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah doesn’t expect perfection from imperfect shepherds; he provides loving arrangements and capable helpers to lighten their load.

¶5 A group overseer may face challenges as he cares for those in his group. Consider Jörg, a group overseer from Germany who, like many elders, must care for more than one assignment in the congregation. He says: “Balancing several responsibilities is not easy. As a result, I find it challenging to meet my goal of contacting everyone in my group.” Abel, a 70-year-old elder from Uganda says: “We have very few elders and ministerial servants in our congregation. Thus, the field service groups are rather large. As a result, I find it hard to shepherd each publisher as often as I would like to.” And Obed, an elder from Suriname, admits: “I love to train others. But training requires time and energy, and the results are not always seen immediately. Because of this, training is often the first thing to get neglected.” What can help elders who face these and similar challenges?
6. How does keeping field service groups small help group overseers to care for those in their group?

Small groups let a group overseer become well-acquainted with everyone and shepherd them effectively. Where elders are scarce, the body of elders may temporarily assign a qualified ministerial servant (a “group servant”) to keep groups from growing too large for personal care.

💬 Comment you could share: “What struck me was that the size of the group is treated as a spiritual safeguard, not just an administrative detail—it directly affects how well people are cared for.”

📌 Ministry connection: In the ministry, I could mention how being part of a smaller group has helped me get personal, practical training rather than getting lost in a crowd.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah values quality shepherding over sheer numbers—he wants every sheep personally known and cared for.

¶6 When possible, field service groups should be kept relatively small so that group overseers can become well-acquainted with all in the group and help them remain spiritually strong. What if there are few elders in the congregation? Rather than make groups so large that a group overseer can no longer fulfill all the aspects of his role, the body of elders may temporarily assign a ministerial servant to care for a group. When groups are relatively small, group overseers are better able to care for the most important aspect of their assignment—their brothers and sisters.
7. In congregations with few elders, what can group overseers do to ensure that all in the group are well cared for? (1 Peter 5:2) (See also picture.)

A group overseer with few elders to rely on should focus on training his assistant—delegating tasks such as taking the lead in the field ministry, discussing the group’s activity together, and bringing the assistant along on shepherding visits. In time, the assistant may qualify to serve as a group overseer himself. (2 Tim. 2:2) Mature publishers and pioneers can also help with training.

💬 Comment you could share: “I found it interesting that ‘shepherd the flock… willingly… not for love of dishonest gain’ applies just as much to a group overseer training his assistant as it does to elders in general.”

📌 Ministry connection: When I’m assigned to work with someone newer in the ministry, I can treat it as training, not just ‘getting the territory covered.’

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah’s own shepherding is unselfish and willing—he is the model every group overseer and assistant is trying to imitate.

¶7 If you serve as a group overseer in a congregation with few elders, focus on training your assistant to help you care for God’s flock. (Read 1 Peter 5:2.) Delegate tasks to your assistant. For example, periodically assign him to take the lead in the field ministry. Prayerfully discuss with him the activity of publishers in the group. Regularly bring your assistant on shepherding visits and provide him with guidance before and after each visit. In time, your assistant may qualify to serve as a group overseer. (2 Tim. 2:2) You can also ask other mature Christians to help with training. Pioneers and other experienced publishers can work with new and less experienced ones. And spiritually mature publishers can assist those who need help with starting conversations in the ministry, making return visits, or conducting Bible studies. Now let us consider three ways we personally can contribute to the success of our field service group.
Contribute to the Success of Your Field Service Group
8. How can we contribute to the success of our group? (Romans 1:12)

Be present. Engaging in the ministry with others provides “an interchange of encouragement,” safety, and confidence. If work schedules, health, or age make this difficult, publishers can ask an employer for schedule adjustments (Neh. 2:4-6) or ask the group overseer about alternatives such as videoconferencing, letter writing, or phone witnessing.

💬 Comment you could share: “I found it interesting that Paul—an apostle with far more experience than the Roman Christians—still expected to be encouraged by them, not just to encourage them. Encouragement really does go both ways.”

📌 Ministry connection: I could ask my employer directly about adjusting my schedule to support meetings for service, the way Nehemiah asked the king—respectfully, but directly.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah gives us “both the desire and the power to act” when we make the effort to be present with our brothers and sisters in the ministry.

¶8 Be present. You contribute to “an interchange of encouragement” when you engage in the ministry with others. (Read Romans 1:12.) Witnessing together may also provide those in the group with a measure of safety as well as greater confidence. If secular work often prevents you from supporting your group’s preaching arrangements, could you ask your employer to adjust your work schedule? You might be surprised at the response. (Neh. 2:4-6) If poor health or advancing age makes it difficult for you to meet with the group in person, why not speak with your group overseer? He may be able to make arrangements for you to tie in to the group by means of videoconference and to engage in letter writing or phone witnessing. Are you young and in good health but still find it hard to get out in service regularly? If so, why not plan to work in person with someone in your group each week? You may need to reassess your priorities or adjust the way you manage your time. (Rom. 12:11; Col. 4:5) Every effort is worth it. Pray to Jehovah. He can give you “both the desire and the power to act.”—Phil. 2:13.
9. How might we contribute to a warm, loving atmosphere in our field service group? (Romans 12:13)

Be hospitable. Making our home available as a meeting location, or taking turns hosting the visiting speaker, builds warmth in the group. As Sarah’s comment shows, hospitality doesn’t need to be elaborate—the goal is getting to know others, not a perfect meal.

💬 Comment you could share: “What struck me was Sarah’s comment about keeping hospitality ‘very simple’—it removed a barrier I sometimes feel about inviting others over.”

📌 Ministry connection: I could offer to host the group before or after a Saturday morning ministry so newer publishers feel welcomed into the group socially, not just organizationally.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah is himself described as supremely hospitable, welcoming ‘strangers’ into his family—our hospitality to one another reflects that quality of his.

¶9 Be hospitable. We contribute to a warm, loving atmosphere within our field service group by following “the course of hospitality.” (Read Romans 12:13.) Might we be able to make our home available as a meeting location for a field service group? In some congregations, groups take turns providing hospitality for the visiting speaker. On their assigned week, some in the group may gather for a meal and for encouragement regardless of whether the speaker can attend. A special pioneer named Sarah says: “I get nervous when it comes to showing hospitality, so I keep it very simple. I try to focus on getting to know others instead of worrying about whether the food will turn out just right.” When we work to create a loving, familylike spirit of hospitality in our field service group, we draw closer to Jehovah and to one another.—Neh. 8:10; Acts 20:35.
10. What might we do if we want to improve in some aspect of the ministry? (Proverbs 1:5; 27:17) (See also picture.)

Take the initiative. Ask an experienced publisher or pioneer in the group for help with a specific skill, and let the group overseer know about the goal. Heather’s experience shows that asking for help, even when we feel shy, can reopen our enjoyment of the ministry.

💬 Comment you could share: “I found it interesting that ‘as iron sharpens iron’ describes a mutual process—both people improve when we ask for help, not just the one being helped.”

📌 Ministry connection: I could specifically ask an experienced publisher to help me start return visits or handle a particular objection I struggle with.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah placed skilled, willing teachers among us precisely so that we could sharpen one another—he wants our progress to be a shared effort.

¶10 Take the initiative. The more skillful we are in preaching and teaching, the more likely we are to enjoy going in service with our group. Is there an aspect of your ministry that you would like to improve? Why not take the initiative to ask someone—perhaps a pioneer or an experienced publisher in your group—for help? (Read Proverbs 1:5; 27:17.) Let your group overseer know about your goal. He may be able to help you reach it. You might also ask others for help. A sister named Heather did that. Although shy, she says: “I asked a pioneer for help in making return visits. She was loving and kind, and she made regular arrangements to work with me. She even helped me start a Bible study with one of my return visits. I am so glad that I asked for help because I now enjoy being out in the ministry again.” Clearly, we can contribute to the success of our field service group in many ways.
How You Can Benefit From Your Field Service Group
11-12. What spiritual benefits can you receive from the field service group arrangement? Relate an experience.

Widows, orphans, and the elderly receive timely, encouraging shepherding through the group (1 Thess. 2:8; Jas. 1:27); all of us receive training in preaching and teaching; and exemplary baptized brothers get help qualifying for added responsibilities (1 Tim. 3:10).

Wendy’s experience illustrates this: because her father opposed the truth, her mother often couldn’t join the group on weekends—but Wendy could, and an older pioneer sister mentored her in skills that have benefited her ever since, along with loving Scriptural advice from her group overseer.

💬 Comment you could share: “What struck me about Wendy’s experience is that her spiritual growth came almost entirely through the field service group, since her home situation made congregation support especially important.”

📌 Ministry connection: I could think of a younger or newer publisher in my group I could mentor the way that pioneer sister mentored Wendy.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah is “especially fond of” widows, orphans, and the elderly—and he cares for them concretely through the field service group arrangement, not just in a general sense.

¶11 Spiritual benefits. Each of us can benefit spiritually from the field service group arrangement. For example, those whom Jehovah is especially fond of—widows, orphans, and the elderly—receive timely and encouraging shepherding. (1 Thess. 2:8; Jas. 1:27) We also receive much-needed training in various aspects of the Kingdom preaching and teaching work. Exemplary baptized brothers receive help to qualify for additional congregation responsibilities.—1 Tim. 3:10.
¶12 Consider how Wendy, mentioned earlier, benefited from the field service group arrangement. “Because my father was opposed to the truth,” she says, “it was often not possible for my mother to go in service on the weekends with her field service group. However, I was allowed to go, and I did. One older pioneer sister took me under her wing and helped me to develop skills that have benefited me both in the ministry and in the congregation. I recall several times when my group overseer gave me loving Scriptural advice that has helped me to this day.”
13. What are some emotional benefits that we can receive when we support our field service group? (See also picture.)

Working “side by side” in the ministry forms close bonds (Phil. 1:27), and the small size of the group can make it easier for shy or anxious ones to build close friendships (2 Cor. 6:13). These friends offer real support during illness, depression, or grief (1 Thess. 5:14)—support we will need even more in the future (Matt. 24:21).

💬 Comment you could share: “I found it interesting that the article says the small size of the group actually helps those who are shy—it’s not just a logistical detail, it’s designed to help build real friendships.”

📌 Ministry connection: I could reach out to someone in my group who has been going through a hard time and simply ask how they’re doing before we start the ministry.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: A “true friend… born for times of distress” reflects the kind of loyal love Jehovah himself shows us—our field service group can be one place we experience that love in a tangible way.

¶13 Emotional benefits. We form close bonds with those in our group as we work “side by side” with them in the ministry. (Phil. 1:27) The small size of the field service group may also make it easier for those who are shy or who feel anxious in large crowds to develop close friendships. (2 Cor. 6:13) We can count on these friends for support during difficult times, such as when we are ill, feeling depressed, or grieving the death of a loved one. (1 Thess. 5:14) No doubt we will need good friends to a greater extent in the future. (Matt. 24:21) Our problems may not go away, but they can be more manageable when we know we can count on brothers and sisters who love and support us.—Prov. 17:17.
14. How do we benefit from our field service group during times of disaster or emergency?

The Congregation Service Committee, together with group overseers, develops a plan to help those with special needs during disease outbreaks, natural disasters, medical emergencies, persecution, and financial crises—locating each family, assessing needs like medical care, food, water, shelter, and clothing so that relief aid can be provided.

💬 Comment you could share: “What struck me is how specific this planning is—it isn’t vague goodwill, it’s an organized system that starts at the field service group level.”

📌 Ministry connection: If I have practical skills (medical, construction, languages), I could make sure my group overseer or elders know, so I can be included in relief efforts if needed.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah’s organization mirrors his own practical compassion—he doesn’t just care about our spiritual needs but also our physical ones in times of crisis.

¶14 Practical benefits. We receive timely help from our field service group during disease outbreaks, natural disasters, medical emergencies, persecution, and financial crises. For example, in each congregation, the Congregation Service Committee, along with group overseers, develops a plan to help those with special needs. Group overseers take the lead in locating each family in their field service group and assessing the immediate needs, including medical care, food, water, shelter, and clothing so that relief aid can be provided. What practical help and loving care we receive from our field service group!
15. What unites all of us in Jehovah’s organization?

Jehovah’s organization is vast—hundreds of millions, perhaps billions, of spirit creatures in heaven, and over nine million on earth and growing (Zech. 8:23; Rev. 5:11)—yet all of Jehovah’s worshippers are united in declaring the good news of God’s Kingdom (Rev. 14:6, 7). The field service group arrangement helps us praise Jehovah with our whole heart. (Ps. 111:1)

💬 Comment you could share: “I found it interesting that the article closes by zooming out from our small field service group to the vastness of Jehovah’s whole organization—the small group is our personal connection to something enormous.”

📌 Ministry connection: This global unity is itself a preaching point—I could mention to someone in the ministry how millions of people worldwide study the same Bible lesson each week.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah unites an enormous, diverse organization in a single purpose: praising him. That kind of unity reflects his own singular, unifying love.

¶15 As mentioned, Jehovah’s organization is very large. The heavenly part alone is made up of hundreds of millions—perhaps billions—of faithful spirit creatures. The earthly part is over nine million strong and growing each day. (Zech. 8:23; Rev. 5:11) All of Jehovah’s worshippers are united in their efforts to declare the good news of God’s Kingdom! (Rev. 14:6, 7) May we benefit fully from the field service group arrangement—an arrangement that helps us to praise Jehovah with all our heart.—Ps. 111:1.
