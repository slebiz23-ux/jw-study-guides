1. Learn From the Warning Example of Zedekiah
10 min · Jer 52:1, 2, 3, 8-11 · w17.10 23 ¶9-12
▾

Theme: Zedekiah, Judah’s last king, had every advantage—Jehovah’s prophet Jeremiah spoke to him directly—yet he gave in to pressure, broke a sacred oath, and lost everything. His life is a warning example for us.

① Zedekiah did not stand up for what is right Jer 52:1, 2

Jer 52:2 (NWT)He continued to do what was bad in Jehovah’s eyes, according to all that Je·hoiʹa·kim had done.

Read the full context: Jer 52:1. Zedekiah was only 21 when he began to reign, yet the record says he “continued to do what was bad in Jehovah’s eyes.”

💬 Comment you could share: “I noticed that Zedekiah ‘continued to do what was bad in Jehovah’s eyes.’ It wasn’t one dramatic act—it was a pattern of going along with the crowd.”
📌 Personal application: “When coworkers or classmates pressure me to compromise, I can decide ahead of time what I’ll say, so I’m not caught off guard.”
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: “Jehovah kept sending warnings through Jeremiah. That shows He is patient and wants people to turn back.”

② Zedekiah broke an oath he swore in God’s name Jer 52:3 2Ch 36:13 Ezek 17:16

Jer 52:3 (NWT)It was because of Jehovah’s anger that these things took place in Jerusalem and in Judah, until he cast them out of his sight. And Zed·e·kiʹah rebelled against the king of Babylon.
2Ch 36:13 (NWT)He also rebelled against King Neb·u·chad·nezʹzar, who had made him take an oath by God, and he remained stubborn and hardhearted and refused to turn to Jehovah the God of Israel.

The Watchtower of October 2017 (“Visions of Zechariah—How They Affect You,” ¶9-10) explains that Zedekiah swore by Jehovah to remain a loyal vassal of Babylon but did not keep his word. Ezek 17:16 shows how seriously Jehovah viewed that broken oath.

💬 Comment you could share: “Zedekiah’s oath was made in Jehovah’s name, so breaking it was a direct insult to God, not just to Nebuchadnezzar.”
📌 Personal application: “I want to be known as someone whose ‘yes’ means yes—on the job, in family commitments, and especially in my dedication vow.”
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: “Jehovah takes promises seriously, and he is faithful to his own promises. That makes him totally trustworthy.”

③ Zedekiah did not escape the consequences Jer 52:8-11

Jer 52:8 (NWT)But the Chal·deʹan army pursued the king, and they overtook Zed·e·kiʹah in the desert plains of Jerʹi·cho, and all his troops were scattered from his side.
Jer 52:9 (NWT)Then they seized the king and brought him up to the king of Babylon at Ribʹlah in the land of Haʹmath, and he passed sentence on him.
Jer 52:10 (NWT)And the king of Babylon slaughtered Zed·e·kiʹah’s sons before his eyes, and he also slaughtered all the princes of Judah there at Ribʹlah.
Jer 52:11 (NWT)Then the king of Babylon blinded Zed·e·kiʹah’s eyes, bound him with copper fetters, brought him to Babylon, and kept him imprisoned until the day of his death.
Observation: The last thing Zedekiah saw was his sons being killed—then he was blinded. Jehovah had told him through Jeremiah that surrender would have spared his life; his disobedience led to the opposite.

FOR MEDITATION: How can I avoid being like Zedekiah? (w17.10 23 ¶11-12)

The article points out that the most important promise we ever make is our dedication to Jehovah, and that our stand in tests “large and small” shows we take it seriously. Ideas to consider: keep a daily Bible reading routine, pray daily, decide ahead how you will respond to pressure, and obey Jehovah even when it is unpopular.

💬 Comment you could share: “If I think of my dedication as a promise I made directly to Jehovah, it helps me say no to small compromises.”
📌 Personal application: “This week I’ll choose one area—daily Bible reading, prayer, or honesty—and make it a firm commitment.”
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: “Jehovah ‘listens to the promises that we make,’ so he truly cares about our relationship with him.”
Sources: Ezek 17:16 · w17.10 pp. 23-24 (sidebar) · jr chap. 13 (sidebar)

Jer 51:6 (NWT)Flee out of the midst of Babylon, And escape for your life. Do not perish because of her error. For it is the time for Jehovah’s vengeance. He is paying her back for what she has done.

Rev 18:4 Her “waters,” symbolizing her peoples, “dry up.” Rev 16:12

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: “Jehovah is a God of justice who ‘pleads the legal case’ of his people (Jer. 51:36), but he also warns first.”

Narrative summary

Israel suffered 20 years under Canaanite King Jabin and his general Sisera, who commanded 900 iron-scythed chariots. Jehovah’s prophetess Deborah summoned Barak with God’s command to gather 10,000 men at Mount Tabor. Barak asked Deborah to come along, and she agreed. When Sisera’s chariots advanced, Deborah told Barak to attack. Jehovah threw Sisera’s army into confusion; rain turned the plain to mud and the whole army fell. Sisera fled on foot.

1. What was life like in Israel under Sisera’s domination? (w15 8/1 13 ¶1)▾

For 20 years Sisera’s army terrorized the land. Judg 4:3 Jabin ‘harshly oppressed the Israelites for 20 years’ with 900 war chariots. Roads were deserted and travelers kept to back roads (Judg 5:6); villagers had ‘no more’ security (Judg 5:7); and few had weapons (Judg 5:8). The chapter adds that the soldiers seem to have taken young women captive. The people had turned to ‘new gods’ and brought this on themselves.

Reflect on the Lessons

When might a brother imitate the way Barak humbly accepted the help of a faithful woman? B▾

Judg 4:8 Barak wanted Deborah beside him. Matt 23:11 Jesus said the greatest must be a minister. A brother can humbly accept help when a sister has more training or experience—for example, a sister who is skilled at a trade, at ministry, or at a project (see Picture B showing a sister training brothers).

A sister training three brothers how to operate heating and cooling equipment. They listen intently and take notes.
Application: Picture B: the brothers are taking notes and listening intently. Humility isn’t weakness; learning from someone with more experience is wise.
Matt 23:11 (NWT)But the greatest one among you must be your minister.
Matt 23:12 (NWT)Whoever exalts himself will be humbled, and whoever humbles himself will be exalted.
💬 Comment you could share: “Barak didn’t feel threatened. He wanted Jehovah’s guidance, and Deborah was a channel of it.”
📌 Personal application: “If a sister has a skill I need, I can ask for help and show appreciation.”
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: “Jehovah values humility.”
Why did Deborah need courage to accompany Barak? When do we need similar courage?▾

Deborah would go to the battlefield with an army facing 900 chariots, and she knew Judg 4:9 the glory would go to a woman, not Barak. We need similar courage when we stand for what’s right at school, at work, or when we share the good news despite opposition.

💬 Comment you could share: “Deborah’s courage included being willing to be in danger herself—not just giving instructions from a safe distance.”
📌 Personal application: “I’ll look for one opportunity this week to speak up for Jehovah.”
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: “Jehovah supports those who act in faith.”
In what other ways might you imitate the courage of Barak and Deborah?▾

(Personal.) Ideas: share the good news with a difficult neighbor, speak up for Bible standards, accept counsel humbly, support someone in a hard assignment, or trust Jehovah’s direction even when it looks risky. Note that Paul lists Barak among men of faith. Heb 11:32

Heb 11:32 (NWT)And what more will I say? For time will fail me if I go on to relate about Gidʹe·on, Baʹrak, Samson, Jephʹthah, David, as well as Samuel and the other prophets.
💬 Comment you could share: “Paul lists Barak among the faithful, which shows that obedience and humility count as real faith.”
📌 Personal application: “I’ll pray for the courage to do one specific thing this week.”
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: “Jehovah remembers and values the faith of his servants.”

Meditate on the Bigger Picture

1. What does this account teach us about Jehovah?▾

Jehovah hears his people (Judg 4:3), gives clear direction through his appointed channel (Judg 4:6), and fights for those who trust him (Judg 4:15). He uses both men and women, and he rewards faith and humility. Research Guide references for these verses (via the NWT Study Edition) can add more depth.

Judg 4:6 (NWT)She sent for Baʹrak the son of A·binʹo·am out of Keʹdesh-naphʹta·li and said to him: “Has not Jehovah the God of Israel given the command? ‘Go and march to Mount Taʹbor, and take 10,000 men of Naphʹta·li and Zebʹu·lun with you.
Judg 4:15 (NWT)Then Jehovah threw Sisʹe·ra and all his war chariots and all the army into confusion before the sword of Baʹrak. Finally Sisʹe·ra got down from his chariot and fled on foot.
💬 Comment you could share: “Jehovah is a Deliverer who knows what his people are going through.”
📌 Personal application: “When I feel oppressed by a problem, I’ll pray as the Israelites did.”
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: “Jehovah is a God of justice and rescue.”
2. Are there points from this lesson we can use in the ministry or our daily life?▾

Yes—Ministry: approach each door confident that Jehovah goes before us; accept help from experienced publishers. Daily life: be humble when receiving help, be willing to face hard situations with faith, and support others as Deborah supported Barak.

💬 Comment you could share: “When I partner with someone in the ministry, I can be like Barak and welcome their support.”
📌 Personal application: “I’ll try to be a ‘Deborah’ to a younger one.”
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: “Jehovah blesses teamwork and humility.”
How does the account tie in with Jehovah’s purposes? & What would I like to ask Barak and Deborah in the resurrection?▾

Jehovah is protecting his people and the line leading to the Messiah, and ultimately shows that his name and rulership will be vindicated. For the resurrection question, think of a sincere personal question: e.g., “What was going through your mind as you saw the chariots?”
