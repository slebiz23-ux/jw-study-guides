WHEN YOU DISAGREE
¶6How can disagreements threaten friendships?

Why is it a challenge? When disagreements arise, each person may be convinced that his own viewpoint is correct. If pride sets in and the matter is never discussed and settled, resentment can build, and friends can drift apart — sometimes over something minor.

💬 Comment you could share: "The example of two brothers disagreeing about organizing a field service group really hit home — it's such a small thing to lose a friendship over."
📌 Ministry connection: I can point out that the Bible doesn't pretend disagreements never happen among God's people — it gives practical, workable counsel for handling them.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah wants unity among his people, not because we never differ, but because we choose to prioritize peace over being "right."
Why is it a challenge? When disagreements arise, each person may be convinced that his viewpoint is correct. Imagine two brothers who disagree about the best way to organize an activity for their field service group. Each is certain that his way is best. Can you see the potential for their difference of opinion to escalate and threaten their relationship? They might allow pride to affect them. If the matter is not discussed and settled, they could start to feel resentful. As time passes, they could risk drifting further apart and eventually risk no longer being friends—all because of a minor matter.
¶7How did Jesus teach his disciples that humility was essential to overcoming disagreements?

Learn from Jesus' humility. When the disciples argued about who was greatest, Jesus taught them to consider others as superior. Even on the night before his death he "set the pattern" by humbly washing their feet — showing that viewing others as superior makes us more willing to yield when we disagree.

"But Jesus called them to him and said: 'You know that the rulers of the nations lord it over them and the great men wield authority over them. This must not be the way among you; but whoever wants to become great among you must be your minister, and whoever wants to be first among you must be your slave. Just as the Son of man came, not to be ministered to, but to minister and to give his life as a ransom in exchange for many.'"Matt. 20:25-28
"So Jesus, knowing that the Father had given all things into his hands and that he came from God and was going to God, got up from the evening meal and laid aside his outer garments. And taking a towel, he wrapped it around his waist. After that he put water into a basin and started to wash the feet of the disciples and to dry them off with the towel that was wrapped around him."John 13:3-5
"Therefore, if I, the Lord and Teacher, washed your feet, you also should wash the feet of one another. For I set the pattern for you, that just as I did to you, you should also do."John 13:12-16

📌 Ministry connection: I can share how Jesus reversed the world's idea of greatness — real greatness is measured by service, not authority.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah sent his own Son to serve rather than to be served — the ultimate expression of divine humility and love for us.
Learn from Jesus' humility. He taught his disciples what was essential to overcoming disagreements—humility. The disciples had for some time been arguing about who was the greatest among them. To counter their deeply ingrained attitude, Jesus explained to them that they needed to consider others as superior. (Matt. 20:25-28) Yes, he was teaching them how to be humble. Even on the night before his death, Jesus "set the pattern" for them by humbly doing the job of a servant—washing their feet. (John 13:3-5, 12-16) In doing that, he showed them how they could preserve their friendship. If they viewed others as being superior, they would be more likely to yield when they disagreed. They would find it easier to remain close friends even when there was a difference of opinion.
¶8How can humility help you to maintain Christian friendships when disagreements occur?

How can you maintain your friendships? Humility helps you overlook minor disagreements and forgive freely, choosing not to react negatively even when unkind words are said, since we all say things we later regret. It can help to ask yourself whether being "right" is worth more than the friendship, and then simply let the matter go rather than reviving it later.

"Continue putting up with one another and forgiving one another freely even if anyone has a cause for complaint against another. Just as Jehovah freely forgave you, you must also do the same."Col. 3:13

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah freely forgives us; imitating that free, ready forgiveness toward others reflects his own mercy.
How can you maintain your friendships? Humility can help you to overlook minor disagreements and to forgive others freely. (Read Colossians 3:13.) With humility, you are more likely to preserve good relations by choosing not to react negatively. (Ps. 4:4) Even if unkind words have been said, remember that we all say things that we later regret. (Eccl. 7:21, 22) Ask yourself, 'Is my insistence on being in the right worth more than our friendship?' You could simply choose to yield to the other person's opinion. Allow yourself to move on by neither dwelling on whatever you disagreed about nor bringing it up again.
¶9How will humility help you if a disagreement persists?

Humility also helps if a disagreement drags on — don't argue just to justify your viewpoint. Set the goal of keeping your friend, kindly approach the person to settle the problem, be ready to admit your own part and apologize, and accept it humbly if your brother or sister apologizes too.

"Whoever forgives a transgression seeks love, But the one who keeps harping on a matter separates close friends."Prov. 17:9
"Really, it is already a defeat for you when you have lawsuits with one another. Why not rather let yourselves be wronged? Why do you not rather let yourselves be defrauded?"1 Cor. 6:7
"Turn away from what is bad and do what is good; Seek peace and pursue it."Ps. 34:14

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah doesn't keep "harping" on our past wrongs once we repent — a pattern of letting go that he wants us to imitate.
Humility can also help you when a disagreement persists. Do not argue to justify your viewpoint. (Read Proverbs 17:9; 1 Cor. 6:7.) You do well to set the goal of keeping your friend. Many have succeeded by kindly approaching the other person to settle the problem. (Ps. 34:14) You could say: 'We've been friends for some time. Can we talk about what happened?' Be prepared to admit your part in the disagreement, acknowledging any hurt feelings and then apologizing. And if your brother or sister also apologizes, accept it in a humble manner.
WHEN PERSONALITIES DIFFER
¶10What can make it difficult to remain friends?

Why is it a challenge? Personality differences — irritating traits, social skills shaped by past mistreatment or cultural background, or simply being outgoing versus shy — can make it hard to stay close to some brothers and sisters.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah created a rich variety of personalities; he doesn't expect everyone to be alike, and he treasures each individual.
Why is it a challenge? We might find it hard to maintain close friendships with brothers and sisters whose personalities differ from ours. Some may have traits that we find irritating. Others may lack certain social skills because of having been mistreated in the past or because of coming from cultures that either tend to be more reserved or tend to be more exuberant. Or we may just be opposites socially—we may be outgoing while they may be shy or vice versa.

Be realistic: make allowances for a shy friend's effort to converse, and don't lose patience with an outgoing friend either. We can't be best friends with everyone, but very different personalities can still work well together.

WHEN OUR BROTHERS AND SISTERS ARE SUFFERING
¶16What challenges may we face when our brothers and sisters are suffering in some way?

Why is it a challenge? Brothers and sisters who are suffering physically or emotionally may respond in ways we find hard to deal with — withdrawn, irritable, or even unpredictable, perhaps saying or doing things that hurt us. Because we may not be aware of what they're going through, we can end up expecting too much of them.

"If only my anguish could be fully weighed And put on scales together with my calamity! For now it is heavier than the sands of the seas. That is why my words have been wild talk."Job 6:2, 3

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah listened patiently even to Job's "wild talk" born of anguish — he understands the effect that intense suffering has on us.
Why is it a challenge? When our brothers and sisters are suffering physically or experiencing emotional distress, they may respond in ways that we find difficult to deal with. Learn from Jesus' kindness and compassion. When blind Bartimaeus caused a disturbance to get Jesus' attention, and some in the crowd found it hard to deal with, Jesus was "moved with pity" — his compassion led to kind, personal, healing action, not annoyance.

"Moved with pity, Jesus touched their eyes, and immediately they recovered their sight, and they followed him."Matt. 20:34

Summary answer: Actively seek peace, imitate Jesus' humility rather than insisting on your own way, and be willing to overlook minor offenses instead of dwelling on them.

💬 Personal comment: I want to remember that being "right" matters far less than preserving the friendship and staying at peace with my brother or sister.
…when your personalities differ?

Summary answer: Expect that friends, even close ones, will not always think or act the same way we do. Cultivate patience and humility, following Jesus' example of putting others first.

💬 Personal comment: I can be slower to judge a friend's different approach and quicker to look for what I can learn from it instead.
…when your brother or sister is suffering?

Summary answer: Apply the Golden Rule, showing fellow feeling, tender compassion, and humility. Offer practical help and comforting words, following the pattern Jesus set when he washed his disciples' feet.

💬 Personal comment: I want to be more proactive in noticing when someone is struggling instead of waiting to be asked for help.
