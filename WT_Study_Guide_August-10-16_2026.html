Introduction
1-2. What happened as Jesus was teaching in Capernaum?

Jesus said that unless people ate his flesh and drank his blood, they would have no life in themselves. Many disciples found this shocking and stopped following him — even though some of these same ones had earlier been so impressed by Jesus that they called him "the Prophet." Having a degree of faith wasn't enough; when their faith was tested, they didn't remain loyal.

Scriptures cited: John 6:60 John 6:53, 54 John 6:14

💬 Comment you could share: "What struck me was that these disciples weren't strangers to faith — they had already called Jesus 'the Prophet.' It shows that a degree of faith isn't the same as loyalty when things get hard to accept."
📌 Ministry connection: Useful when someone says a Bible teaching is "too hard to accept" — we can point out that even Jesus' own disciples struggled with some of his sayings, and it's normal to need to keep searching for understanding rather than walk away.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jesus didn't water down what he taught to keep the crowd — even Jehovah's own Son valued truth over popularity.
1 "THIS speech is shocking; who can listen to it?" That is the way that many of Jesus' disciples reacted to statements Jesus made while he was teaching in Capernaum. Jesus had said: "Unless you eat the flesh of the Son of man and drink his blood, you have no life in yourselves." And he continued: "Whoever feeds on my flesh and drinks my blood has everlasting life, and I will resurrect him on the last day."

2 Those who were deeply offended by Jesus' words stopped following him. Did they not have any faith in him at all? Actually, before this incident some — including Jesus' disciples — were so impressed by what Jesus did that they said: "This really is the Prophet who was to come into the world." So it is evident that those disciples had a degree of faith in Jesus. But when their faith was tested, they did not remain loyal to him.
"When they heard this, many of his disciples said: "This speech is shocking; who can listen to it?"" / ""So Jesus said to them: "Most truly I say to you, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of man and drink his blood, you have no life in yourselves. Whoever feeds on my flesh and drinks my blood has everlasting life, and I will resurrect him on the last day;"" / "When the people saw the sign he performed, they began to say: "This really is the Prophet who was to come into the world.""John 6:60; 6:53, 54; 6:14
3. How did the apostles react to Jesus' words, and why? (See also picture.)

Unlike the disciples who left, Peter answered that they had nowhere else to go, since Jesus had "sayings of everlasting life." Notice Peter didn't claim to fully understand what Jesus meant — the apostles chose loyalty even without complete understanding, because their faith rested on the undeniable truth that Jesus was "the Christ of God."

Scriptures cited: John 6:68, 69 Luke 9:20, 35

💬 Comment you could share: "I found it meaningful that Peter didn't claim to understand everything — he simply recognized he had nowhere better to go. That takes the pressure off needing every question answered before choosing to stay loyal."
📌 Ministry connection: Helpful when someone raises a hard Bible question we can't fully answer on the spot — we can be honest that we don't have every answer, while still expressing confidence in where the truth is found.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah doesn't require perfect understanding before we can be loyal to him — he values a heart that stays, even amid unanswered questions.
3 The apostles reacted differently to Jesus' words. When Jesus asked them if they wanted to leave him as well, Peter answered: "Lord, whom shall we go away to? You have sayings of everlasting life. We have believed and have come to know that you are the Holy One of God." Notice that Peter did not say that he and the other apostles fully understood the meaning of what Jesus said when he told them that they needed to eat his flesh and drink his blood. Regardless of what they understood at that moment, they chose to remain loyal to Jesus. Why? Because their faith was based on an undeniable truth — Jesus was "the Christ of God."
"Simon Peter answered him: "Lord, whom shall we go away to? You have sayings of everlasting life. We have believed and have come to know that you are the Holy One of God.""John 6:68, 69
Though many disciples left Jesus, Peter and the other apostles stayed with him (See paragraph 3)
Application: Loyalty doesn't require having every question answered — like the apostles, we can stay close to Jehovah even while some things remain hard to fully grasp.

Our loyalty can be tested by an adjustment in Bible understanding, a change in how the disciple-making work is organized, difficulty understanding why Jehovah allows a tragedy, or being hurt by a close friend or treated unfairly by someone we respect.

📌 Ministry connection: Good for showing a Bible student that testing of faith is normal and expected — the Bible itself addresses it directly rather than pretending faith is always easy.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah doesn't shield us from every hard question or every hurt — but he equips us with truths solid enough to stand on through them.
4 Our loyalty to Jehovah can be tested in a number of ways. The apostles were convinced of three truths: Jehovah is worthy of worship as the Maker of all things; Jesus is God's Son with "sayings of everlasting life"; and Jesus has Jehovah's full support. These same three truths — Jehovah is the Creator, Jehovah inspired the Bible, and Jehovah is backing his people today — can help us stay firmly grounded through any test.
