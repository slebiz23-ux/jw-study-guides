Bible Principles—Why Are They Important?
1-2. (a) Why can the Bible be called an ancient book? (b) Why is the Bible still relevant today?

(a) The Bible's writing began about 3,500 years ago and was completed nearly 2,000 years ago — yet "the word of God" found in it is still alive and powerful today (Heb. 4:12; 2 Tim. 3:16, 17).

(b) Two reasons: first, the Bible's message comes from the "living God," the all-wise Creator (1 Tim. 4:10; Rom. 16:26, 27). Second, the Bible reveals timeless principles that remain beneficial no matter the era or the challenges a person faces.

What Are Bible Principles?
4. What are Bible principles?

Bible principles are basic truths on which God's laws are based. Sometimes a principle is clearly stated within the law itself (Matt. 22:37). Principles are superior to specific laws — laws may be limited to a particular time or situation, but principles reflect Jehovah's thinking in a broader sense and are timeless (Ps. 119:111; Isa. 40:8). Laws may need to change, but principles never expire or become outdated.

💬 Comment you could share: "I like how the article puts it — a principle is 'the reason behind the law.' That helped me see that Jehovah isn't just giving commands; he's sharing his thinking with us."
4 Bible principles are basic truths on which God's laws are based. At times, a principle may be clearly stated within the law. Principles are, however, superior to specific laws. Specific laws may be limited in application. They may be given for a particular time or situation. But Bible principles reflect Jehovah's thinking in a broader sense. Principles could be described as the reason behind the law. There is a valid reason behind every one of Jehovah's laws. But the principles on which they are based can be applied in a number of different circumstances and are timeless. Laws may need to change, but principles do not expire or become outdated.
5. How can we illustrate the difference between laws and principles?

Think of a mother telling a small child, "Don't touch the stove." That's a law. But the principle behind it — don't touch something hot or you'll get hurt — applies to a clothing iron, a heater, or anything else that could cause injury, and it applies outside the home too. As the child grows and eventually uses the stove himself, the law changes, but the principle behind it remains the same.

📌 Ministry connection: This illustration is easy to use when explaining to a householder why Bible "rules" written thousands of years ago can still guide decisions today — the principle behind the rule never goes out of date.
5 To help us better understand the difference between laws and principles, consider this illustration. A mother may say to a small child, 'Don't touch the stove.' That is a command or a law. But the principle, or thinking, behind that law is: Don't touch something that is hot; otherwise, you will get hurt. That principle is not just limited to a stove. It could be applied to a clothing iron, a heater, or any other hot item that could cause injury. That principle does not just apply in the family's home but applies elsewhere too. Of course, as the child grows older, he will probably use the stove. However, he will still need to be careful not to touch it in a way that would harm him. So the law may change, but the basic principle behind it remains.
Why Are Bible Principles Important?
6. (a) What does Jehovah provide for us in his Word? (b) How does Jehovah dignify us?

(a) Out of love, Jehovah gives us specific direction in the form of laws so we can avoid danger (Jas. 2:11). He also helps us understand the thinking behind those laws, and by means of his Word he guides us in many areas of life not covered by specific laws.

(b) By providing principles rather than an exhaustive rulebook, Jehovah dignifies us and allows us the freedom to make decisions that prove our love for him and his standards (Gal. 5:13).

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah could have given us a rule for every possible situation, but instead he trusts us to reason from principles. That says something beautiful about how he views us — not as children who need to be micromanaged, but as loved ones capable of proving our devotion.
6 Out of love for us, Jehovah gives us specific direction in the form of laws so that we can avoid danger. Additionally, he helps us to understand the principles, or the thinking, behind those laws. By means of his Word, he provides us with his guidance in many areas of life that may not be governed by specific laws. Bible principles help us to make good decisions in life and to do what is right. By providing these principles, Jehovah dignifies us and allows us the freedom to make decisions that prove our love for him and his standards.
7. How can we illustrate the value of Bible principles?

Think of road signs along a highway. Some indicate laws with penalties (speed limits, stop signs). But other signs simply warn of possible dangers — a slippery road, animals crossing. An experienced driver uses good judgment and drives more cautiously when he sees these warning signs, adjusting for rain or snow. Similarly, Christians strictly avoid conduct specifically condemned in God's Word, but they also use good judgment to avoid thoughts or actions that could lead them to break God's laws.

7 To illustrate how Bible principles can help us, think of the road signs that drivers see along highways in some countries. Many of these signs inform us of laws that carry penalties for those who disregard them. These signs may specify the maximum speed limit or indicate when a driver must bring his vehicle to a stop. However, many other signs alert drivers to possible dangers. For example, these signs may warn that the road might be slippery or that animals might be crossing the road. An experienced driver uses good judgment and drives cautiously when he sees signs that alert him to dangers. For example, if it is raining or snowing, the driver will adjust his driving in these dangerous conditions. Similarly, while Christians strictly avoid any conduct that is specifically condemned in God's Word, they also avoid any thoughts or actions that could lead them to break God's laws. This calls for good judgment on their part.

Searching for Bible principles and living by them trains us to understand Jehovah's thinking on matters. When reading Bible accounts, we learn to look for practical lessons, asking why Jehovah included them and how we can benefit. In this way we learn to use our "power of reason" to serve Jehovah, proving to ourselves "the good and acceptable and perfect will of God."

How Can We Identify Bible Principles?
10. How can we identify Bible principles?

When we search the Bible for lessons, we find principles that help us understand how Jehovah thinks and feels about matters. We can also identify principles by looking for the reasons behind certain laws — but understanding God's thinking requires prayer and developing our own thinking ability (Prov. 2:10-12). Helpful questions: 'Why did God give this law? If Jehovah does not approve of this conduct, how would he feel about similar conduct? What lesson do I learn, and how can I apply it?'

📌 Ministry connection: These three questions are a great tool to share with a Bible student who wants to move beyond memorizing rules to actually reasoning the way Jehovah does.
10 When we search the Bible for lessons, we find principles that help us to understand how Jehovah thinks and feels about matters. We can also identify principles when we look for the reasons behind certain laws. The more we understand the reasons, the more we understand Jehovah. But in order to understand God's thinking, we must pray to him for help and we must develop our own thinking ability. We can ask ourselves such questions as: 'Why did God give this law? If Jehovah does not approve of this conduct, how would he feel about similar conduct? What lesson do I learn from this Bible account, and how can I apply it in my life?' Once we identify the reasons and the lessons behind the laws and the accounts in the Bible, we will be able to make good decisions in life — decisions that are in harmony with Jehovah's will.
11. In what way did Jesus show us how to identify Bible principles in his Sermon on the Mount?

In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus first stated the law, then revealed the thinking, or principle, behind it. We'll examine three examples: the laws against murder, adultery, and hating one's enemy — and the principles behind them.

11 In his Sermon on the Mount, Jesus showed us how we can identify Bible principles. Let us examine three examples. In each case, Jesus first stated the law. Then he revealed the thinking, or the principle, behind that law. As we meditate on what Jesus revealed, we will see how these principles can be applied in our modern world. At the same time, we will discern the practical benefits of these life lessons.
12-13. What is one principle behind the law at Matthew 5:21, 22, and how can it be applied today?

(12) "You must not murder." The principle: Jehovah does not want us to hate others — in action, word, or even thought. Jesus showed that a person who "continues wrathful with his brother" or verbally abuses him is "accountable," even without committing murder — because these attitudes are what lead to murder (1 John 3:15).

"'You heard that it was said to those of ancient times: "You must not murder, but whoever commits a murder will be accountable to the court of justice." However, I say to you that everyone who continues wrathful with his brother will be accountable to the court of justice; and whoever addresses his brother with an unspeakable word of contempt will be accountable to the Supreme Court; whereas whoever says, 'You despicable fool!' will be liable to the fiery Gehenna.'" Matt. 5:21, 22

(13) How to apply it: avoid becoming resentful or holding a grudge (Lev. 19:18; Job 36:13), because such feelings can stir up hatred that leads to hurtful speech (Prov. 10:12) — including gossip and slander that damage others' reputations (Prov. 20:19; 25:23). Jesus' principle applies just as much to social media, texting, and online comments as to spoken words.

12 Read Matthew 5:21, 22. "You must not murder." What is one principle behind this law? Jehovah does not want us to hate others — in action, word, or even thought. Jesus indicated that even though a person may not commit an act of murder, he could still violate the reason for that law if he hates his brother. If someone "continues wrathful with his brother" or verbally abuses him, the individual who is wrathful would still be "accountable." 13 How can the principle found at Matthew 5:21, 22 be applied today? We should avoid becoming resentful or holding a grudge. Why? Because these feelings can stir up hatred in our heart, which may lead to hurtful speech or actions. This includes gossip and slander that can damage or ruin the reputation of others. So even before there were such things as social media, blogs, the Internet, or messaging apps, Jesus provided a principle that would alert mature Christians to the possible dangers when using these modern tools. In all situations, though, we should avoid hateful speech that could lead to what is often called character assassination.
14-15. What is the principle behind the law at Matthew 5:27, 28, and how can it be applied today?

(14) "You must not commit adultery." The principle: Jehovah hates not only immorality but also thoughts that can lead to immoral conduct. A married man who keeps looking at another woman with passion has already sinned (Matt. 5:29, 30). This principle applies to single Christians too.

"'You heard that it was said: "You must not commit adultery." But I say to you that everyone who keeps on looking at a woman so as to have a passion for her has already committed adultery with her in his heart.'" Matt. 5:27, 28

(15) How to apply it: avoid dwelling on immoral desires (2 Sam. 11:2-4; Job 31:1-3) — this means avoiding all forms of pornography, including "soft porn," sexting, or virtual activities that mimic immorality. This principle helps a married person avoid dealing treacherously with a marriage mate (Mal. 2:15) and helps all Christians avoid anything leading to sexual immorality (Prov. 5:3-14).

14 Read Matthew 5:27, 28. "You must not commit adultery." What is the principle behind this law? Jehovah hates not only immorality but also thoughts that can lead to immoral conduct. Jesus explained that if a married man continues to look at a woman (other than his wife) in such a way as to have a passion for her, he has sinned. 15 How can the principle found at Matthew 5:27, 28 be applied today? We should avoid dwelling on immoral desires. Obviously, then, a mature Christian avoids all forms of pornography. He does not reason that he is only looking at immorality, so he is not doing anything wrong. He does not try to excuse such activity as simply looking at "soft porn," which some consider to be less offensive than more explicit forms of pornography. In Jesus' day, there were no electronic devices, movies, or photographs. Even so, Jesus gave us a principle that teaches us how Jehovah feels about the material that might be published on such modern devices. This helps us understand that Jehovah would be greatly displeased by such modern practices as sexting or engaging in virtual activities that mimic sexual immorality. Heeding this principle helps a married person to avoid dealing treacherously with his or her marriage mate. It also helps all Christians — married or single — to avoid anything that could lead to sexual immorality.
16-17. What is the principle behind the law at Matthew 5:43, 44, and what is the benefit of applying it?

(16) "You must love your neighbor." The principle: Jehovah wants us to view everyone as our neighbor and show them love — regardless of race or nationality (Matt. 5:45-48). The Jews of Jesus' day had misapplied the law to justify hating enemies, but Jesus corrected that misunderstanding.

"'You heard that it was said: "You must love your neighbor and hate your enemy." However, I say to you: Continue to love your enemies and to pray for those who persecute you, so that you may prove yourselves sons of your Father who is in the heavens.'" Matt. 5:43, 44

(17) Benefits: love of neighbor moves us to refuse involvement in this world's wars and conflicts (Isa. 2:4; Mic. 4:3); it helps us deal kindly with people of different backgrounds (Acts 10:34, 35); and it helps us forgive those who have hurt us (Matt. 18:21, 22; Mark 11:25; Luke 17:3, 4).

It will help us to deal kindly with those who are from a different racial, national, or religious background. It will also help us to forgive others who have hurt us or have hurt those whom we love.
