Jer. 48:6

“Flee, escape for your lives! You must become like a juniper tree in the wilderness.”Jeremiah 48:6 (NWTsty)

Insight on the Scriptures, Vol. 2, “Juniper” No. 2 (fetched) explains that the Hebrew word (ʽaroh·ʽerʹ / ʽar·ʽarʹ) points to a shrublike juniper (probably Juniperus phoenicia) found in the Sinai region and Edom’s desert. The root idea is “nakedness” or being “stripped,” and the dwarf juniper is described as rather gloomy in appearance, growing in rocky parts of the desert and on crags. It is used at Jeremiah 17:5, 6 for a man whose heart turns from Jehovah and at Jeremiah 48:6 in warning the Moabites to take flight.

💬 Comment you could share:“The Moabites were told to flee like a lone, stripped juniper in the wilderness — living in a barren place with little security. It pictures how everything they had trusted in would be taken away, and only those who fled would survive.” 📌 Personal application:“It reminds me that when Jehovah gives a warning, listening and acting quickly matters.”

Passage summary: Jehovah announces woe on Moab’s cities (v. 1-5). The people are told to flee (v. 6), warned that trust in their works and treasures will not save them (v. 7), that the destroyer will come on every city (v. 8), and that those who carry out Jehovah’s mission neglectfully are cursed (v. 10). Moab’s long ease is pictured as wine left undisturbed on its dregs (v. 11), and Jehovah promises to send men to overturn them (v. 12), after which Moab will be ashamed of its god Chemosh (v. 13).

📕 CONGREGATION BIBLE STUDY — wcg chap. 14
Rahab tying a scarlet cord in the window of her house.
Application: The window cord was a small, visible act of obedience. Rahab trusted the spies’ promise and did exactly what they said. Ask yourself where Jehovah may be asking you for a simple but obedient step.

Narrative summary

Rahab lived in Jericho and heard of how Jehovah had defeated Pharaoh and the Amorite kings. Rather than fear, she felt hope and was drawn to Jehovah. When the king demanded the Israelite spies, she hid them on her roof and misdirected the king’s men. She asked for protection for her family, tied the scarlet cord in her window, stayed in the house with her family, and was spared when the walls fell. She joined Israel, married, and became an ancestor of Jesus.

Dig Deeper

Rahab was a prostitute (Josh. 2:1), living in a house built on the city wall (Josh. 2:15). According to the chapter narrative, she saw gloom and fear on every face, and she may have yearned for something better than her degrading life.

💬 Comment:“Rahab’s life was harsh and degrading, yet what she heard about Jehovah gave her hope instead of dread.”

Joshua sent the men to “inspect the land” (Josh. 2:1), and a prostitute’s house was a place where strangers would not draw attention. The account shows they were on a mission, not seeking immorality: Rahab hid them (2:4), they spoke with her only about the mission and her request for protection (2:122:14), and the Bible later praises her faith, saying she “received the spies in a peaceable way” (Heb. 11:31).

The Bible says she had hidden the men “among stalks of flax laid in rows on the roof” (Josh. 2:6). Flax stalks were laid out to dry; flax was the source of linen fibers. Check it “Flax” ¶2-4 for the publication’s exact explanation.

The king’s men had no right to the information they wanted — they intended to harm Jehovah’s servants. Rahab said: Josh. 2:42:5. Compare how James 2:24, 25 says she was “declared righteous by works” when she received the messengers hospitably and sent them out by another way.
