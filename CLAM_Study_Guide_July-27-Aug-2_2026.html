1. He Was a Bold Preacher

▼
Jeremiah fearlessly proclaiming Jehovah's message in public. Some refuse to listen while others shout at him.
🖼️ Image application The illustration shows how mixed the response to our message can be — some walk away, others react with hostility. It's a reminder that Jeremiah kept speaking even when the reaction was ugly, and that the content of the message, not the audience's reaction, is what determines whether we keep proclaiming it.

Jeremiah was persecuted for delivering Jehovah's judgments.

Jer. 20:2Jer. 20:8
“Then Pashʹhur struck Jeremiah the prophet and put him in the stocks that were at the Upper Gate of Benjamin, which was in the house of Jehovah.”Jeremiah 20:2, NWT Study Edition
“For whenever I speak, I must cry out and proclaim, “Violence and destruction!” For me the word of Jehovah has been the cause of insults and jeering all day long.”Jeremiah 20:8, NWT Study Edition

Jeremiah became discouraged and considered giving up.

Jer. 20:9
“So I said: “I am not going to make mention of him, And I will speak no more in his name.” But in my heart it became like a burning fire shut up in my bones, And I was tired of holding it in; I could no longer endure it.”Jeremiah 20:9, NWT Study Edition

Jeremiah was able to continue preaching boldly because of Jehovah's word and His support.

Jer. 20:11
“But Jehovah was with me like a fearsome warrior. That is why those persecuting me will stumble and will not prevail. They will be put to great shame, for they will not succeed. Their everlasting humiliation will not be forgotten.”Jeremiah 20:11, NWT Study Edition

Further reading cited in the workbook: w11 3/15 p.30 ¶9 and w10 2/15 p.7 ¶8‑9 (Watchtower Library / Online Library).

💬 Comment you could share "I found it interesting that even though Jeremiah felt like giving up, he says the message became 'like a burning fire' inside him — he couldn't hold it in. That shows real conviction wasn't something he could just switch off."
📌 Personal application There are days I don't feel like starting a conversation in the ministry. Jeremiah's honesty about wanting to quit — and then not being able to stay silent — reminds me that feelings of reluctance don't have to be the last word.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah Jehovah didn't leave Jeremiah to face persecution alone; he calls himself "a fearsome warrior" on Jeremiah's behalf. That tells me Jehovah personally takes note of what his servants go through for speaking up for him.

2. Spiritual Gems

▼
Jer. 20:12 — What does this verse motivate us to do? Jer. 20:12
“But you, O Jehovah of armies, are examining the righteous one; You see the innermost thoughts and the heart. Let me see your vengeance on them, For to you I have committed my legal case.”Jeremiah 20:12, NWT Study Edition
Jeremiah didn't try to avenge himself against Pashhur or those mocking him — he handed the matter over to Jehovah, confident that Jehovah "examines" hearts and motives with perfect accuracy. This motivates us to leave judgment of unfair treatment in Jehovah's hands rather than reacting in anger or trying to get even. (Cited source: w13 3/15 p.10 ¶11.)

3. Jeremiah 20:7‑18

▼

This reading is Jeremiah's unusually candid prayer after being beaten and publicly humiliated by Pashhur. It moves through raw complaint (v.7‑10), renewed confidence in Jehovah (v.11‑13), and back into deep personal anguish (v.14‑18) — read as an outburst of genuine emotion, not a scripted speech, so vary your pace and tone between the discouraged verses and the confident ones.

Jer. 20:14‑18
“Cursed be the day I was born! May the day my mother gave birth to me not be blessed! … Why did I have to come out of the womb To see trouble and grief, To end my days in shame?”Jeremiah 20:14, 18, NWT Study Edition

Teaching quality (th study 2 — Read with feeling and appropriate tone): Because this reading contains such an emotional swing, pause briefly at the end of verse 13 before dropping into the lament of verse 14 — the shift itself communicates how real Jeremiah's struggle was.

💬 Key-verse comment "What struck me in verse 11 is that right in the middle of describing his suffering, Jeremiah suddenly declares, 'Jehovah was with me like a fearsome warrior.' He didn't wait until his circumstances improved to say that — he said it while still in the middle of the trial."

4. Starting a Conversation

▼

Goal: find a natural way to let the person know you are one of Jehovah's Witnesses. (lmd lesson 2 point 4)

Setting: Publisher strikes up a conversation with a cashier/co‑worker/neighbor in an everyday setting.

Publisher: "Busy day today! Do you get much of a break, or is it go-go-go the whole shift?"

← lmd lesson 2 pt.4: opens with genuine, ordinary small talk — no scripted "spiritual" opener yet.

Householder: "Barely ten minutes! It's been non-stop."

Publisher: "I hear you. Actually, that's part of why I like what I do on the weekends — I get out and talk with people, kind of the opposite of rushing around. I'm one of Jehovah's Witnesses, and we're out in the neighborhood this week."

← lmd lesson 2 pt.4: identifies himself naturally, growing out of the conversation already happening, rather than announcing it as an opening line.

Householder: "Oh okay, I've seen you all around."

Publisher: "We have been! I'd love to leave you something short to look at whenever you do get a real break — would that be alright?"

📌 Personal application This approach works because it doesn't force a spiritual topic into a conversation that hasn't earned it yet — it lets genuine interest in the person come first.

7. Be Adaptable — Show Personal Interest

▼

Before going out in the ministry, we might prepare to start a conversation on a topic many in the territory are thinking about (lmd lesson 1 point 3). But what if a different topic would serve a particular householder better? Before sharing the good news with a blind man, Jesus first showed him kindness.

John 9:1John 9:6, 7John 9:35‑38
“As he was passing along, he saw a man who had been blind from birth.”John 9:1, NWT Study Edition
“After he said these things, he spat on the ground and made a paste with the saliva, and he smeared the paste on the man's eyes and said to him: “Go wash in the pool of Si·loʹam” (which is translated “Sent Forth”). And he went and washed, and came back seeing.”John 9:6, 7, NWT Study Edition
“Jesus heard that they had thrown him out, and on finding him, he said: “Are you putting faith in the Son of man?” The man answered: “And who is he, sir, so that I may put faith in him?” Jesus said to him: “You have seen him, and in fact, he is the one speaking with you.” He said: “I do put faith in him, Lord.” And he did obeisance to him.”John 9:35‑38, NWT Study Edition

When someone senses that we genuinely care, he is more likely to listen to our message. So when preaching house to house, it's important to imagine how the householder feels and to demonstrate personal interest.

Read 1 Corinthians 9:20‑23.

1 Cor. 9:20‑23
“To the Jews I became as a Jew in order to gain Jews; to those under law I became as under law, though I myself am not under law, in order to gain those under law. To those without law I became as without law, although I am not without law toward God but under law toward Christ, in order to gain those without law. To the weak I became weak, in order to gain the weak. I have become all things to people of all sorts, so that I might by all possible means save some. But I do all things for the sake of the good news, in order to share it with others.”1 Corinthians 9:20‑23, NWT Study Edition
How did Paul show that he cared about people?
Paul deliberately adjusted his approach to fit each audience — becoming "as a Jew" to Jews, adapting to those "under law" and those "without law," and becoming "weak" to the weak — not to change his message, but so that people from very different backgrounds would feel he understood and respected them.
Why did Paul make the effort to adapt his message?
His stated motive: "so that I might by all possible means save some" — his focus was on the individual's salvation, not on personal convenience or a fixed script.

8. Chapter 1 — Enoch: "He Walked With God"

▼

wcg intro to Section 1, timeline, and chapter 1.

Enoch proclaiming God's judgment message to people on a busy street. Some in the group look angry and annoyed, and some have weapons.
🖼️ Image application The armed, hostile crowd shows just how dangerous Enoch's assignment was — he wasn't reciting a message to friendly listeners, he was confronting an angry, violent culture. It's a picture worth holding onto whenever declaring Bible truth feels socially risky.

Enoch was the only man of his time whom the Bible describes as one who "kept walking with the true God." He stood apart from the world of his day in two ways: in what he refused to do, and in what he did. Cain's violent, godless spirit had spread through the human family, and Enoch refused to be shaped by it, remaining a man of peace. He also refused to take part in the blasphemous, counterfeit "calling on the name of Jehovah" practiced by some in his generation.

Gen. 4:23, 24Gen. 4:26
“Then Laʹmech composed these words for his wives Aʹdah and Zilʹlah: “Hear my voice, you wives of Laʹmech; Give ear to my saying: A man I have killed for wounding me, Yes, a young man for striking me. If 7 times Cain is to be avenged, Then Laʹmech 77 times.””Genesis 4:23, 24, NWT Study Edition
“There was also born to Seth a son, and he named him Eʹnosh. At that time people began calling on the name of Jehovah.”Genesis 4:26, NWT Study Edition

God gave Enoch a special assignment — to serve as a prophet, delivering a strong judgment message to a hostile audience. Enoch's prophecy, quoted centuries later by Jude, was worded as if God's judgment had already happened, showing how certain its fulfillment was:

Jude 14, 15
“Yes, the seventh one in line from Adam, Eʹnoch, also prophesied about them when he said: “Look! Jehovah came with his holy myriads to execute judgment against all, and to convict all the ungodly concerning all their ungodly deeds that they did in an ungodly way, and concerning all the shocking things that ungodly sinners spoke against him.””Jude 14, 15, NWT Study Edition

When persecution against Enoch grew dangerous, Jehovah lovingly transferred him from the dangers of life into peaceful death, rather than allowing enemies to harm him.

Gen. 5:21‑24Heb. 11:5
“Eʹnoch lived for 65 years and then became father to Me·thuʹse·lah. After becoming father to Me·thuʹse·lah, Eʹnoch continued to walk with the true God for 300 years. And he became father to sons and daughters. So all the days of Eʹnoch amounted to 365 years. Eʹnoch kept walking with the true God. Then he was no more, for God took him.”Genesis 5:21‑24, NWT Study Edition
“By faith Eʹnoch was transferred so as not to see death, and he was nowhere to be found because God had transferred him; for before he was transferred he received the witness that he had pleased God well.”Hebrews 11:5, NWT Study Edition

Printed Questions

For discussion: In what ways did Enoch show courage?
He refused to imitate the violence and false religion of his contemporaries, and he obeyed an assignment to deliver an unpopular, confrontational message to a hostile audience — fully aware it could cost him his life.
1. How might people have begun "calling on the name of Jehovah" (Gen. 4:26) in a way Enoch refused to join?
The Bible's later description suggests this early "calling on the name of Jehovah" was corrupted into a form of false worship — invoking Jehovah's name blasphemously rather than sincerely — which is why Enoch, though living among such people, still stood apart. (Source cited in the chapter: w01 9/15 p.29 ¶3.)
2. Enoch was the first faithful human to do what? How was he able to fulfill his assignment?
Enoch was the first faithful prophet of God recorded in the Bible. He was able to fulfill the assignment because of his close relationship with Jehovah — described as "walking with" God — which gave him the courage to deliver a message he knew would provoke hostility. (Source cited: w01 9/15 p.31 ¶5.)
3. Even before he started prophesying, why would Enoch likely have known his courage would be tested?
Enoch lived among people descended from Cain, whose murderous, defiant spirit toward Jehovah was well known within the family line; anyone who chose to remain loyal to Jehovah in that environment could expect opposition. (Source cited: w06 10/1 p.19 ¶13‑14.)
4. How could you show that Enoch was not taken to heaven, as some believe?
John 3:13 Heb. 11:13
“Moreover, no man has ascended into heaven but the one who descended from heaven, the Son of man.”John 3:13, NWT Study Edition
“In faith all of these died, although they did not receive the fulfillment of the promises; but they saw them from a distance and welcomed them and publicly declared that they were strangers and temporary residents in the land.”Hebrews 11:13, NWT Study Edition
Jesus stated plainly that no man had ascended to heaven before him, and Hebrews groups Enoch among the faithful ones who "died" without receiving the fulfillment of God's promises — both facts rule out the idea that Enoch went to heaven.

Reflect on the Lessons

When might we especially need courage to preach? Based on Enoch's example, what can help us?
Situations where the message is unwelcome — at work, among unbelieving relatives, or in a territory hostile to religion — call for the same kind of courage Enoch needed. What helped him was his ongoing relationship with Jehovah ("walking with" him), not a personality trait; cultivating that same closeness through prayer and Bible study strengthens our own courage.
How does Jehovah feel when we walk courageously with him? Prov. 27:11
“Be wise, my son, and make my heart rejoice, So that I can make a reply to him who taunts me.”Proverbs 27:11, NWT Study Edition
Jehovah says our wise, courageous conduct literally makes his heart rejoice — it isn't a small thing to him. Enoch likewise "received the witness that he had pleased God well" before he died.
How might you imitate Enoch's courage in your life?
Open comment — think of one specific situation this week (a conversation at work, with a relative, or in the ministry) where speaking up for Jehovah will take deliberate courage.

Wrap-Up Questions

What does this account teach us about Jehovah?
Jehovah notices and rewards quiet, personal loyalty even when it isn't publicly celebrated in the moment — and he actively protects his loyal ones, even through means as unexpected as bringing Enoch's life to a peaceful end rather than letting him suffer at his enemies' hands.
Are there any points from this lesson we can use in the ministry or our daily life?
Enoch's example shows that "walking with God" — staying in step with him through consistent prayer, study, and obedience — is what equips ordinary people for uncomfortable assignments, whether that's a bold conversation in the ministry or standing firm among people who don't share our values.
