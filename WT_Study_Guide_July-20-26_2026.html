Introduction — What Decisions Do Young People Face? ▸
1-2. (a) What decisions do young people face? (b) What is meant by the term "additional education"?
(a) Young people are often asked what kind of work they'd like to do when they grow up. While full-time service is the best "career" a Christian can choose, everyone still needs to support themselves financially, so many young ones wonder whether they should pursue additional education, and if so, what type.
(b) "Additional education" refers to any secular education or training beyond the basic education provided by the government — including universities, apprenticeships, trade schools, technical schools, vocational training, short courses, and continuing education.

Should You Obtain Additional Education? ▸
3. Why might some Christians consider obtaining additional education?

In some places a person can earn an adequate living without further education. In other places, additional education may help someone find employment that pays better or has better hours — which could free up more time for preaching or other theocratic activities. But additional education also requires sacrifices and presents real challenges.

💬 Comment you could share: "What struck me was that this isn't a one-size-fits-all answer — it really depends on where you live and what opportunities are realistically available."
📌 Ministry connection: Helpful when talking with someone discouraged about job prospects — showing that Jehovah's organization doesn't hand down a single rule, but principles that respect each person's circumstances.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah doesn't micromanage every detail of our lives; he trusts us to reason on principles. That shows the kind of respect he has for our ability to make good decisions.

4. Who should decide if a Christian will obtain additional education?

The Bible says that each person must "carry his own load of responsibility" Gal. 6:5. So each adult Christian must make his or her own decision about whether to obtain additional education. Parents are responsible for decisions regarding their young child's education Eph. 6:1, and their guidance can prepare their child to make wise decisions about education once he is an adult Prov. 22:6.

💬 Comment you could share: "I found it interesting that this decision is placed squarely on the individual — not on the elders, not on the branch, but on each Christian personally."
📌 Ministry connection: Useful with parents who ask "what does your religion say about college?" — you can explain there's no blanket rule, just personal responsibility guided by Bible principles.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah gives us the dignity of carrying our own load — he's a God who builds our decision-making ability rather than dictating every choice.

Young people often need to decide before finishing their required schooling, so it's wise to discuss the topic with parents well before the decision point — giving time to research options together. But a decision isn't always necessary before finishing school Prov. 21:5. Some have chosen to finish school, find work, and start pioneering while they determine whether to obtain additional education.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah's timing isn't rigid — he values careful, unhurried planning over hasty decisions.
5 Young people often need to make a decision about whether they will pursue additional education before they finish their required schooling. Of course, it is wise for a young person to discuss this topic with his parents well before he has to decide. This will give them time to work together, researching different options. They may be able to choose subjects at school that will prepare him for his future education or employment. Do they have to make a decision before the young person finishes his required schooling? Not necessarily. Prov. 21:5 Some young ones have chosen to finish school, find work, and start pioneering while they determine whether to obtain additional education. Be sure to pray about the matter Jas. 1:5. Furthermore, take two steps: first, honestly analyze your motives Ps. 26:2; second, carefully analyze the education you are considering Prov. 14:15.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah, who "gives generously... and without reproaching" (Jas. 1:5), welcomes our prayers even about "ordinary" life decisions.

Analyze Your Motives ▸
7. What care should Christians take when considering additional education?

A key question to ask is, "Why do I want this education?" Wanting a job you'll enjoy or that pays better isn't necessarily wrong 1 Tim. 5:8. However, the Bible warns of the dangers of materialism and seeking security in this system of things Prov. 23:4, 5, 1 Tim. 6:8-10, 1 John 2:17. A motive of obtaining wealth or prestige could leave a person feeling empty and could cause him to drift from Jehovah.

💬 Comment you could share: "What struck me was that wanting to provide for family isn't the wrong motive to watch for — it's wanting wealth or status for their own sake."
📌 Ministry connection: Helpful when discussing materialism with someone in the territory who equates more education with a "better life."
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah sees past our outward plans to our heart's real motives — he wants our friendship with him, not our resumé, to be central.
7 If you are thinking of obtaining further education, a key question to ask yourself is, 'Why do I want to obtain this form of education?' Not necessarily. 1 Tim. 5:8 However, the Bible warns us of the dangers of materialism and of seeking security in this system of things. Prov. 23:4, 5; 1 Tim. 6:8-10; 1 John 2:17 If your motive is to obtain wealth or prestige, not only might you be left feeling empty but you could also drift away from Jehovah.

Analyze the Education ▸
10. How can the principle found at Deuteronomy 32:29 be applied when deciding on additional education?

Consider other possibilities besides the one specific course you have in mind — compare Prov. 18:17. Also consider whether you could support yourself without further education at all, as Johanna from Finland did. Weigh whether the advantages are worth the disadvantages — read Deuteronomy 32:29; 1 Cor. 10:23.

💬 Comment you could share: "I found it interesting that not getting additional education is treated as a completely legitimate option too, not just a fallback."
📌 Ministry connection: Encouraging for a young publisher who feels pressure to pursue college simply because 'everyone else is.'
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah always keeps his promise to provide for those who put him first, as Johanna experienced firsthand.
10 You may have in mind a specific course or training program. But it is also useful to consider other possibilities — either in the same field or in other fields. (Compare Prov. 18:17.) Today, there are education options that did not exist in the past, such as online courses. And do not forget to consider whether you could support yourself without further education. For example, Johanna, from Finland, did not get any additional education. She says: "After I finished school, I started working part-time and pioneering. I've had a variety of jobs, and I've seen how Jehovah always keeps his promise to provide what we need." Remember that all decisions regarding additional education — whether to obtain it and what type to obtain — involve advantages and disadvantages. So ask yourself: 'Are the advantages worth the disadvantages?' (Read Deuteronomy 32:29; 1 Cor. 10:23.) Let us consider some factors that will help you answer that question.
11. Why is it important to consider the schedule of the education or training? (See also pictures.)

Consider how many hours a week you'd spend on classes, training, and homework — will there still be time for your spiritual routine and family obligations? Phil. 1:10. Jeroz found the schedule left him too tired for meeting preparation and personal study, and he missed meetings — in hindsight, not worth it. Rabeca, by contrast, found a two-hour-a-day course that let her keep pioneering.

💬 Comment you could share: "What struck me was Jeroz's honesty — he said looking back, the course wasn't worth the time and energy it cost him spiritually."
📌 Ministry connection: A practical talking point when discussing work-life-spiritual balance with someone juggling studies.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah cares whether we have "time" for him — he doesn't want to be squeezed into the leftover hours of a busy schedule.
11 The schedule. Consider how many hours you will spend each week attending classes, receiving training, and doing homework. Will you still have time for your spiritual routine and family obligations? Phil. 1:10 Could your secular studies leave you feeling too tired mentally for meeting preparation or personal study? This was the case for Jeroz, from India. He says: "I struggled to focus on spiritual activities. At times, I missed meetings. Looking back, I realize that the course was not worth the time and energy." Then again, some courses are not so time-consuming; some are part-time, others require little homework. For example, Rabeca, from Mozambique, is happy with her choice of education. She says: "The course was only two hours a day. It allowed me to continue regular pioneering."
Application: Picture the full week — meeting duties, ministry, secular work, and class — before committing, not after.
12. How can a person make the best use of his time with respect to education?

Consider the length of the program — would it be the best use of your time? Eph. 5:15-17. If young, will it still let you give Jehovah your best during your youth, perhaps in full-time ministry? Read Ecclesiastes 12:1. Shorter options like vocational training or trade schools may give practical skills faster and cheaper than universities, as Mario's two-year technical course did.

💬 Comment you could share: "I found it interesting that a shorter, practical course is presented as just as valid a choice as a longer university degree — maybe more so for some."
📌 Ministry connection: Useful when someone assumes a four-year degree is the only "real" path to a career.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Remembering our Grand Creator "in the days of your youth" (Eccl. 12:1) means giving him our best years, not our leftover ones.
12 The length. Consider how many months or years it will take to complete the education program. Would acquiring that education be the best use of your time? Eph. 5:15-17 If you are young, will the education program allow you to give Jehovah your best during your youth, perhaps in the full-time ministry? (Read Ecclesiastes 12:1.) Are there shorter education options in the same field? For example, vocational training and trade schools may give you practical skills in less time and at less cost than universities. Mario, from Chile, says: "I chose a two-year technical course that was cheaper than a university course. I had classes only four days a week, which permitted me to pioneer while taking the course."
13. How could a person be affected by where he lives while obtaining additional education?

Moving away from family — especially to room with people who don't share your faith — poses a serious spiritual danger Prov. 22:3, 1 Cor. 15:33. Matias regretted an inexpensive course that required boarding school, where he faced daily temptation. A Russian sister avoided danger by living at home instead of on campus.

💬 Comment you could share: "What struck me was that the cheapest course isn't always the wisest choice — Matias's course was inexpensive but cost him spiritually."
📌 Ministry connection: Good discussion point with parents concerned about a child moving away for school.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah warns us, out of love, about the danger of bad associations — he wants to protect our relationship with him, not restrict us needlessly.
13 The location. You may find an education program that is close to home. But what if you find a program in another city? Or what if the education program requires you to live on campus? Be aware that moving away from your family poses a serious spiritual danger, especially if you will room with people who do not share your faith. Prov. 22:3; 1 Cor. 15:33 Matias, from Mozambique, completed an inexpensive one-year course. Yet, he regrets his decision. Why? He says: "I had to spend the entire time at a boarding school. I was tempted daily to do wrong things. I recommend taking a course that will not separate you from your family." A sister from Russia says: "I avoided dangers and temptations by living at home with my parents instead of on campus or in a dormitory." You may even be able to find an online course.
14. How can the principle found at Luke 14:28 help you when considering additional education?

Calculate the full cost before committing, the way a builder calculates a tower's expense — read Luke 14:28. Ask: How much will it cost? Can I or my family afford it? Is there a cheaper option? If debt is involved, how long to pay it off, and will future income sustain both living costs and the loan? Prov. 22:7. Adilson's family sacrificed food and other basic needs to fund his four-year course — a cost he now regrets.

💬 Comment you could share: "I found it interesting that the Bible's own illustration about calculating a tower's cost applies so directly to something as modern as student debt."
📌 Ministry connection: Very relevant when discussing financial pressures and debt with people in the territory.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah values careful planning over presumption — he wants us to "sit down" and think things through, not rush ahead.
14 The financial cost. Some courses are inexpensive or may be funded by the government. In such cases, getting additional education may be an affordable way to gain knowledge and skills that will help you to obtain employment. But in other cases, additional education is expensive. There may also be indirect costs, such as private schooling or tutoring to help you qualify for enrollment. Some courses leave students with many years of debt. Adilson, from Mozambique, regrets his choice in education. He says: "My family sacrificed food and other basic needs to pay for my four-year course." As you analyze a course or training program, ask yourself: 'How much will it cost? Can my family or I afford it? Is there a less expensive option?' (Read Luke 14:28.) 'If I get into debt, how long will it take to pay it off? Will my future employment pay enough to sustain me and pay off the loan?' — Prov. 22:7
15. Why should you consider the work prospects before deciding on a particular type of education?

Will the education actually help you find a job in the local employment market? Some courses provide little practical training and are mostly theoretical, academic, or philosophical Col. 2:8. A sister from India found her course didn't develop the practical skills employers required. Sublime's course (repairing air conditioners) had little demand where he lives.

💬 Comment you could share: "What struck me was how practical this is — checking real job listings before enrolling, not just assuming a diploma equals a job."
📌 Ministry connection: Good angle when talking with young adults weighing "passion" versus "practicality" in career choices.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah wants our thinking grounded in reality and truth, not "empty deception" — that's true whether we're studying the Bible or a career field.
15 The work prospects. Consider the employment market where you live or where you want to live. Will the education actually help you to find a job? Some courses provide little practical training. Instead, they are mostly theoretical or they focus on intellectual, academic, or philosophical subjects. Col. 2:8 A sister from India says: "My course did not help me to develop the practical skills that employers require. As a result, I was not able to find work in the field I had studied." Other education may be in a field with few job opportunities. Sublime, from the Central African Republic, completed a course on repairing air conditioners. However, he says: "Where I live, most people repair things themselves, so it can be difficult to find work."
16. Why is it worthwhile to consider the type of work that the education may lead to?

Will you enjoy the work? Eccl. 3:12, 13. What's the environment like — safe, competitive, stressful? Will pay be enough, and will the work allow you to put Kingdom interests first? Eccl. 12:13. Tabitha's six-month sewing course gave her flexible, always-in-demand work that supported her pioneering.

💬 Comment you could share: "I found it interesting that Tabitha reasoned this all out in advance — flexible schedule, steady demand, low investment — and it worked out exactly as she planned."

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: "Fear the true God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole obligation of man" (Eccl. 12:13) — a good reminder of what should shape every career choice.
16 Contemplate also the type of work you are likely to find. Will you enjoy it? Eccl. 3:12, 13 What will the work environment be like? Is it an industry known for being unsafe, competitive, or stressful? How much money does that type of work typically pay? Will it be enough to support yourself? Will you need ongoing education to keep the job? Most important, will it allow you to put Kingdom interests first? Eccl. 12:13 Of course, when work is scarce, we cannot afford to be too choosy. But the choice you make regarding education can affect your employment opportunities in the future. Tabitha, from India, chose a six-month sewing course. She says: "I reasoned that working as a tailor would help me to pioneer. It is a job that would always be in demand, give me a flexible schedule, and not require a large investment." The course helped Tabitha find work to support her pioneering.

Also talk to others who are happily serving Jehovah Prov. 15:22 — they may suggest options you hadn't considered.
(b) See the box "Bible Principles to Consider," which lists six key questions with their supporting scriptures — motives, time, length, location, cost, and whether the work supports Kingdom goals.

How Would You Answer?

1. How does Galatians 6:5 apply to decisions regarding additional education?

Since "each one will carry his own load" Gal. 6:5, the decision to pursue additional education (or not) belongs personally to each adult Christian — not to elders or fellow believers to dictate.

💬 Personal comment: "This verse reminds me that I shouldn't judge someone else's choice about education — that load is theirs to carry, not mine to weigh in on."
2. How should Christians view further education?

As a means to support ourselves while we keep serving Jehovah wholeheartedly at the center of our life — not as an end in itself, and never at the expense of our relationship with him Matt. 6:33.

💬 Personal comment: "I appreciated how the article put it — education should revolve around our spiritual goals, not the other way around."
3. What factors should a Christian consider when deciding whether to obtain additional education?

Motives, schedule, length, location, financial cost, and work prospects — all weighed prayerfully and in light of Bible principles, using research from the institution, job listings, and trusted, spiritually mature people.
