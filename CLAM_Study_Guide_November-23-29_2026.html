⛏️ TREASURES FROM GOD’S WORD

Theme: Lamentations 3 was written by Jeremiah while looking at Jerusalem in ruins. Yet in the middle of the book’s darkest chapter he finds three reasons not to “give out”: Jehovah’s loyal love, Jehovah’s faithfulness, and a waiting attitude. The schedule closes the point with: “While we wait on Jehovah, we keep praying to him in faith.”

🖼️ Image application: Her circumstances have not changed — the IV is still in her arm — but her face is calm because she is talking to Jehovah. That is Lamentations 3:24 in action: a waiting attitude is not passive; it is filled with prayer. When I am in a waiting room of my own, I can choose to pray rather than worry.

1️⃣ Appreciate what Jehovah has done for you

“22It is because of Jehovah’s loyal love that we have not come to our finish, For his mercies never end.”
— La 3:22 (NWT Study Edition) 📖 La 3:22

Key point (w24.01 30 ¶16): We have not “come to our finish” only because of Jehovah’s loyal love. The article’s example of Rachel shows how that love often reaches us — friends who spend time with us, bring meals and send scriptures. 📄 w24.01 30 ¶16

💬 Comment you could share: I found it interesting that Jeremiah says we have not come to our finish “because of Jehovah’s loyal love.” The credit for my endurance goes to Jehovah, not to my own strength. When I look back at a hard time, I can usually name the brothers and sisters Jehovah used to keep me going.

2️⃣ Trust that Jehovah will continue to be with you

“23They are new each morning; your faithfulness is abundant.”
— La 3:23 (NWT Study Edition) 📖 La 3:23
“34So never be anxious about the next day, for the next day will have its own anxieties. Each day has enough of its own troubles.”
— Mt 6:34 (NWT Study Edition) 📖 Mt 6:34

Key point: Jehovah’s mercies are “new each morning” — like manna, there is a fresh supply for each day. That is why Jesus could say not to be anxious about tomorrow: tomorrow’s help will arrive with tomorrow. The Research Guide article “Throw All Your Anxiety on Jehovah” explains that Jesus was warning against undue anxiety, not saying we would never feel concern. 📄 w16.12 21-22

📌 Personal application: When I am tempted to carry next month’s problems today, I can ask myself: “Do I have enough of Jehovah’s help for today?” La 3:23 says yes — and tomorrow there will be a new supply.

3️⃣ Wait patiently for your trials to end

“24“Jehovah is my share,” I have said, “that is why I will show a waiting attitude for him.””
— La 3:24 (NWT Study Edition) 📖 La 3:24

Key point (w21.08 13 ¶17-19): Jehovah himself is “waiting patiently to show you favor” (Isa. 30:18). While we wait, each bad habit we conquer and each godly quality we develop is Jehovah preparing us for life under his Kingdom. “The best is yet to come!” 📄 w21.08 13 ¶17-19 📄 Research Guide: w11 9/15 8-10

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jeremiah, a Levite, called Jehovah “my share.” Levites received no land — Jehovah himself was their inheritance. That tells me Jehovah is willing to be someone’s whole portion, and he never runs out. When I lose something material, I have not lost my share.
🔔 While we wait on Jehovah, we keep praying to him in faith.
“27Good it is for a man to carry the yoke during his youth.”
— La 3:27 (NWT Study Edition) 📖 La 3:27

Answer: Facing tests of faith while young may mean hardship and ridicule, but it is “good” to carry the yoke in youth because learning to bear a yoke of suffering while young prepares a person to deal with challenges in later years. 📄 w07 6/1 11 ¶5

Research Guide enrichment (w97 5/1 32): Young ones who face up to tests of faith squarely and refuse to compromise will, in the long run, have fewer anxieties. Jesus’ yoke is kindly and his load light. 📄 w97 5/1 32

💬 Comment you could share: I found it encouraging that Jeremiah calls the yoke in youth “good.” The courage a young one builds by saying no at school isn’t wasted — it is training for every test that comes later. Our CBS this week even pictures a teenage sister politely refusing a rainbow bracelet; that’s exactly the kind of yoke this verse means.
📌 Personal application: If I am older, I can tell a young one in the congregation how a test I faced as a youth helped me later — that makes this verse very real for them.

Answer: They must take care of themselves first so that they are in a good place spiritually and physically: take walks for strength, keep a regular bedtime and set aside electronic devices, keep up personal study (even late at night when the house is calm), ask a relative to step in so they can enjoy something restful, confide in a close friend, and be humble enough to say, “I’m tired; I need help.” The article adds: be modest and set boundaries, be discerning, keep a sense of humor, and picture life in Paradise.
