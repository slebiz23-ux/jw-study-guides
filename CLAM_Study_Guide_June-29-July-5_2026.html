1 "How to 'Run a Race Against Horses'" 10 min · Talk
⌄
Treasures Talk

Theme: Like Jeremiah, we will face increasing opposition — but Jehovah prepares us now for greater challenges ahead. Cultivating brotherly love today is our best preparation for the great tribulation.

📌 Point 1 — Expect Opposition as Jeremiah Did

Jeremiah faced a death plot from the men of his own hometown Anathoth. Jehovah revealed it to him, helping him understand that opposition was part of his assignment.

📖 Jer 11:21

📰 Watchtower Reference: w04 11/1 p. 16 ¶12

Jehovah revealed the plot against Jeremiah so that his prophet could maintain courage and continue serving faithfully despite threats from his own community. We too can trust that Jehovah sees the challenges we face and strengthens us.

📌 Point 2 — Harder Trials Are Ahead: "Run Against Horses"

Jehovah challenged Jeremiah with a thought-provoking question: if he struggled among footmen (ordinary adversaries), how would he cope running against horses (much greater opposition during Jerusalem's fall)? The answer: by relying on Jehovah now.

📖 Jer 12:5

📰 Watchtower Reference: w11 3/15 p. 32 ¶19

The metaphor of running against horses illustrates the escalating nature of trials for God's people. What we face today — while challenging — is preparing us for the far greater test of the great tribulation. Jehovah's question to Jeremiah is also a loving challenge to us: Are we building the spiritual stamina we will need?

🔗 Open w11 in WOL

📌 Point 3 — Build Brotherly Love Now for the Great Tribulation

Isaiah's prophecy assures God's people that Jehovah provides a place of protection during his wrath. Cultivating brotherly love within the congregation now is essential preparation.

📖 Isa 26:20

📰 Watchtower Reference: w23.07 pp. 6-7 ¶15-16

Brotherly love is not merely a nice quality — it is one we need to cultivate deliberately, especially as the great tribulation approaches. Our bonds of love within the congregation will be a source of strength and unity when conditions become most difficult. This Watchtower article encourages us to deepen these bonds now, in times of relative peace.

3 Bible Reading: Jer 12:1-11 4 min · th Study 2
⌄
Bible Reading

Teaching Quality — th Study 2: Effective Pausing — Read at a natural pace, using pauses to let the meaning of each thought land before moving on. Don't rush through poetic language.

📖 Passage Overview: Jer 12:1-11

Jeremiah honestly questions why wicked people prosper (vv. 1-4), then receives Jehovah's challenge to be strong for harder trials ahead (v. 5). Jehovah then describes his own anguish over unfaithful Israel, comparing the land to a desolate, mourning wilderness (vv. 7-11).

Key verse for expression: Verse 5 — "If you get tired running with footmen, how can you run a race against horses?" Read this as a genuine, thought-provoking question — not harsh, but motivating.

Mood shift: Verses 7-11 are deeply sorrowful — Jehovah expresses grief over abandoning his "beloved" people to their enemies because of their wickedness. Slow down and let the emotion show in your voice.

💬 Comment You Could Share After Bible Reading

"Verse 7 really moved me — Jehovah says 'I have abandoned my house, I have forsaken my inheritance.' Discipline from Jehovah is never casual. He deeply loves those he must correct, and that love never disappears."

4 Starting a Conversation 3 min · Informal Witnessing · lmd Lesson 1, Pt. 4
⌄
Informal Witnessing

Assignment: Initiate a conversation after a person says or does something kind.

lmd Lesson 1, Point 4 — Use what the person says or does as a natural bridge into spiritual conversation. When someone extends kindness, it opens a door. Express genuine appreciation, then connect their goodness to a larger truth about human nature or Jehovah.

🎭 Sample Dialogue Script — Informal Witnessing

Publisher:
"Excuse me — I noticed you helped that person with their bags just now. That was really kind of you!" ← lmd L1 pt.4: Start from something the person actually did — it's genuine and flattering without being hollow.
Person:
"Oh, it's nothing, just seemed like the right thing to do."
Publisher:
"You know, I think most people want to be kind — I've always believed kindness is actually built into human nature. Do you feel that way?" ← Bridge to a broader topic naturally; you're building rapport and curiosity, not launching into a presentation.
Person:
"Yeah, I think people are basically good. The world just gets in the way sometimes."
Publisher:
"That's an interesting way to put it. I actually read something recently that ties into that — the Bible says kindness is a fruit of God's spirit. It suggests that when we're kind, we're actually reflecting something divine. Have you ever thought about that?" ← lmd L1 pt.4: The spiritual bridge is natural because it flows from their own words. Keep it conversational — not preachy.
Person:
"Hmm, I've never thought of it that way."
Publisher:
"There's actually a website — jw.org — that has a lot of practical articles on topics like that. You might enjoy some of them. It was nice meeting you!" ← Leave a seed without pressure. A brief, warm close is ideal for informal witnessing.

📌 Tips for This Assignment

The key is genuine observation — notice what the person actually did and comment on it sincerely before transitioning. Your tone should be warm and curious, never rehearsed. Practice the bridge line so it sounds natural.

📖 LESSON 98 — Christianity Spreads to Many Nations

📜 Bible account: Acts 13:1–23:35

Paul and Barnabas preach to the governor of Cyprus, Sergius Paulus
Paul and Barnabas preached to people of all backgrounds and positions — even governors. A sorcerer tried to stop them, but Jehovah's power was evident when the sorcerer was struck blind, causing Governor Paulus to believe. This account reminds us that no opposition can stop Jehovah's purpose, and that people in influential positions also need the Kingdom message.
Paul preaches in a marketplace
Paul preached everywhere — from house to house, in markets, on the road, and in synagogues. He didn't wait for a perfect venue or receptive audience. This illustrates the importance of being a "public" preacher and using every opportunity and setting to share the good news.

📰 Narrative Summary

Starting in 47 C.E., Paul and Barnabas launched the first of Paul's three missionary journeys, traveling through Asia Minor. They preached everywhere — rich and poor, young and old. Despite a stoning that left Paul for dead, he recovered and continued. By 49 C.E., Paul pushed even further into Europe — Athens, Ephesus, Philippi, Thessalonica — with Timothy, Silas, and Luke joining him. For 1.5 years he strengthened the congregation at Corinth. A third journey in 52 C.E. followed, including years in Ephesus where many people "changed their ways."

Key verse: "Go, therefore, and make disciples of people of all the nations." — Matthew 28:19

❓ CBS Questions & Answers

Q: Using the map in your Bible (Appendix B13), can you follow Paul's preaching tours? What stands out to you about his journeys? ▶

Paul's three tours covered an enormous geographical area — from Antioch in Syria westward through Asia Minor (modern Turkey), into Greece (Philippi, Thessalonica, Athens, Corinth), and eventually to Rome. What stands out is the sheer determination involved: these journeys were undertaken on foot and by sea, often in hostile conditions. Paul was shipwrecked, stoned, imprisoned, and beaten — yet he kept going. This illustrates the depth of his conviction that the good news was worth sharing at any personal cost.

💬 Comment You Could Share

"Looking at the map in Appendix B13, I'm struck by how far Paul traveled — often in very difficult conditions. His willingness to preach 'from house to house, in markets, on the road, and in synagogues' (Lesson 98) encourages me to look for opportunities wherever I am, not just in formal ministry."

What happened when Paul and Barnabas healed a lame man in Lystra, and how did they respond? ▶

When they healed a lame man, the crowd thought Paul and Barnabas were gods and tried to worship them. Paul and Barnabas immediately and firmly rejected this, saying: "Worship only God! We are just men." Despite then being stoned and left for dead, Paul recovered and continued his work. This response demonstrates deep humility — refusing glory that belonged only to Jehovah — and extraordinary resilience in the face of violent opposition.

🎯 Wrap-up Questions

1. What does Lesson 98 teach us about Jehovah? ▶

Lesson 98 reveals that Jehovah's purpose cannot be stopped by human opposition. When a sorcerer tried to prevent Paul from preaching to a governor, Jehovah intervened directly. When Paul was stoned and left for dead, Jehovah preserved him. When authorities imprisoned his servants, Jehovah continued advancing the preaching work. This shows that Jehovah is actively involved in supporting his people's ministry. He is not a passive observer — he backs his servants with real power.

💬 Comment

"This lesson shows me that Jehovah sees and supports each witness that goes out in his name. Just as he preserved Paul through stoning and imprisonment, he watches over us in our ministry too."

2. What from Lesson 98 can we use in the ministry or daily life? ▶

Paul preached "from house to house, in markets, on the road, and in synagogues" — he used every setting and opportunity. In daily life, this encourages us to look for natural opportunities to share our faith rather than limiting it to formal ministry. Also, Paul's quick recovery after being stoned and returning immediately to the city shows us not to let setbacks end our service. When things don't go well in the ministry, we can follow Paul's example and simply continue.

📖 LESSON 99 — A Jailer Learns the Truth

📜 Bible account: Acts 16:16-34

The jailer finds the prison doors open — yet no one has fled
The jailer was about to kill himself, convinced all prisoners had escaped — which would have brought him severe punishment. Paul's immediate cry — "Don't hurt yourself! We are all here!" — was an act of selfless compassion. Paul's concern for the jailer's physical life opened the door for the jailer to receive spiritual life. This shows how genuine care for people is one of our most powerful witnessing tools.

📰 Narrative Summary

In Philippi, a slave girl with a demon followed Paul and Silas for days, shouting announcements about them. Paul commanded the demon out of her in Jesus' name. Her owners, furious at losing their source of income, dragged Paul and Silas before the magistrates. They were beaten and thrown into the innermost part of the prison with their feet in stocks.

At midnight, Paul and Silas were praying and singing praises to Jehovah — their fellow prisoners listening. A powerful earthquake opened every door and freed every chain. The jailer rushed in, ready to kill himself. Paul stopped him. The jailer, shaken, asked: "What must I do to get saved?" That night, he and his entire household were taught the truth and baptized.

Key verse: "People will lay their hands on you and persecute you... It will result in your giving a witness." — Luke 21:12, 13

❓ CBS Questions & Answers

Q1: Why were Paul and Silas thrown into prison? ▶

A demon-possessed slave girl was being used as a fortune-teller by her owners for profit. When Paul expelled the demon in Jesus' name, her owners lost their source of income. In anger, they fabricated charges against Paul and Silas — accusing them of "breaking the law and upsetting the whole city." The magistrates, responding to the crowd, had them beaten and imprisoned without a proper trial. This was a clear case of financial self-interest masquerading as concern for law and order — a pattern that has repeated itself throughout Christian history.

Q2: How did the jailer learn the truth? ▶

The jailer's conversion followed a dramatic sequence: (1) He heard Paul and Silas singing praises to Jehovah in the darkness of prison — an unexplainable response to suffering. (2) The earthquake opened every door and freed every chain. (3) Paul's selfless act of preventing the prisoners from escaping showed extraordinary character — putting the jailer's welfare above their own freedom. (4) The jailer, "trembling," fell before Paul and Silas and asked sincerely: "What must I do to get saved?" (5) Paul and Silas spent the rest of the night teaching him and his entire household, who were all baptized before dawn. The jailer's heart was opened by what he witnessed — faith expressed in song, miraculous confirmation, and genuine compassion.

💬 Comment You Could Share

"What opened the jailer's heart wasn't a carefully crafted argument — it was Paul's compassion in a moment of crisis. He could have escaped, but he stayed. That act of love made the jailer ready to listen. It reminds me that how we treat people often matters more than what we say."

🎯 Wrap-up Questions

1. What does Lesson 99 teach us about Jehovah? ▶

Lesson 99 reveals that Jehovah can use even unjust persecution to advance his purpose. Paul and Silas were thrown into prison for doing good — and Jehovah turned that imprisonment into an opportunity that reached an entire household. The earthquake was not simply a dramatic rescue — it became the context for a beautiful witnessing opportunity. Jehovah is a God who makes "all things work together for good" for those who love him (Romans 8:28), including our trials and setbacks.

💬 Comment

"Jehovah turned Paul and Silas's unjust imprisonment into a witnessing opportunity that reached a whole household. It reassures me that even when things go wrong — when opposition or setbacks come — Jehovah can work through those circumstances in ways we don't expect."

2. What from Lesson 99 can we use in the ministry or daily life? ▶

Several practical lessons: (1) Paul and Silas's singing in prison shows the power of maintaining a positive, faith-filled spirit even in suffering — it was itself a witness. (2) Paul's compassionate act of preventing the jailer from self-harm prioritized the jailer's welfare above personal freedom — a powerful model of genuine care for people. (3) The jailer's household being baptized that same night shows how one person's conversion can affect an entire family — family witnessing is worth pursuing. (4) Luke 21:12,13 reminds us that persecution "will result in your giving a witness" — our faithfulness under pressure is itself a testimony.
