Question 1 • Paragraph 1
What surprising statement did Jesus make about being hated?

Suggested Comment

Jesus' Sermon on the Mount contained a statement that must have surprised his listeners: "Happy are you whenever men hate you." Most people associate happiness with being liked and accepted, so this was counterintuitive. Jesus said this because his followers would face exactly this kind of hatred from the world. The reason this statement is worth examining is that it helps us understand both why we are hated and why we can still be happy despite it. It sets the foundation for everything the article covers.

Key scriptures: Luke 6:22

🎯 How Would You Answer?

Review Question 1
Why are Jesus' followers hated?

Summary Answer

Jesus' followers are hated for three main reasons: (1) they worship Jehovah—and those blinded by Satan oppose anyone who does (John 16:2, 3); (2) they respect Jesus and acknowledge his kingship, which those loyal to earthly rulers reject (Mark 13:13); and (3) they are "no part of the world"—they reject its ungodly conduct, political allegiances, and moral standards, which sets them apart and draws ridicule (John 15:18-20). Ultimately, Satan is behind all opposition to Jehovah's people.
💬Personal comment

"The three reasons all come back to one thing: our allegiance is to Jehovah, not to the world. That clarity of purpose is what makes opposition predictable and manageable."

Review Question 2
How is our love stronger than hatred?

Summary Answer

Our love is stronger than hatred in several ways: It moves us to pray for our opposers rather than retaliate (following Jesus and Stephen's example). It empowers us to endure trials without abandoning our faith (as Paul demonstrated with his "spirit of power and love" — 2 Tim. 1:7). It motivates us to support each other under pressure, like the brothers and sisters who came to the courthouse in Russia. And it allows us to find genuine happiness—not despite the hatred, but because love fills the spaces hatred tries to destroy.
💬Personal comment

"Hatred is reactive and self-focused; love is proactive and other-focused. That's why it wins—it operates on a completely different level."

Review Question 3
Why can we be happy when hated?

Summary Answer

We can be happy when hated for at least four reasons: (1) It fulfills Bible prophecy, confirming we serve the true God and that His word is reliable. (2) Jehovah's spirit rests on us when we endure—persecution itself becomes evidence of God's approval (1 Pet. 4:14). (3) We have love stronger than hatred—a quality that sustains us through the deepest trials. (4) We have a future to look forward to—a time when we will be loved, not hated, for serving Jehovah, when all opposition will end. (2 Thess. 1:6-8)
