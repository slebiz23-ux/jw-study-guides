Focus

How meditating on the book of Isaiah can lift our spirits when we feel sad or downhearted.

"This is what Jehovah says: . . . 'As a mother comforts her son, so I will keep comforting you.'" — Isaiah 66:12, 13
Isa. 66:13

w26 July pp. 8-13 — "Find Comfort in the Book of Isaiah" · Studied the week of September 14-20, 2026.

Introduction
1-2. In what way does Jehovah comfort us, and how do we know that he understands what we are feeling?

A loving mother has a special way of comforting her child — with tender words and a warm embrace. Jehovah uses this same picture to describe how he comforts us: "As a mother comforts her son, so I will keep comforting you" (Isa. 66:13). He promises he will never forget us, even if others do (Isa. 49:15). One of the ways he provides comfort today is through his Word, including the book of Isaiah (Rom. 15:4).

💬 Comment you could share: I find it touching that Jehovah didn't just say he comforts us — he chose the tenderest human relationship we know, a mother comforting her child, to describe how he feels toward us.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah's promise that he will never forget us, even if a mother could forget her own nursing child, shows a level of attentive care that goes beyond even our closest human relationships.
1-2 A loving mother has a special way of comforting her child — with soft words, a warm embrace, and the reassurance that everything will be all right. Jehovah uses this very picture to describe how he comforts his people: "As a mother comforts her son, so I will keep comforting you." Even when it seems no one truly understands what we are going through, Jehovah does, and he cares. He promises: "Can a woman forget her nursing child or have no compassion for the son of her womb? Even if these women forget, I would never forget you." One of the ways Jehovah comforts us today is through his Word, including the prophecy of Isaiah, for "all the things that were written beforehand were written for our instruction, so that through our endurance and through the comfort from the Scriptures we might have hope."
3. What will we consider in this article?

Isaiah's prophecy opens with the words "Comfort, comfort my people" (Isa. 40:1) — comfort we need today, living as we are in "critical times hard to deal with" (2 Tim. 3:1). The article considers comfort Isaiah offers in three situations: when our circumstances change unexpectedly, when we feel plagued by excessive guilt, and when we are grieving the death of a loved one.

💬 Comment you could share: It struck me that Isaiah's very first words are about comfort — as if Jehovah wanted that to be the lens through which the rest of the book is read.

When Circumstances Change Unexpectedly
4. Why should it not surprise us when our circumstances change unexpectedly?

Life rarely unfolds exactly as we plan — "time and unexpected events" can overtake any of us (Eccl. 9:11). A brother named Joseph, a former Bethelite whose assignment changed unexpectedly, learned this firsthand. Whatever the change, none of us are exempt from living in a world whose "scene... is changing" (1 Cor. 7:31).

💬 Comment you could share: Ecclesiastes 9:11 was written thousands of years ago, yet it describes exactly what unsettles us today — the sense that outcomes aren't fully in our control.
4 Life rarely unfolds exactly as we plan. "Time and unexpected events" can overtake any of us. A brother named Joseph, a former Bethelite, experienced this when his assignment changed unexpectedly. Whatever unexpected change we face, we know that "the world is passing away" and none of us are exempt from its uncertainties.
5. (Read Isaiah 42:16.) What has Jehovah promised to do for us when we face unfamiliar circumstances?

Read Isaiah 42:16 — Jehovah promises to lead us on unfamiliar paths and never to abandon us. His word acts as "a lamp to my foot, and a light for my path" (Ps. 119:105), and he assures us of guidance at every decision: "This is the way. Walk in it" (Isa. 30:21). He tells us, "Do not be afraid, for I am with you" (Isa. 41:10), and invites us to throw all our anxiety on him "because he cares for [us]" (1 Pet. 5:6, 7).

💬 Comment you could share: What stands out to me in Isaiah 42:16 is the phrase "unfamiliar paths" — Jehovah doesn't promise to remove the unfamiliarity, only to walk it with us.
📌 Ministry connection: Someone going through an unexpected upheaval — a job loss, a move, a health scare — may find real comfort in the assurance that Jehovah is walking an unfamiliar path with them, not just watching from a distance.
5 Read Isaiah 42:16. Even when we feel that we are walking an "unfamiliar path," Jehovah promises to guide us, just as his word is "a lamp to my foot, and a light for my path." We can also trust that when we face a decision, Jehovah will guide us: "This is the way. Walk in it." Jehovah also assures us: "Do not be afraid, for I am with you." We can throw all our anxiety on him, "because he cares for [us]."
6. What further comfort do Isaiah 54:10 and 58:11 provide, and how did Joseph benefit from them?

Jehovah's "loyal love will not be removed" from us, and his "covenant of peace" will not be shaken (Isa. 54:10). He also promises to lead and satisfy us "even in a parched land" (Isa. 58:11). Joseph found this true in his own experience, which strengthened his resolve — a sentiment echoed in the original song "I Know Jehovah's With Me."

💬 Comment you could share: The image of a "well-watered garden... a spring whose waters never fail" in Isaiah 58:11 is a striking promise for anyone feeling depleted by an unwanted change.

When Plagued With Excessive Guilt
7. What steps can help us if we struggle with feelings of guilt?

Making mistakes is part of being human. "Whoever confesses and abandons [his sins] will be shown mercy" (Prov. 28:13). When needed, we can call the elders to pray over us (Jas. 5:14-16), confident that Jehovah "is faithful and righteous so as to forgive us our sins" (1 John 1:9). Fellow believers can help too, by extending forgiveness and comfort so we are "not... overwhelmed by excessive sadness" (2 Cor. 2:7). Left unaddressed, guilt affects us deeply, as the psalmist described (Ps. 38:3, 8).

💬 Comment you could share: Psalm 38's description of unaddressed guilt — "no peace within my bones" — is a vivid reminder that guilt isn't just an emotion; it can affect us physically too.
📌 Ministry connection: Many people carry guilt from their past and assume forgiveness has to be earned back slowly; James 5:14-16 and 1 John 1:9 show a much more direct, hope-filled path.
7 Making mistakes is part of being human. The Bible promises that "whoever confesses and abandons [his sins] will be shown mercy." When needed, we can call the elders to pray over us; "the prayer of faith will make the sick one well," and confessed sins "will be forgiven." Jehovah "is faithful and righteous so as to forgive us our sins." Fellow believers, too, can help by extending forgiveness and comfort so that we are "not... overwhelmed by excessive sadness." Left unaddressed, guilt can affect us deeply — as the psalmist wrote, "my whole body is sick because of your indignation," and "my anguished heart makes me groan aloud."
8-9. (Read Isaiah 1:18.) How does the account of King David illustrate Jehovah's willingness to forgive?

Read Isaiah 1:18 — After committing adultery with Bath-sheba (2 Sam. 11:3, 4), David arranged to have her husband, Uriah, killed in battle (2 Sam. 11:14, 15). Yet when David humbly confessed, Jehovah forgave him: "Jehovah, in turn, forgives your sin. You will not die" (2 Sam. 12:13). Jehovah went on to promise David an everlasting kingdom line, conditioned on faithfulness (1 Ki. 9:4, 5).

💬 Comment you could share: What strikes me about David's account is how immediate Nathan's declaration of forgiveness was, the moment David genuinely confessed — no lengthy probation, just mercy.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Despite the severity of David's sins, Jehovah still went on to build an everlasting promise around David's line — proof that forgiven sin doesn't permanently define how Jehovah views a repentant person.
8-9 Read Isaiah 1:18. Consider King David. After committing adultery with Bath-sheba, David then arranged to have her husband, Uriah, killed in battle. 10. (Read Isaiah 38:17 and footnote.) What assurance does this give us about forgiven sin?

Read Isaiah 38:17 and footnote — Jehovah does not hold our confessed, repented-of sins against us. He is "the One who is blotting out your transgressions for my own sake," and says, "I will not remember your sins" (Isa. 43:25).

💬 Comment you could share: "Thrown all my sins behind your back" is such a vivid picture — not filed away where they could resurface, but put completely out of reach.
10 Read Isaiah 38:17 and footnote. Jehovah does not hold our confessed, repented-of sins against us. As he told Hezekiah's nation through Isaiah, he is "the One who is blotting out your transgressions for my own sake," and he says, "I will not remember your sins."
11. What further comfort do Isaiah 57:16 and 55:7 give to those who feel unworthy of forgiveness?

Jehovah assures repentant ones that he will not "always remain indignant" (Isa. 57:16). He invites the wicked one to "return to Jehovah, who will have mercy on him... for he will forgive in a large way" (Isa. 55:7).

💬 Comment you could share: The phrase "forgive in a large way" at Isaiah 55:7 pushes back against the idea that Jehovah's forgiveness is grudging or partial — it's generous.
11 Jehovah assures repentant ones: "I will not... always remain indignant." He invites the wicked one to "return to Jehovah, who will have mercy on him... for he will forgive in a large way."
When Grieving the Loss of a Loved One
12. What painful loss did Michael experience?

A brother named Michael lost his parents in a fire — a devastating loss that left him grieving deeply.

12 A brother named Michael lost his parents in a fire — a devastating loss that left him grieving deeply.
13. (Read Isaiah 25:8.) What hope does this hold out to those who grieve, and how is it echoed in Revelation?

Read Isaiah 25:8 — Isaiah also foretold that those "redeemed by Jehovah" will return with a joyful cry and "grief and sighing will flee away" (Isa. 35:10). This hope is echoed in Revelation, where "death will be no more, neither will mourning nor outcry nor pain be anymore" (Rev. 21:4, 5).

💬 Comment you could share: It's meaningful that this same hope — an end to death and mourning — appears at both the beginning and the very end of the Hebrew and Christian Greek Scriptures, in Isaiah and in Revelation.
📌 Ministry connection: Isaiah 25:8 is a gentle, direct scripture to share with someone grieving who may not yet know the Bible teaches a literal resurrection hope.
13 Read Isaiah 25:8. Isaiah also foretold: "Those redeemed by Jehovah will return and come to Zion with a joyful cry... And grief and sighing will flee away." This hope is echoed in Revelation: "death will be no more, neither will mourning nor outcry nor pain be anymore... 'I am making all things new.'"
14. (Read Isaiah 26:19.) What does this promise, and how is it illustrated by an account in the Gospels?

Read Isaiah 26:19 — Compare this with the account of the young girl Jesus resurrected, who "rose and began walking," to the amazement of all present (Mark 5:42).

💬 Comment you could share: Jesus' resurrection of Jairus' daughter gives Isaiah 26:19 a concrete, historical preview — it's not just poetic language, it's something Jesus actually demonstrated.

15-16. How can meditating on Isaiah's words help us cope with grief, and what conclusion can we draw?

Reflecting on Isaiah 26:19, Michael found deep comfort in the certainty of the resurrection hope — that the promise is not vague but personal and sure. Whatever trial we face, Jehovah's comfort can soothe our souls when anxieties overwhelm us (Ps. 94:19).
