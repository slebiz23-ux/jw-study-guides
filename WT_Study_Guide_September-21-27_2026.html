What Does It Mean to Come to Know Jehovah?
4. What is involved in coming to know Jehovah?

Jehovah promised the Jews returning from exile: “I will give them a heart to know me, that I am Jehovah” (Jer. 24:7). They already knew basic facts about him, but coming to know him would involve their heart — their feelings and motives. As one reference work puts it, there is a difference between knowing about God and knowing God, like the difference between reading about love and falling in love. We want to know more than Jehovah’s name and that he is God; we want to know the Person behind the name.

5. What can we learn from King Josiah regarding what it means to come to know Jehovah?

Coming to know Jehovah includes learning what makes him happy and what displeases him. While still a boy, Josiah “started to search for the God of David his forefather” (2 Chron. 34:3). But he did more than study — he acted on what he learned. Jehovah said that Josiah “upheld justice and righteousness” (Jer. 22:15, 16) and “defended the legal claim of the afflicted one and the poor one” (Jer. 22:15, 16), and then asked: “‘Is that not what it means to know me?’” (Jer. 22:15, 16)

Why Continue to Get to Know Jehovah?
7. After we have been serving Jehovah for many years, what could happen?

Over time, we may not put as much effort into getting to know Jehovah as when we first learned about him. When reading the Bible, we might focus mainly on practical benefits for ourselves or on how to use it to help others. The Scriptures encourage that — they are “beneficial for teaching, for reproving, for setting things straight, for disciplining in righteousness” (2 Tim. 3:16, 17). But the Bible also reveals much about its Author (“Good and upright is Jehovah” (Ps. 25:8-10)). We should never be so absorbed in the benefits that we fail to ask, ‘What does this tell me about Jehovah God?’

8. What impact will getting to know Jehovah better have on our friendship with him? (Ps. 73:24-28)

As we get to know Jehovah better, our friendship with him grows stronger. After daily Bible reading, a monthly broadcast, or an assembly or convention, we often feel closer to our heavenly Father because we have learned more about him — and we are moved to praise and thank him. Like the psalmist, we can say: “God is the rock of my heart and my portion forever” (Ps. 73:24-28) and “drawing near to God is good for me” (Ps. 73:24-28). Every effort we make to know Jehovah deepens our gratitude for our friendship with him.

10. As we learn about Jehovah, what will we be able to do?

We are made in God’s image (“Let us make man in our image, according to our likeness” (Gen. 1:26)), so we can reflect his qualities. The more we learn about those qualities, the better we understand how to imitate him. Jesus condemned the scribes and Pharisees because they “disregarded the weightier matters of the Law, namely, justice and mercy and faithfulness” (Matt. 23:23). Unlike them, we want to understand how Jehovah’s qualities are reflected in his laws and principles, which helps us display those very qualities — especially love, since “God is love” (1 John 4:8, 11).

12-13. What can we learn about Jehovah from reading the Bible? Give an example. (Jer. 39:15-18) (See also picture.)

Reading the Bible: Sometimes the Bible directly identifies Jehovah’s qualities, as when he declared himself “a God merciful and compassionate, slow to anger and abundant in loyal love and truth” (Ex. 34:6, 7). More often, it relates events involving people and their decisions. When we read such accounts, we should think about what they teach us about Jehovah.

Example — Ebed-melech: In Jer. 38:6-13, the Ethiopian Ebed-melech rescued Jeremiah from a muddy cistern. We easily see his self-sacrifice, courage, and love for fellow worshippers. But Jeremiah 39:15-18 also reveals Jehovah’s qualities. He is appreciative — he sent a personal message to Ebed-melech: “‘But I will rescue you in that day,’ declares Jehovah” (Jer. 39:15-18). He is fair — he rewarded Ebed-melech “because you trusted in me” (Jer. 39:15-18). And he is impartial — this foreigner and eunuch received the same loving attention as anyone in Israel. Whenever we read a passage, we can identify Jehovah’s qualities and then thank and praise him for them.

We Will Never Stop Learning
17-18. Why can we say that we will never stop learning about Jehovah?

In the new world (¶17): We will learn much more about Jehovah, for new scrolls will reveal his requirements — John saw that “scrolls were opened” (Rev. 20:12). We will be able to interact more closely with the natural world, when “the earth will certainly be filled with the knowledge of Jehovah As the waters cover the sea” (Isa. 11:6-9). We will also understand our Father better as we see our loved ones resurrected and grow closer to perfection every day.

Forever (¶18): Jehovah has lived for an eternity — “From everlasting to everlasting, you are God” (Ps. 90:2) — and “His understanding is beyond measure” (Ps. 147:5). Mankind “will never find out the work that the true God has made from start to finish” (Eccl. 3:11). So regardless of how long we have been in the truth, we should seize every opportunity to learn more about our loving Father.

• What does it mean to come to know Jehovah?

It means more than knowing facts about him — it involves our heart. We come to know the Person behind the name: what makes him happy and what displeases him. Like Josiah, we act on what we learn (“‘Is that not what it means to know me?’” (Jer. 22:15, 16)), and as we know him better, our trust in him grows (“Those knowing your name will trust in you” (Ps. 9:10)).
