An Appeal on the Basis of Love

FOCUS: What we learn about promoting peace in the congregation from the apostle Paul, Philemon, and Onesimus.
“I would rather appeal to you on the basis of love, seeing that I am Paul an older man, yes, now also a prisoner for the sake of Christ Jesus.” — Philem. 9
The Appeal¶1-3
1. What responsibility do we all have?
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

We each have a personal responsibility to maintain and protect the peace we enjoy among Jehovah’s people. Jehovah grants this peace, but it does not stay without our effort.

“If possible, as far as it depends on you, be peaceable with all men.” — Rom. 12:18

💬 Comment you could share: What struck me is that peace is both a gift and a responsibility — Jehovah provides it, but I help protect it by how I speak and act with each person in the congregation.

📌 Ministry connection: When people we study with ask why our congregations feel so peaceful, we can explain that it is the fruitage of living by Bible principles, and that each of us works at it.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah is a God of peace who trusts us to share in preserving it.

1 WE CHERISH the peace we enjoy among Jehovah’s people. This peace gives us a taste of how good life will be in Paradise. (Isa. 32:17, 18) Although Jehovah grants us this peace, we have a personal responsibility to maintain and protect it. (Rom. 12:18) How can we promote peace in the congregation when interacting with others? When we offend someone? When someone offends us? The answers are found in an appeal made on the basis of love. This appeal involved three Bible characters: the apostle Paul, Philemon, and Onesimus.

Isa. 32:17, 18 Rom. 12:18
2. Summarize the account of Philemon and Onesimus. (Philemon 8, 9, 17)
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

Philemon was a respected brother in Colossae, known for faith and love. His unbelieving slave Onesimus fled to Rome, possibly after robbing him. There Onesimus befriended Paul and became a Christian. Roman law required him to return to his master, so Paul sent him back with a letter appealing to Philemon on the basis of love rather than giving an order.

“I would rather appeal to you on the basis of love, seeing that I am Paul an older man, yes, now also a prisoner for the sake of Christ Jesus.” — Philem. 9

💬 Comment you could share: I noticed that Paul had the authority to give an order, yet he chose to appeal. That shows how much weight he gave to love as the motivation for doing right.

📌 Ministry connection: Some we speak to in the ministry have broken relationships; this account shows that the Bible’s counsel on forgiveness has changed lives, even across the barrier of slave and master.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah draws people from every background and wants them to be reconciled as brothers.

2 Read Philemon 8, 9, 17. In his letter to Philemon, Paul exhorts him to welcome back his slave Onesimus. Paul appeals to Philemon’s love for Christ and for his fellow Christians. Are you familiar with the account? Philemon was a brother in the congregation in Colossae, known for his outstanding faith and love. He had an unbelieving slave named Onesimus, who abandoned him and fled to Rome, possibly robbing him before leaving. (Philem. 18; see study note.) While in Rome, Onesimus developed a close friendship with Paul and became a Christian. (Col. 4:9) Under Roman law, however, Onesimus had to return to his master, so Paul sent him back to Philemon.—Philem. 12, 13; see study note on “I am sending him back to you.”

3. What concerns may Paul have had about sending Onesimus back?
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

Paul may have wondered how Philemon would react, since he legally could have had Onesimus severely punished. He may also have wondered how the congregation would react, and whether masters and slaves in the congregation would become divided. Any of these could have disrupted the congregation’s peace, so Paul sent a letter to Philemon and another to the congregation, via Tychicus.

💬 Comment you could share: It stands out to me that Paul did not leave the outcome to chance; he prepared the ground with a loving letter. Thinking ahead can help us protect peace too.

📌 Ministry connection: Before we visit someone who may be upset, we can pray and think about how to approach them kindly.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah cares about the peace of the whole congregation, not only individuals.

3 How would Philemon react to Onesimus’ return? Legally, he had the right to have Onesimus severely punished. Would he exercise that right? How would the congregation react? What if other masters and slaves became divided over the matter? Paul knew that the situation could potentially disrupt the congregation’s peace. So he wrote a letter to Philemon and sent it via Tychicus. (Eph. 6:21, 22; Col. 4:7-9; see study note on Colossians 4:7.) At the same time, Paul also sent a letter to the congregation in Colossae. Let us examine what we can learn from the examples of Paul, Onesimus, and Philemon based on those two letters.

Eph. 6:21, 22 Col. 4:7-9
Lessons to Learn From Paul’s Example¶4-9
4-5. How can elders contribute to the peace of the congregation?
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

Elders contribute to peace by treating others as friends rather than ordering them around. Paul treated Philemon as a friend and fellow worker. Loving shepherds do not “lord it over” the flock; they spend time with the sheep, listen to them, value them, and support them when they need it.

“not lording it over those who are God’s inheritance, but becoming examples to the flock.” — 1 Pet. 5:3

💬 Comment you could share: I appreciate it when an elder simply sits and listens. That kind of friendship makes it easy to approach him, and that promotes peace.

📌 Ministry connection: Elders who are approachable help new ones feel at home, so those we bring to meetings feel welcome.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah is a loving Shepherd, and he wants shepherds who imitate his gentleness.

4 What can we learn from Paul’s example? Paul treated others in a way that promoted peace. His example shows how we can help to promote peace in the congregation when interacting with others.

5 Paul treated Philemon as a friend. (Philem. 17) Paul did not think he could just order Philemon around, as if he were his master. (2 Cor. 1:24) Paul viewed Philemon as a fellow worker and reminded him of their friendship. What a good example Paul set for Philemon regarding how to treat Onesimus, his new brother! It also serves as a fine example for all of us, especially for those who serve as elders in the congregation. Elders who treat others as friends are a force for peace. As loving shepherds, they do not ‘lord it over’ the flock. (1 Pet. 5:1-3) Instead, they spend time with God’s sheep, listen to them, value them, and support them in times of need. Like Paul, elders who treat others as friends set a fine example for the flock and contribute to an environment where peace can flourish.

Philem. 17 2 Cor. 1:24 1 Pet. 5:1-3
6. Why is commendation important?
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

Many of us struggle with insecurity, which can make us anxious, overly sensitive, defensive, or competitive, and those traits hinder peace. Commendation reassures a person that he is loved and appreciated. We should commend sincerely, for efforts as well as results — when we see something positive, we say something positive.

“Anxiety in a man’s heart weighs it down, But a good word cheers it up.” — Prov. 12:25

💬 Comment you could share: I want to make a goal this week to give one sincere, specific compliment after the meeting, especially for effort rather than results.

📌 Ministry connection: A genuine word of appreciation for a householder’s sincere interest or a student’s effort can encourage them to keep progressing.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah notices and values our efforts, even small ones.

6 Paul commended Philemon. (Philem. 4, 5, 7) We all need commendation. Why? For one thing, many of us struggle with feelings of insecurity. We may worry about our appearance, whether others like us, or whether we may lose a cherished privilege of service. These feelings are normal. However, they can sometimes make us anxious, overly sensitive, defensive, or even competitive—characteristics that can hinder our efforts to promote peace. A person can better cope with his feelings of insecurity if he receives commendation. (Prov. 12:25) It reassures him that he is loved and appreciated. Would you like to have that effect on others? If so, look for opportunities to commend others sincerely. Commend others not just for good results but also for the efforts they put forth. Simply put, when you see something positive, say something positive.

Philem. 4, 5 Philem. 7 Prov. 12:25
7. Why should we think carefully about what we say regarding others? (Philemon 10-12)
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

What we say about fellow worshippers affects how others feel about them and treat them. Paul’s tender words about Onesimus surely influenced Philemon. Speaking positively helps others treat our brothers and sisters kindly; speaking negatively may influence them to treat them unkindly. To speak well of others, we must first think well of them.

“I am appealing to you for my child, whose father I became while in prison, O·nesʹi·mus. He was formerly useless to you, but now he is useful to you and to me. I am sending him back to you, yes him, my very own heart.” — Philem. 10-12

💬 Comment you could share: It was thought-provoking to me that Paul called Onesimus “my very own heart.” Describing someone with warmth shapes how everyone else sees him.

📌 Ministry connection: We can speak well of the congregation to those we meet in the ministry; they are often influenced by what they hear about our brothers.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah speaks of his servants with affection, and we can reflect that.

7 Paul spoke positively about Onesimus. (Read Philemon 10-12.) How might Paul’s tender words about Onesimus have affected Philemon? Would they have influenced how Philemon felt about, spoke to, and treated Onesimus? Surely! Similarly, what we say about fellow worshippers affects how others feel about them and treat them. Speaking positively about our brothers and sisters helps others treat them kindly. Unfortunately, the opposite is also true. Speaking negatively about our brothers and sisters may influence others to treat them unkindly. But to speak well of others, we must first think well of them. What can help us to do that? Consider a custom that Paul had.

Philem. 10-12
8. What could help us to think and speak positively about fellow worshippers?
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

Paul made it his custom to thank Jehovah for the fine qualities of fellow Christians, including Philemon. He was not blind to their weaknesses, but he looked for their good qualities. If we cultivate the habit of thinking positively about others and praying sincerely for Jehovah to bless them, we will be more inclined to speak positively about them.

“I always thank my God when I mention you in my prayers, as I keep hearing of your faith and the love that you have for the Lord Jesus and for all the holy ones.” — Philem. 4, 5

💬 Comment you could share: One thing I can do is mention a specific brother or sister in my prayers by name and thank Jehovah for something I admire about them.

📌 Ministry connection: We can also pray about the people we study with, thanking Jehovah for every positive quality we see in them.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah delights in hearing our thanks for others and sees the good in them.

8 Paul regularly thanked Jehovah for Philemon’s fine qualities. (Philem. 4, 5) He did the same for many others. (1 Cor. 1:4-7; Phil. 1:3-5; Col. 1:3, 4; 1 Thess. 1:2, 3) Paul was not blind to the negative traits of his brothers and sisters, but he looked for their good qualities. And it was his custom to thank Jehovah for such friends. If you find it difficult to speak positively of others, remember this: If you cultivate the habit of thinking positively about others and of praying sincerely for Jehovah to bless them, you will be more inclined to speak positively about them.

Philem. 4, 5 1 Cor. 1:4-7 Phil. 1:3-5 Col. 1:3, 4 1 Thess. 1:2, 3
9. Why is Paul’s positive tone especially remarkable?
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

Paul had been violently attacked, unjustly detained in Jerusalem and Caesarea, and shipwrecked on the way to Rome. When he wrote to Philemon he was still a prisoner under house arrest. Yet his letter was kind and tactful. We can contribute to peace by treating others kindly even when we are tired, stressed, or enduring a trial; the peaceful atmosphere we promote helps us cope with our challenges.

💬 Comment you could share: What encouraged me is that Paul’s trials did not make him harsh. When I am tired, I can pause before I speak.

📌 Ministry connection: In the ministry we sometimes meet gruff people on bad days; staying kind, as Paul did, can soften hearts.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah gives strength that allows us to stay kind even under pressure.

9 Despite facing challenging circumstances, Paul treated Philemon kindly. Have you ever lost your patience with someone when you were tired or under stress? When we have been experiencing difficult circumstances, it can be easy to take our frustrations out on others. With that in mind, consider Paul’s circumstances around the time when he wrote to Philemon. Just a few years before, Paul was violently attacked and then unjustly detained. He was held in custody in Jerusalem and in Caesarea. Then, he was transferred to Rome but suffered shipwreck along the way. And now as he wrote to Philemon, Paul was still “a prisoner,” under house arrest in Rome. (Philem. 1) But did Paul allow his stressful experiences to affect the way he addressed Philemon? No. Paul’s letter to Philemon was kind and tactful. The lesson? We can contribute to peace by doing our best to treat others kindly even when we are tired, stressed, or enduring a trial. In turn, the peaceful atmosphere we promote helps us cope with our challenges.

Lessons to Learn From Onesimus’ Example¶10-11
10. What can we learn from Onesimus’ example?
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

Onesimus admitted his mistake and did his part to reconcile with Philemon. He made a costly, exhausting trip of over 1,400 kilometers (900 mi), and he had to be willing to give up his freedom and possibly accept other consequences. Even if he felt justified in running away, he did not let that stop him from doing what he could to promote peace as a spiritual brother.

💬 Comment you could share: What struck me is the courage it took. Admitting a mistake is hard, and Onesimus did it at real personal cost.

📌 Ministry connection: Those we study with may need to make costly changes; Onesimus shows that Bible truth can move a person to repair relationships.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah values a repentant heart and helps those who humbly make things right.

10 We all do and say things that offend others. Onesimus’ example teaches us how to restore peace when that happens. He did something that sounds simple but is often difficult to do. He admitted his mistake and did his part to reconcile with Philemon. Onesimus surely knew that Roman law required that he return to his master. So he made a costly and exhausting trip of over 1,400 kilometers (900 mi) to return to Philemon. He had to be willing to give up his freedom and possibly accept other consequences for his actions. That could not have been an easy choice. And what if he felt that he was justified in running away from Philemon? If he did feel that way, he did not let it stop him from returning to his master and doing what he could to promote peace between them as spiritual brothers.

11. What can we do to restore peace when we have offended someone?
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

Take the initiative. Start with a sincere apology, listen to the other person and acknowledge his feelings, accept responsibility, and avoid justifying our actions or shifting blame. Humility is often the quickest path to peace.

““If, then, you are bringing your gift to the altar and there you remember that your brother has something against you, leave your gift there in front of the altar, and go away. First make your peace with your brother, and then come back and offer your gift.” — Matt. 5:23, 24

💬 Comment you could share: I like the point that justifying ourselves can weaken an apology. A simple, sincere “I am sorry” goes a long way.

📌 Ministry connection: This applies in the ministry as well: if we were abrupt with someone, a humble follow-up can repair the impression.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah is pleased when we seek peace before offering him our worship.

11 If you have offended someone, take the initiative to restore peace. (Matt. 5:23-25) True, this may be difficult, but the resulting peace will be worth it. Start with a sincere apology. Be willing to listen to the other person and acknowledge his feelings. Accept responsibility for your mistake. Avoid justifying your actions or shifting the blame. Doing so can weaken an apology, prolong a dispute, or even make things worse. It takes humility to apologize, but that is often a quicker way to peace.—Rom. 14:19.

Matt. 5:23-25 Rom. 14:19
Lessons to Learn From Philemon’s Example¶12-18
12. Why was Paul confident that Philemon would forgive Onesimus?
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

Although the account does not say how Philemon responded, Paul knew Philemon had qualities that would incline him to forgive. The following paragraphs identify them.

“I am confident that you will comply, so I am writing you, knowing that you will do even more than what I say.” — Philem. 21

💬 Comment you could share: I found it touching that Paul expressed confidence in Philemon. Expressing trust can actually help someone live up to it.

📌 Ministry connection: We can express confidence in Bible students’ ability to progress.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah also expresses confidence in us and helps us rise to it.

12 Philemon’s example teaches us how to maintain peace when someone wrongs us. Did Philemon forgive Onesimus and receive him back kindly? The account does not say. But Paul was confident that Philemon would. (Philem. 17, 21) Paul knew that Philemon had qualities that would incline him to forgive. What qualities?

13. Why is love key to maintaining peace? (Colossians 3:14)
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

Philemon loved his brothers and lived up to his name, “Loving.” Paul described love as “a perfect bond of union,” meaning no other quality contributes more to peace and unity. The more we love others, the easier it is to forgive and to treat them kindly.

“But besides all these things, clothe yourselves with love, for it is a perfect bond of union.” — Col. 3:14

💬 Comment you could share: The way I see it, love makes the difference between merely tolerating someone and really wanting what is best for him.

📌 Ministry connection: Love for people is what motivates us to keep returning to a householder who has not responded.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah is the source of love, and our love reflects his.

13 Philemon loved his brothers. (Philem. 5) Philemon lived up to his name, which means “Loving.” He was known for his outstanding love, a quality that would be key in forgiving Onesimus. In fact, in Paul’s letter to Philemon’s congregation in Colossae, he described love as “a perfect bond of union.” (Read Colossians 3:14.) In other words, no other quality contributes more to peace and unity than love. The more we love others, the easier it becomes to forgive them and treat them kindly. (1 Cor. 13:4-7) Let us see how this worked in Philemon’s case.

Philem. 5 Col. 3:14 1 Cor. 13:4-7
14. Why can we say that Philemon was hospitable?
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

Philemon opened his home to the congregation for meetings, and Paul asked him to prepare a place for him to stay. His warm hospitality made others feel welcomed and refreshed.

“For I received much joy and comfort on hearing of your love, because the hearts of the holy ones have been refreshed through you, brother.” — Philem. 7

💬 Comment you could share: What I noticed is that hospitality in his case was ongoing. His home was a gathering place, and people left refreshed.

📌 Ministry connection: We can invite those we are studying with to simple gatherings so they feel part of the family.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah is hospitable and welcomes all who come to him.

14 Philemon had a quality that strengthens love: hospitality. He opened his home to the congregation for meetings. (Philem. 2) Paul also alluded to Philemon’s hospitality when he asked Philemon to prepare a place for him to stay. (Philem. 22) Philemon’s warm hospitality undoubtedly made others feel welcomed and refreshed.—Philem. 7.

15. How can showing hospitality strengthen our love for others?
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

We get to know our brothers and sisters better when we show them hospitality, especially those outside our circle of close friends. Learning their background, their journey into the truth, and how they endured trials deepens our love and respect and helps us see why Jehovah drew them. We then find it easier to love them, assume the best about them, and forgive them.

“No man can come to me unless the Father, who sent me, draws him, and I will resurrect him on the last day.” — John 6:44

💬 Comment you could share: I have found that an hour of sharing a meal reveals things about a person that years of greeting at the hall never do.

📌 Ministry connection: Hospitality extended to new ones and to interested people can open their hearts to the truth.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah draws individuals, and learning their stories helps us appreciate his hand in their lives.

15 How can imitating Philemon’s hospitable spirit strengthen our love for others? We get to know our brothers and sisters better when we show them hospitality. Spending time with those outside our immediate circle of friends, even those we may not initially be drawn to, helps us to appreciate the variety of personalities that make Jehovah’s people beautiful. Learning about their background, their journey into the truth, or their endurance through trials often deepens our love and respect for them. We see why Jehovah has drawn them to his family of worshippers. (John 6:44) Showing hospitality therefore helps us love our brothers and sisters more, assume the best about them, and forgive them when they make mistakes.

John 6:44
16. How may Philemon have applied Paul’s counsel found at Colossians 3:13?
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

Imagine Philemon hearing those words read in his own home. If he was harboring any bitterness toward Onesimus, the application was clear: he needed to continue putting up with Onesimus and forgive him freely.

“Continue putting up with one another and forgiving one another freely even if anyone has a cause for complaint against another. Just as Jehovah freely forgave you, you must also do the same.” — Col. 3:13

💬 Comment you could share: It made me think that Paul’s letter to the congregation worked together with his private letter. The same counsel reached Philemon from two directions.

📌 Ministry connection: We can show householders that the Bible’s counsel is practical for relationship problems.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah freely forgives, and he asks us to do likewise.

16 Are you struggling to forgive someone right now? Even if we love our brothers and sisters, forgiving them may be difficult at times. What can help? Consider Paul’s words to the congregation in Colossae, which included Philemon, recorded at Colossians 3:13. (Read.) Imagine Philemon hearing those words read to the congregation in his own home. Whom might he have been thinking about? Was he harboring bitterness toward Onesimus? If so, the application for him was clear: He needed to continue putting up with Onesimus, and he needed to forgive him freely. How can Paul’s advice guide us in our own struggles to forgive?

Col. 3:13
17. In what way should we “continue putting up with one another”?
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

We should be patient and kind to fellow believers even when they irritate or offend us. Paul pointed to Jehovah’s example: he has reasonable expectations, is aware of our limitations, and focuses on our good qualities. If we view fellow worshippers as Jehovah does, patience comes easier. Like remembering how beautiful a plant is when it blooms, remembering the good in our brothers motivates us to forgive them when they let us down.

“As a father shows mercy to his sons, Jehovah has shown mercy to those who fear him. For he well knows how we are formed, Remembering that we are dust.” — Ps. 103:13, 14

💬 Comment you could share: The plant illustration helped me. When someone irritates me, I can remind myself of their “blooming” season and be patient through the rest.

📌 Ministry connection: In the ministry we also meet people who are not “in bloom” yet; patience and remembering their potential keeps us going.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah remembers that we are dust and still shows us mercy every morning.

17 What did Paul mean by “continue putting up with one another”? He was encouraging us to be patient with and kind to fellow believers even when they irritate or offend us. In the same verse, Paul highlighted Jehovah’s heartwarming example. Jehovah has reasonable expectations for his worshippers. He is aware of our limitations but focuses on our good qualities. (Ps. 103:13, 14; 130:3) Because of that, he continues to extend his undeserved kindness and mercy to us. (Lam. 3:22, 23) If we do our best to view our fellow worshippers as Jehovah does, we will find it easier to be patient in putting up with their mistakes—just as they patiently put up with ours. To illustrate: When we remember how beautiful a plant is when it blooms, we are motivated to care for it when it is not in bloom. Similarly, if we remember the good in our brothers and sisters, we will feel motivated to forgive them and treat them kindly even when they let us down.

Col. 3:13 Ps. 103:13, 14 Ps. 130:3 Lam. 3:22, 23
18. What are you determined to do?
▸ Show comment▾ Hide comment

Look daily for opportunities to promote peace, speak kindly to and about others, apologize and make amends when we offend, and cultivate strong love that helps us forgive freely. Then we will be among the meek who will possess the earth and find exquisite delight in the abundance of peace.

“But the meek will possess the earth, And they will find exquisite delight in the abundance of peace.” — Ps. 37:11

💬 Comment you could share: I am determined to say one positive thing each day about a brother or sister, and to apologize quickly when I slip.

📌 Ministry connection: We can tell others that Bible-based peace has a wonderful future in the new world.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah promises a future with abundant peace and he helps us start building it now.

18 Jehovah appreciates everything you do to contribute to peace in your congregation. Daily look for opportunities to promote peace. Continue to speak kindly to and about others. Apologize. Make amends when you offend someone. And cultivate strong love for your brothers and sisters—the kind of love that helps you forgive freely. By doing these things, you will be a force for peace in your congregation. And you will be among the meek ones who “will possess the earth” and “find exquisite delight in the abundance of peace”—forever!—Ps. 37:11.
