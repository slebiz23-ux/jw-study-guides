Learn From "the God of All Comfort"

Watchtower Study · June 28, 2026
🎵
Opening Song 90 Encourage One Another
Focus

How to comfort others by following the examples of Jehovah and of those who imitate him.

Theme Verse
"Comfort others . . . with the comfort that we receive from God."
2 Cor. 1:4
Question 1
Why should we comfort others?

💡 Core Idea

We should comfort others because Jehovah — "the God of all comfort" — sets the example of providing comfort to all who suffer, and because we love our brothers and sisters. It's not an obligation but an expression of love.

📖 Key Scriptures

1 Pet. 1:6 — We are all distressed by various trials. Matt. 7:12 — The Golden Rule applies: we should give others what we'd want. 2 Cor. 1:3, 4 — Jehovah comforts us so we can comfort others. 1 Thess. 4:18 — We are commanded to keep comforting one another.

💬Comment you could share:

"What struck me is that the article says we comfort others not because we have to, but because we love them. That motivation makes all the difference in how naturally comfort flows from us."

📌Ministry connection:

We can point out to people in our territory that there is a God who truly comforts — and that his people are trained to reflect that comfort to others. That's a unique message.

🙏Getting to know Jehovah:

Jehovah is called "the Father of tender mercies." His comfort is tied to his personality — it's not distant or corporate, but genuinely tender and personal.

Paragraph 1

WE ARE all "distressed by various trials," so we at times need comfort and encouragement. (1 Pet. 1:6) Just as we appreciate receiving comfort from others, we should be quick to provide it to those who are suffering adversity. (Matt. 7:12) Jehovah, "the God of all comfort," shows us how to "comfort others in any sort of trial." We can follow his example by "comforting one another." (2 Cor. 1:3, 4; 1 Thess. 4:18) We do so, not because we have to, but because we love our brothers and sisters. In this article, we will consider (1) the meaning of comfort as described in the Bible, (2) how we can comfort others, and (3) what we can do when we need comfort.

Comfort as Described in the Bible
Question 2
In the Bible, what range of meanings can the word translated "comfort" have?

💡 Core Idea

The Greek word for "comfort" is broader than we might assume. It means "to call to one's side" — like a friend rushing over to help. It can also mean to encourage, exhort, or urge. Whatever form the help takes, the outcome is the same: the suffering person is built up and strengthened.

📖 Key Scriptures

Rom. 12:8 — "encourages" — same Greek root. The literal picture is a friend rushing to stand alongside someone in need.

💬Comment you could share:

"I found it interesting that the original word literally means 'to call to one's side.' That image of someone immediately coming to stand with you — not sending a text later, but coming alongside — is really moving."

🙏Getting to know Jehovah:

Jehovah is literally the one who "calls to his side" anyone who is suffering. That's an intimate, present picture of who he is — not a distant God, but one who rushes to our side.

Paragraph 2

In the Bible, the Greek word translated "comfort" can have a wide range of meanings, depending on the context. It can mean "encourage," "exhort," or "urge." (Rom. 12:8) The literal meaning of this word is "to call to one's side." So we can picture a friend coming quickly to the aid of his companion who is suffering, providing whatever help or support he needs. Regardless of the type of help that friend gives, true comfort produces the same outcome — the one suffering is built up and strengthened.

Question 3
How did Barnabas set an example in comforting others?

💡 Core Idea

Barnabas was so skilled at comforting and encouraging that his given name — Joseph — was completely replaced by the nickname "Son of Comfort." His most notable act: he trusted the newly converted Saul (Paul) when everyone else feared him, and introduced him to the apostles. Comfort sometimes requires personal courage.

📖 Key Scriptures

Acts 4:36 — "Son of Comfort." Acts 9:26–28 — Barnabas vouches for Paul despite the risk.

💬Comment you could share:

"What stood out to me about Barnabas is that his comfort often involved taking a personal risk — introducing someone unpopular, defending someone others doubted. Real comfort isn't always the safe or easy choice."

📌Ministry connection:

When we introduce ourselves to new ones in the ministry who feel like outsiders or are nervous, we're doing what Barnabas did — standing alongside them and vouching for them.

Paragraph 3

A Christian named Joseph displayed such an outstanding ability to encourage and comfort others that the apostles called him Barnabas, which means "Son of Comfort." (Acts 4:36) That name fit him so well that the Bible never again refers to him by his given name. On various occasions, Barnabas lived up to his name by coming to the aid of his fellow believers when they needed help. For instance, when the recently converted disciple Saul arrived in Jerusalem, he wanted to meet the disciples. However, they were afraid of him because of his reputation as a persecutor. But Barnabas trusted Saul and introduced him to the apostles. (Acts 9:26–28)

📷 Barnabas lived up to the meaning of his name (See paragraph 3)
Consider: Barnabas didn't just offer words — he physically placed himself between Saul and skeptical apostles. How can you similarly "stand in the gap" for someone in your congregation who feels like an outsider?
How to Comfort Others
Question 4
What is one of the best ways to provide comfort? READ: Romans 1:11, 12

💡 Core Idea

Personal visits are the most powerful form of comfort. Jehovah used an angel to visit the depressed Elijah. Paul longed to visit his brothers personally. Even when a visit isn't possible, don't underestimate a phone call, letter, card, or text.

📖 Romans 1:11, 12 (READ)

"For I am longing to see you, that I may impart some spiritual gift to you for you to be made firm; or, rather, that we may have an interchange of encouragement among you, by each one being encouraged by the other's faith, both yours and mine." — Romans 1:11, 12

📖 Other Key Scriptures

1 Ki. 19:4–7 — Angel visited and fed the depressed Elijah. 1 Cor. 16:7 — Paul wanted more than a brief interaction.

💬Comment you could share:

"I liked that Paul didn't just want to visit — he wanted mutual encouragement. He expected to receive comfort too. That's a beautiful picture of how visiting someone isn't a one-way service; it often strengthens both people."

📌Ministry connection:

Making return visits in the ministry is a form of this same principle — showing up again for someone says: "I was thinking about you. You matter."

🙏Getting to know Jehovah:

Jehovah didn't just send a message to the exhausted Elijah — he sent an angel to touch him, prepare food, and let him sleep. Jehovah's comfort is physically attentive and patient.

Paragraph 4

Visit them if possible. Jehovah uses his servants to provide comfort to others. For instance, when the prophet Elijah was depressed, Jehovah used an angel to encourage him. (1 Ki. 19:4–7) The apostle Paul also recognized the power of visiting his brothers in person to encourage them. (Read Romans 1:11, 12.) When you visit someone to provide comfort, allow time for more than just a brief interaction. (1 Cor. 16:7) If you are unable to visit someone in need, however, do not underestimate the power of a phone call, a letter, a card, or a text message to strengthen him. Will you allow Jehovah to use you to provide comfort to others?

Question 5
What challenges can we expect to face?

💡 Core Idea

As the last days intensify, comforting brothers in person will become more dangerous. Onesiphorus traveled from Ephesus to Rome to find Paul in prison — at personal risk. Brothers in Russia today visit imprisoned Witnesses in courtrooms, risking their own freedom. We should prepare ourselves spiritually for this kind of sacrificial support.

📖 Key Scriptures

2 Tim. 1:16–18 — Onesiphorus "searched diligently" for Paul and "found" him — implying real effort and risk.

💬Comment you could share:

"The example of brothers in Russia showing up in courtrooms to support persecuted Witnesses really moved me. They could lose their own freedom just by being present. That's not passive comfort — that's courageous solidarity."

🙏Getting to know Jehovah:

Jehovah trains his people collectively to face persecution together, so no one faces it alone. That communal strength is a gift he provides through the congregation.

Paragraph 5

As the last days become more critical, we can expect to face increased challenges when we try to comfort our brothers and sisters in person. During Paul's imprisonment in Rome, Onesiphorus, who was from Ephesus, searched for him at the risk of being imprisoned or even being put to death. (2 Tim. 1:16–18) Witnesses in Russia have shown a similar spirit when their brothers and sisters are being persecuted for their faith. When Witnesses are brought to trial, their brothers and sisters are present in the courtroom to show their support, even though doing so could put their own freedom at risk. Why not prepare yourself to follow their brave example by studying how God's people in ancient and in modern times have supported one another and have found joy despite persecution?

Question 6
How can we imitate Jehovah as we comfort others?

💡 Core Idea

Listen. Jehovah listened to Elijah even when Elijah repeated himself and said things that were inaccurate about his situation. Jehovah also let Habakkuk express doubts that seemed to challenge God's authority. The lesson: before we offer solutions or corrections, we should truly listen — quickly, respectfully, without interrupting.

📖 Key Scriptures

1 Ki. 19:9, 10, 14 — Elijah repeated himself and Jehovah listened both times. Hab. 1:2, 3 — Habakkuk's bold questions; Jehovah responded with patience. Jas. 1:19 — "Be quick to listen." Eccl. 7:9 — Do not be hasty to become upset.

💬Comment you could share:

"What struck me is that Jehovah listened to Elijah twice even though Elijah repeated himself almost word for word. Jehovah didn't say 'I already told you the answer.' He let Elijah process. That's a patience I want to imitate."

📌Ministry connection:

In the ministry, when a householder has repeated concerns or doubts they keep coming back to, the most comforting thing we can do is keep listening — not try to quickly "fix" them.

🙏Getting to know Jehovah:

Jehovah "listens closely to our prayers even though he already knows what we need." He doesn't skip the conversation because he has the answers. He values our expression of faith in coming to him.

Paragraph 6

Listen to them. When the prophet Elijah felt that his life was in danger, he poured out his heart to Jehovah. Our loving God listened to Elijah as he expressed his concerns even though He already knew what was troubling him. Jehovah even listened patiently as Elijah repeated himself! (1 Ki. 19:9, 10, 14) Jehovah also allowed the prophet Habakkuk to express his concerns and doubts in a way that could have seemed as if he were challenging God's authority. (Hab. 1:2, 3) Today, Jehovah listens closely to our prayers even though he already knows what we need. We can imitate Jehovah by listening carefully and respectfully when our brothers and sisters speak. "Be quick to listen" (Jas. 1:19; Eccl. 7:9) as they talk, and avoid interrupting or taking offense, even if they seem to be speaking rashly or if they are repeating themselves.

📷 Imitate Jehovah by listening carefully when your brothers and sisters speak (See paragraph 6)
Notice she's staying late — into the evening. True listening doesn't watch the clock. It creates a safe space where the person feels unhurried and truly heard.
Question 7
How can we discern the needs of someone in distress?

💡 Core Idea

We can't read hearts like Jehovah. To discern what someone actually needs, we must listen attentively — and not assume we know what they need based on what we would need in the same situation. People are different. Ask tactful questions to draw out their feelings.

📖 Key Scriptures

Prov. 20:5 — "The thoughts of a man's heart are like deep waters, but the discerning man draws them out." — Tactful questions do the drawing.

💬Comment you could share:

"I appreciated the warning not to assume you know what someone needs just because you know what you'd need. I've caught myself doing that — projecting my own needs onto others. It's a subtle mistake that can make people feel more misunderstood."

📌Ministry connection:

In the field service, we learn to ask open questions — "What do you think about...?" rather than telling people what they should believe. That same skill helps us comfort people in the congregation.

Paragraph 7

Discern what a person needs. We cannot read hearts as Jehovah does, so we can only discern what comfort a person needs if we listen to him attentively. Do not assume that you know what the person needs because you know what you would need if you were in a similar situation. We are all different. So take your time, listen carefully, and ask tactful questions that will help to draw out his feelings. (Prov. 20:5)

Question 8
How did Jesus comfort Martha and Mary after Lazarus died?

💡 Core Idea

Martha and Mary had the same loss, but Jesus comforted them differently. With Martha, he shared truths about the resurrection — she needed intellectual grounding. With Mary, who came weeping, Jesus simply wept alongside her and asked where Lazarus was — she needed emotional presence. There is no one-size-fits-all approach.

📖 Key Scriptures

John 11:20–35 — The full account. Verse 35 is famously the shortest verse in the Bible: "Jesus gave way to tears." Even knowing he would raise Lazarus, Jesus still wept with Mary.

💬Comment you could share:

"What I find remarkable is that Jesus knew he was about to raise Lazarus — yet he still cried with Mary. He didn't skip the emotion because he had the solution. He fully entered her grief. That's something I want to imitate."

🙏Getting to know Jehovah:

Jesus perfectly reflected his Father. Jehovah too meets us where we are — whether we need doctrine (like Martha) or just a presence in our grief (like Mary). He adapts to our individual need.

Paragraph 8

Consider how Jesus comforted Martha and Mary after the death of their brother, Lazarus. Both women had suffered the same loss; yet, Jesus comforted them in different ways. After Martha spoke with him, Jesus shared truths about the resurrection and helped to reinforce her faith in that loving provision. But when Mary came to him weeping, Jesus apparently did not mention the resurrection. Instead, he wept with Mary and asked where Lazarus had been laid. (John 11:20–35) The lesson? When we comfort those who are suffering, we must discern their needs rather than try to use a one-size-fits-all approach.

📷 When comforting others, strive to discern what they need (See paragraph 8)
The elder in the image approaches each widower differently — physical comfort for one, shared memories and scripture for the other. Discernment in action: watching, listening, and adapting.

In the ministry, we can offer someone a Bible verse and invite them to read it themselves — that personal discovery of scripture's comfort can be more powerful than us simply explaining it.

Paragraph 9

Use the Bible. When you share "the comfort from the Scriptures" with someone, you can help that person to gain hope. (Read Romans 15:4, 5.) That hope, in turn, provides further comfort and encouragement. (Isa. 40:31) How can you find a Bible passage to encourage someone? Some keep a list of verses that they consult when needed, such as those under the topic "Comfort" in Scriptures for Christian Living. Ask Jehovah to help you to use his Word in the best way possible in order to provide encouragement. His spirit can help you to recall appropriate scriptures when you need them. (John 14:26)

Question 10
Why must we speak kindly when we try to encourage someone?

💡 Core Idea

Sometimes comfort requires correction — but the delivery must be kind. Jehovah corrected Elijah's mistaken thinking without directly pointing out his errors. Words can heal or hurt; the same message delivered harshly can wound rather than help. Think carefully before speaking.

📖 Key Scriptures

Prov. 12:18 — "Thoughtless speech is like the stabs of a sword, but the tongue of the wise is a healing." 1 Ki. 19:15–18 — Jehovah's gentle redirection of Elijah, giving him a new assignment and revealing there were 7,000 faithful ones.

💬Comment you could share:

"The article points out that Jehovah corrected Elijah's wrong thinking without directly pointing out his errors. Instead, he gave Elijah something productive to do and expanded his view. Sometimes the kindest correction is redirecting someone toward a purpose, not confronting their mistake head-on."

🙏Getting to know Jehovah:

Jehovah's correction is never harsh. When Elijah was wrong, Jehovah's response was to give him food, rest, meaningful work, and perspective. That's a model of how comfort and gentle correction go hand in hand.

Paragraph 10

Speak kindly. The original-language word used for "comfort" can also be rendered "exhort," or "urge." At times, you might need to exhort, or urge, the person you are comforting to adjust his thinking. If that becomes necessary, think carefully before you speak so that your words heal rather than hurt. (Prov. 12:18) Jehovah set the perfect example when he spoke with Elijah, who was mistaken about the value of his work and about the scope of Israel's apostasy. Jehovah kindly helped Elijah to correct his thinking without directly pointing out his errors. (1 Ki. 19:15–18) With kindness and careful thought, you can do the same. When you give talks or make comments at Christian meetings, always speak kindly and positively as you try to encourage and strengthen your brothers and sisters.

Question 11

Words alone are often not enough. Practical help — food, labor, finances — can be the most tangible expression of love. Barnabas sold property to support struggling Christians. Today, many brothers follow his example. When actions accompany words, the comfort becomes real and memorable.

📖 1 John 3:18 (READ)

"Little children, we should love, not in word or with the tongue, but in deed and truth." — 1 John 3:18

📖 Other Key Scriptures

Acts 4:36, 37 — Barnabas sold property to help others.

📌Ministry connection:

We can offer practical help to people we meet in our territory too — connecting them with resources, following up during a crisis, or simply being present when they're dealing with something hard.

Paragraph 11

Provide practical help. Those who are in distress often need more than words of comfort. (Read 1 John 3:18.) Barnabas set a good example when he sold some property and donated the proceeds to support his newly baptized brothers and sisters who were in need. (Acts 4:36, 37) Many of our fellow Christians today follow his fine example by providing practical help to those who need it. Gabriela, from Poland, was devastated when a flood severely damaged her home. She relates: "I was really frightened and worried, and so were my parents, who lived with me. I wondered how I was going to cope. But brothers from nearby congregations came and did a lot of work in just one day. That experience strengthened my faith that Jehovah is directing his people to comfort those who are suffering."

Question 12
How can you help your fellow Christians when you faithfully endure trials?

💡 Core Idea

Our endurance itself comforts and energizes others. When Paul heard that the Thessalonians were enduring faithfully, he gained renewed energy. He also hoped that his own trials — even his imprisonment — would comfort brothers far away. Our faithful endurance is a gift we give the whole congregation.

📖 Key Scriptures

1 Thess. 3:5–8 — Paul renewed by the Thessalonians' endurance. Col. 2:1, 2 — Paul's struggles comfort brothers he hadn't even met.

💬Comment you could share:

"I hadn't thought about this before — that simply enduring a trial faithfully, without even saying a word, can be a source of comfort to others. People watch how we handle hardship. Our response either strengthens or discourages those around us."

🙏Getting to know Jehovah:

Jehovah designed the congregation so that each person's faithfulness strengthens the whole body. No one's endurance is wasted — it ripples outward in ways we may never see in this system.

Paragraph 12

Endure your trials. Paul told the Thessalonians that he gained comfort and renewed energy when he learned about their faithful endurance. He knew that his work among them had not been in vain. (1 Thess. 3:5–8) Paul, in turn, hoped to comfort his brothers — even those who had not met him — by means of his struggles in their behalf. (Col. 2:1, 2) Similarly, when our fellow Christians see how Jehovah is helping us to endure our trials successfully, they are greatly encouraged.

Question 13
What must we continue to do as we encourage others?

💡 Core Idea

Be patient. Discouraged people may not respond quickly. The phrase "keep encouraging" in 1 Thessalonians 5:11 describes an ongoing activity — not a one-time task. Don't give up on someone just because your first or second effort didn't seem to help.

📖 Key Scriptures

1 Thess. 5:11 — "Keep encouraging one another." 1 Thess. 5:14 — "Console the depressed, support the weak, be patient with everyone."

💬Comment you could share:

"The idea that 'keep encouraging' describes an ongoing activity really helped me. It's not like you comfort someone once and check it off. Some people need repeated, patient, steady presence — and that's the kind of comfort the article is describing."

Paragraph 13

Be patient. Brothers and sisters who are discouraged may not respond immediately to our efforts to comfort them. But the Scriptures admonish us to "keep encouraging one another." The expression "keep encouraging" describes an ongoing activity. (1 Thess. 5:11) Be patient as you continue to console and comfort those who need your support. (1 Thess. 5:14) Of course, at times, each of us will need to receive comfort. What can we do then?

When You Need Comfort
Questions 14–15
What should we do when we are facing times of anxiety or distress?

💡 Core Idea

Two main steps: (1) Pray — be specific, pour out your heart; (2) Ask others for help — your brothers and sisters may not know you're struggling unless you tell them.

(a) Pray to Jehovah

Be specific in your prayers. Sharing your innermost thoughts demonstrates faith. Jehovah's peace "surpasses all understanding" — it's not a feeling you manufacture; it's something he provides in response to faith-filled prayer. Ps. 94:19 Ps. 62:8 Mark 11:24 Phil. 4:6, 7

(b) Ask Others for Help

Share your feelings with a mature friend or trusted elder. People cannot read minds — they need you to open up. Ask for what you actually need: a listening ear, a scripture, an article. Prov. 14:10

💬Comment you could share:

"Philippians 4:6, 7 says the peace of God 'surpasses all understanding.' That word 'surpasses' is significant — it means it works in ways we can't fully explain or predict. Even when circumstances don't change, that peace can guard your heart. I've personally experienced that."

🙏Getting to know Jehovah:

Jehovah designed prayer as a two-way channel: we pour out our heart, and he responds with peace that transcends logic. He never ignores a sincere, faith-filled prayer.

Paragraph 14

Pray to Jehovah. Ask him to comfort you in times of anxiety or distress. (Ps. 94:19) Be specific in your prayers, pouring out your heart to him. (Ps. 62:8) Granted, Jehovah already knows how you feel even before you pray. But sharing your innermost thoughts in heartfelt prayer demonstrates your faith. And Jehovah never fails to respond in his own way to prayers offered in faith. (Mark 11:24) Philippians 4:6, 7 urges us: "Let your petitions be made known to God; and the peace of God that surpasses all understanding will guard your hearts and your mental powers." (Phil. 4:6, 7)

Paragraph 15

Ask others for help. Share your feelings with a mature friend, or confide in a trusted elder. Your brothers and sisters can be a source of comfort, but they may not know how you feel or what you need unless you tell them. (Prov. 14:10) You can ask them to listen empathetically or to share a scripture or an article that they have found comforting.

Question 16
What might happen when others try to encourage you, but what should you remember?

💡 Core Idea

Others may say or do something that unintentionally makes you feel worse. Be patient and forgiving. Remember: "no one is perfect in word." Focus on their good motives rather than their imperfect execution. "The spirit is eager, but the flesh is weak" applies to those helping us, not just to us.

📖 Key Scriptures

1 Cor. 13:4, 7 — Love is patient; love bears all things. James 3:2 — "If anyone does not stumble in word, he is a perfect man." Matt. 26:41 — "The spirit is eager, but the flesh is weak."

"The reminder that 'the spirit is eager but the flesh is weak' applies to those comforting us, not just ourselves, was really helpful. When someone says the wrong thing, they're usually trying hard. Choosing to see their good motive rather than their poor execution is an act of love."

📌Ministry connection:

We should extend the same patience to householders who react poorly to our message. They may have good questions underneath a rough exterior — just like our brothers may have good intentions underneath imperfect words.

Paragraph 16

Be patient and forgiving. Your brothers and sisters might make mistakes when they try to encourage you. For instance, they might say or do something that makes you feel worse, not better. If that happens, be patient with them. (1 Cor. 13:4, 7) And remember the words found at James 3:2: "If anyone does not stumble in word, he is a perfect man." (James 3:2) Focus on the good motives of your brothers and sisters. Bear in mind that "the spirit, of course, is eager, but the flesh is weak." (Matt. 26:41)

📋 How Would You Answer?

As described in the Bible, what is meant by "comfort"?

The Greek word translated "comfort" literally means "to call to one's side" — picturing a friend who immediately rushes to the aid of someone suffering. It can also mean to encourage, exhort, or urge. Regardless of the specific form help takes, the outcome is always the same: the suffering person is built up and strengthened. (Rom. 12:8; 2 Cor. 1:3, 4)

💬Brief personal comment:

"What I found meaningful is that 'comfort' in the Bible isn't a passive feeling — it's an active rushing-to-the-side. Jehovah is described as that kind of God. He doesn't wait for us to come fully composed; he comes alongside us in our distress."

How can we comfort others?

The article identifies six ways: (1) Visit them in person — or use calls, messages if visits aren't possible. (2) Listen attentively — as Jehovah listened to Elijah, even patiently through repetition. (3) Discern what they actually need — don't assume one approach fits all. (4) Use the Bible to bring hope. (5) Speak kindly — correct gently if needed. (6) Provide practical help — love in deed, not just word. And continue patiently, since discouraged ones may not respond immediately. (1 John 3:18; 1 Thess. 5:11)

What can we do when we need comfort?

Three steps: (1) Pray specifically to Jehovah — pour out your heart and experience his surpassing peace. (2) Ask others for help — don't assume they know what you need; be specific about what would help. (3) Be patient and forgiving when others' attempts fall short — focus on their good motives, remembering that no one is perfect in word. (Phil. 4:6, 7; Jas. 3:2)
