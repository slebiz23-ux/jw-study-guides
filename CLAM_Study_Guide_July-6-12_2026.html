⛏️ TREASURES FROM GOD'S WORD

1. Jehovah Deserves Our Obedience

Talk · 10 min.
▾
Jeremiah follows Jehovah's instructions: he holds the linen belt, travels by camel into the desert, and later leads the camel home as the sun sets.
🖼️ Image application

Jeremiah didn't wait to understand the "why" before obeying — he set out for the Euphrates simply because Jehovah said so. That willingness to act on trust, even before all the reasons are clear, is exactly the quality Jehovah is looking for in us today.

Jehovah told Jeremiah to buy a linen belt, wear it, and then travel a long distance to hide it near the Euphrates Jer 13:1-7. Only much later did Jeremiah dig it up and find it completely ruined. The instructions must have seemed strange and inconvenient, yet Jeremiah carried them out anyway. It wasn't until afterward that the lesson became clear.

Jehovah then explained the point of the illustration Jer 13:8-11: just as a belt is meant to cling closely to a person, Jehovah wanted Israel and Judah to cling closely to him as a treasured people. Because they refused to listen and chased after other gods instead, they became as useless to him as that ruined belt.

💬 Comment you could share

"I found it interesting that Jeremiah obeyed the instruction to hide the belt even though he probably had no idea what it meant at the time. It shows that real obedience doesn't wait for a full explanation."

📌 Personal application

There are congregation and family arrangements I don't always fully understand the reasoning behind. This account reminds me that I can trust Jehovah's direction even when the "why" isn't immediately obvious, the way Jeremiah did.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah

Jehovah doesn't ask for blind obedience for its own sake — he later explained the reason to Jeremiah. This shows a God who is patient and who wants his servants to understand him, not just comply with him.

🎬 Related theme

This point pairs naturally with the discussion in Living as Christians (part 7) and the video on obeying Jehovah even when we can't see him — both hinge on the same underlying quality: trust that precedes full understanding.

2. Spiritual Gems

10 min.
▾

Jer 15:16, 17 — How can we imitate Jeremiah and fight discouragement?

Jeremiah admits that his assignment left him isolated — he couldn't join in the light-hearted gatherings of the people around him, and he felt indignation because of the message Jehovah gave him to deliver. Yet in the very same verses, he says that Jehovah's words became "the exultation and the rejoicing of" his heart. Personal Bible reading was what refreshed him and kept him going even while the preaching work itself brought loneliness and rejection.

10 comment ideas for this question (pick 1–2 you can say naturally, in 30–60 seconds):

1. "What stood out to me is that Jeremiah's discouragement came from doing the right thing — from delivering an unpopular message — not from doing wrong. That helped me see that opposition to my ministry isn't a sign I'm doing something wrong."

2. "I appreciated that Jeremiah didn't hide his feelings from Jehovah. He openly admitted he sat alone and felt indignation. That tells me it's okay to be honest with Jehovah in prayer about discouragement instead of masking it."

3. "The phrase 'I ate them' about Jehovah's words really struck me — it suggests personal Bible reading isn't just information gathering, it's something that nourishes us the way food does."

4. "This reminds me that feeling isolated at times doesn't mean Jehovah has left me alone — Jeremiah still had a close relationship with him even during his loneliest moments."

5. "I can imitate Jeremiah by making sure my personal Bible reading isn't rushed — he clearly spent time letting God's words affect his heart, not just his head."

6. "Something practical I took from this is to expect that faithful service can bring some friction with others, and to not let that friction convince me something is wrong with my spirituality."

7. "I like how honest this account is — the Bible doesn't hide Jeremiah's struggle, which makes it easier for me to relate to him instead of feeling like Bible characters never struggled."

8. "This helped me see a direct connection between how much joy I get from Jehovah's word and how well I can handle discouragement in the ministry."

9. "I want to build the habit Jeremiah had — turning to Jehovah's word specifically in moments of discouragement, rather than only during my 'regular' study time."

10. "It struck me that Jeremiah's source of joy wasn't his circumstances — those were hard — but his relationship with Jehovah's word. That's an example I want to follow when things around me feel discouraging."

3. Bible Reading

Jer 13:1-14 · 4 min. (th study 2)
▾

This reading covers the linen belt account Jer 13:1-14 and a second illustration involving wine jars: just as jars are meant to be filled with wine, Judah was meant to be filled with Jehovah's blessing, but instead Jehovah warns that the nation, from the king down to the common people, would be smashed against each other like clay jars.

🎯 th study point 2 — Correct pronunciation

Watch the unfamiliar names and places in this reading (Euphrates, Jerusalem) and practice them beforehand so the reading flows smoothly without stumbling over pronunciation.

💬 Key-verse comment suggestion

Verse 11 shows the purpose behind the belt illustration — Jehovah wanted Israel and Judah to cling to him as "a name, a praise, and something beautiful." That phrase highlights how much Jehovah valued a close relationship with his people, which makes their refusal to obey all the more tragic.

4. Starting a Conversation

House to House · 3 min. (lmd lesson 1 pt. 5)
▾

Assignment: use a tract to start a conversation, house to house.

Publisher: "Hi, good afternoon. I'm visiting the neighborhood with a short message that's been an encouragement to a lot of people lately." ← lmd lesson 1 pt. 5: open with a brief, warm self-introduction that doesn't feel scripted

Householder: "Oh, okay. What's it about?"

Publisher: "It's about whether we can really trust that things will get better. This tract asks the question, 'Is there any real hope for the future?' Can I leave it with you?" ← lets the tract's question do the work of arousing interest rather than over-explaining

Householder: "Sure, I'll take a look."

Publisher: "Thank you. Actually, may I ask — when you think about the future, does it feel more hopeful or more uncertain to you?" ← follows the tract with a simple, non-threatening question to keep the conversation going

Householder: "Honestly, uncertain. There's a lot going on in the world."

Publisher: "That's exactly why I wanted to share this. The Bible actually gives a very specific reason for confidence, at Jeremiah 29:11, where Jehovah says he knows the thoughts he has for us — thoughts of peace, not calamity. We could talk more about that next time I'm in the area, if that's alright." ← ends by setting up a return visit naturally

Householder: "Yeah, that would be fine."

📌 Personal application

Keeping the opening short and letting the tract's question spark curiosity takes the pressure off me to "explain everything" in the first 30 seconds.

5. Following Up

Informal Witnessing · 4 min. (lmd lesson 9 pt. 4)
▾

Assignment: share an article from jw.org on the subject of a previous conversation, in an informal setting (e.g., with a coworker or acquaintance).

Publisher: "Hey, remember last week you mentioned you'd been feeling really stressed about work? I actually thought of you when I came across something." ← lmd lesson 9 pt. 4: reference the specific earlier conversation to show genuine personal interest

Acquaintance: "Oh yeah, it's been a rough few weeks."

Publisher: "There's an article on jw.org called 'How Can I Cope With Stress?' I pulled it up on my phone — it's short, just a couple of minutes to read. Want me to send you the link?" ← keeps it low-pressure by offering to simply send the link rather than reading it aloud on the spot

Acquaintance: "Sure, that'd be great, thanks."

Publisher: "One point that helped me is that it suggests identifying exactly what's causing the stress instead of just feeling overwhelmed by everything at once. I'll send it over — let me know what you think after you read it." ← previews one practical point and invites feedback, opening the door to a future conversation

📌 Personal application

Following up on something specific someone told me shows I was really listening, not just looking for an opportunity to preach.

7. "To Obey Is Better Than a Sacrifice"

Discussion · 15 min.
▾

These words were spoken by Samuel to King Saul after Saul disobeyed a clear, direct instruction from Jehovah delivered through the prophet.

1 Samuel 15:3, 7-9, 22, 23

How did King Saul disobey Jehovah, and for what possible reasons?
Jehovah instructed Saul, through Samuel, to completely destroy the Amalekites and all their livestock. Instead, Saul spared King Agag and kept the best of the sheep and cattle, claiming later that the animals were meant for sacrifice. Likely reasons include valuing personal gain and public approval (he "feared the people," v. 24) over strict obedience, and perhaps rationalizing that a partial obedience dressed up in religious language (sacrifice) would be acceptable.
Why is obedience to Jehovah better than a sacrifice?
A sacrifice can be offered while the heart is still self-willed, but obedience reflects genuine submission to Jehovah's authority. Samuel's words show that Jehovah values a heart that listens to him above ritual acts performed for appearance's sake or convenience.
How is rebelliousness the same as the sin of divination?
Divination looks to a source other than Jehovah for guidance and direction. When someone rebels against Jehovah's clear instructions and substitutes his own judgment instead, he is effectively doing the same thing — rejecting Jehovah as the source of direction and trusting his own reasoning instead.

Luke 16:10

Why should we obey even in seemingly minor matters?
Jesus said that whoever is faithful in what is least will be faithful also in much. Our handling of small matters reveals our underlying character and trains us in faithfulness for greater responsibilities and trust.
What might some consider to be minor matters?
Things like exact starting times for meetings and field service, small points of congregation procedure, minor counsel from an elder, or details in a work or school assignment — situations where cutting a corner seems harmless because "no one will notice."

💬 Comment you could share

"What struck me is that Saul's disobedience was dressed up as devotion — he called the spared animals a 'sacrifice.' It made me think about whether I ever rationalize doing things my own way while telling myself it's still serving Jehovah."

8. Lessons You Can Learn From the Bible — Lessons 100–101

30 min.
▾

Lesson 100 — Paul and Timothy

Timothy's mother, Eunice, and grandmother Lois, taught him about Jehovah from early childhood.
🖼️ Application

Timothy's spiritual foundation began at home, long before Paul ever met him. It's a reminder that consistent, everyday Bible teaching in the family — even from just one or two believing relatives — can produce a servant of Jehovah who is later entrusted with major responsibility.

Even while a prisoner under guard, Paul continued writing letters to encourage Timothy and the congregations.
🖼️ Application

Paul's circumstances were about as limiting as they could get, yet he still found a way to keep training and encouraging Timothy. It shows that difficult personal circumstances don't have to stop us from strengthening others.

Timothy grew up in Lystra with a Jewish mother and Greek father, and from childhood learned about Jehovah from his mother, Eunice, and grandmother, Lois. When Paul met him during his second missionary tour, he recognized Timothy's genuine love for the brothers and invited him to join the preaching work. Over the years that followed, Paul trained him carefully, and the two labored side by side through hardship — including being forced to flee persecution in Thessalonica and Beroea. Paul later relied on Timothy heavily, sending him to encourage struggling congregations and calling him a son-like companion whose concern for others was genuine rather than self-seeking.

Who was Timothy? Why were Paul and Timothy such good friends?
Timothy was a young disciple from Lystra, raised in the faith by his mother and grandmother, whom Paul invited to join his missionary journeys after observing his sincere love for the brothers. Their friendship grew out of years of shared hardship, mutual trust, and Timothy's proven, unselfish concern for the congregations — qualities Paul specifically praised, noting that Timothy genuinely cared for others' interests rather than his own. Php 2:20, 21

Lesson 101 — Paul Is Sent to Rome

Paul was escorted by Roman soldiers after appealing to Caesar, ultimately being shipped toward Rome under guard.
🖼️ Application

Paul used the legal system available to him (his right as a Roman citizen to appeal to Caesar) as a means of advancing the preaching work, rather than as a way of avoiding hardship. It's a reminder that legitimate means available to us can be used to further Kingdom interests.

After his third missionary tour ended in his arrest in Jerusalem, Paul spent two years in prison in Caesarea before appealing to Caesar and being placed on a ship bound for Rome. During the voyage a violent storm raged for fourteen days, and it looked as though everyone aboard would perish — yet an angel assured Paul that all 276 people on the ship would survive, and they did, washing ashore safely on Malta after the ship broke apart. Three months later Paul finally reached Rome, where — although still a prisoner, guarded by a soldier — he was permitted to live in a rented house for two years, receiving visitors and continuing to preach about God's Kingdom and teach about Jesus.

Why did Festus send Paul to Rome? What happened to Paul on the way to Rome?
Paul exercised his right as a Roman citizen to appeal directly to Caesar, so Festus was obligated to send him to Rome. On the journey, the ship encountered a violent 14-day storm and was wrecked off the island of Malta, yet — exactly as an angel had told Paul in advance — every one of the 276 people aboard reached shore safely. 2Co 6:4

Wrap-up questions

What does this teach us about Jehovah?
Jehovah kept his word to protect Paul and everyone with him, exactly as promised in advance, even amid a violent and life-threatening storm — showing that Jehovah's promises hold firm even when circumstances look hopeless. He also continued to use Paul productively even while Paul was a prisoner, showing that Jehovah is not limited by human restrictions when it comes to accomplishing his purpose.
Are there any points from this lesson we can use in the ministry or our daily life?
Like Timothy, we can benefit from mentors who invest time in training us, and we can look for opportunities to do the same for others. Like Paul, we can look for ways to keep preaching and encouraging others even when our circumstances are restricted or difficult, using whatever legitimate means are available to us.
🙏 Getting to know Jehovah

Both lessons show a God who works through imperfect, ordinary circumstances — a young man's upbringing, a legal appeal, even a shipwreck — to accomplish his purpose and care personally for his servants.
