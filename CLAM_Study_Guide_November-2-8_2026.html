⛏️ Treasures From God’s Word

Theme: Jehovah rightly judges proud oppressors, yet he shows abundant mercy to those who repent—and he invites us to help others benefit from that mercy.

1. Jehovah rightly condemned Babylon for her great sins and arrogant attitude

Jer. 50:14 Jer. 50:29 Jer. 50:31 (it “Presumptuousness” ¶10)

Come against Babylon in battle formation on every side, All you who are bending the bow. Shoot at her, spare no arrow, For it is against Jehovah that she has sinned.
— Jer. 50:14 NWT Study Edition
Summon archers against Babylon, All who are bending the bow. Camp all around her; let no one escape. Repay her according to her activity. Do to her just as she has done. For she has acted arrogantly against Jehovah, Against the Holy One of Israel.
— Jer. 50:29 NWT Study Edition
“Look! I am against you, O defiant one,” declares the Sovereign Lord, Jehovah of armies, “For your day must come, the time that I will call you to account.
— Jer. 50:31 NWT Study Edition

Notice the repeated reason: Babylon’s sin was “against Jehovah” and “arrogantly against Jehovah.” Her downfall was not random; it was the result of pride and of treating God’s people harshly.

Comment prep

💬 Comment you could share: I noticed that Jehovah says Babylon’s real offense was against him—she “acted arrogantly against Jehovah.” That reminds me that pride is something Jehovah takes personally.

📌 Personal application: I can ask myself whether I ever slip into a ‘Babylon-like’ attitude—thinking I don’t need guidance or correction.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah is just. He does not overlook arrogance, but he also gives long warning before acting.

2. Jehovah completely forgave repentant Israelites

Jer. 50:20

“In those days and at that time,” declares Jehovah, “Israel’s guilt will be searched for, But there will be none, And the sins of Judah will not be found, For I will forgive those whom I let remain.”
— Jer. 50:20 NWT Study Edition
Comment prep

💬 Comment you could share: What stands out to me is the phrase “there will be none” and “will not be found.” When Jehovah forgives, he doesn’t keep searching for the guilt.

📌 Personal application: If I’m weighed down by a past mistake that I’ve repented of, I can believe Jehovah’s forgiveness is complete. I can also forgive others more fully.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: He is a God who forgives “completely”—not grudgingly or partially.

Jer. 50:2 Jer. 50:8 (w08 6/15 8-9 ¶8-11)

“Declare it among the nations and proclaim it. Raise a signal and proclaim it. Do not hide anything! Say, ‘Babylon has been captured. Bel has been put to shame. Merʹo·dach has become terrified. Her images have been put to shame. Her disgusting idols have become terrified.’
— Jer. 50:2 NWT Study Edition
“Flee out of the midst of Babylon, Go out of the land of the Chal·deʹans, And be like the leading animals before the flock.
— Jer. 50:8 NWT Study Edition
Comment prep

💬 Comment you could share: Jeremiah was told to “declare it among the nations” and “not hide anything.” I see that as a picture of our own preaching work—we are helping people ‘go out’ of false religion before it’s too late.

📌 Personal application: In the ministry I can remember that the message of warning is also a message of mercy: it gives people time and a way out.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah’s warnings come from a heart that wants people to be saved, not destroyed.

📕 Congregation Bible Study

Narrative summary

Naomi lost her husband and both sons in Moab, so she returned to Bethlehem. She urged her daughters-in-law to go back to their families. Orpah left, but Ruth clung to her and chose Jehovah as her God. In Bethlehem Ruth gleaned in Boaz’s field; Boaz, a righteous man, noticed her diligence and loyalty. Following Naomi’s instructions under the Law’s provision for widows, Ruth approached Boaz. Boaz arranged matters properly before the city elders and married her. Their son Obed became an ancestor of David and of the Messiah.

Read the Bible account: Ruth 1:1–4:22 (read the full account in your Bible; key verses are quoted below)

Dig Deeper

1. How was the Israelite provision of gleaning unique in the ancient Near East? (ia 39 ¶23, ftn.)

Jehovah’s Law built compassion for the poor into everyday farming:

“‘When you reap the harvest of your land, you must not reap the edge of your field completely and you must not pick up the gleaning of your harvest.
— Lev. 19:9 NWT Study Edition
Also, you must not gather the leftovers of your vineyard or pick up the scattered grapes of your vineyard. You should leave them for the poor and the foreign resident. I am Jehovah your God.
— Lev. 19:10 NWT Study Edition
“When you reap your harvest from your field and you have forgotten a sheaf in the field, do not go back to get it. It should be left for the foreign resident, the fatherless child, and the widow, so that Jehovah your God may bless you in all that you do.
— Deut. 24:19 NWT Study Edition

The provision let the poor, the foreign resident, the fatherless and the widow gather food with dignity by working for it, as Ruth did (Ruth 2:2). The cited footnote (ia 39 ¶23, ftn.) was not retrieved; check it to add what it says about other ancient Near Eastern practice.

2. Why can we conclude that Ruth did not approach Boaz with impure motives? (ia 47 ¶17-18)

Several details show she acted according to God’s law and Naomi’s guidance, not out of immorality:

He said: “Who are you?” She replied: “I am Ruth, your servant. Spread out your garment over your servant, for you are a repurchaser.”
— Ruth 3:9 NWT Study Edition
At that he said: “May Jehovah bless you, my daughter. You have shown your loyal love more in this last instance than in the first instance, by not going after the young men, whether poor or rich.
— Ruth 3:10 NWT Study Edition
And now, my daughter, have no fear. I will do for you everything that you say, for everyone in the city knows that you are an excellent woman.
— Ruth 3:11 NWT Study Edition

Boaz called her “my daughter,” praised her “loyal love,” and said everyone knew she was “an excellent woman.” He also told her to stay the night in a way that protected her reputation and arranged things properly before the elders (Ruth 3:13). Ruth’s request to “spread out your garment” was an appeal to his role as repurchaser under the Law (Deut. 25:5).

3. What is significant about Boaz addressing Ruth as “daughter”? (Ruth 2:8; w16.11 3)
Then Boʹaz said to Ruth: “Listen, my daughter. Do not go away to glean in another field, and do not go anywhere else; stay close by my young women.
— Ruth 2:8 NWT Study Edition

Boaz was likely older than Ruth, and calling her “my daughter” expressed fatherly care and respect. It put her at ease and signaled he saw her as someone to protect, not take advantage of. He repeated it in Ruth 3:10 and Ruth 3:11. (Check w16.11 3 for additional points.)

Comment prep

💬 Comment you could share: I noticed how often Boaz used the word “daughter.” It shows that his kindness toward Ruth wasn’t flattery but fatherly care.

📌 Personal application: I can speak to younger ones in the congregation with the same warmth.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah’s servants imitate his tender care for those who seek refuge in him.

4. In contrast to the man called So-and-so, how did Boaz show unselfishness when he agreed to marry Ruth? (w23.03 14)
Then Boʹaz said: “On the day you buy the field from Na·oʹmi, you must also buy it from Ruth the Moʹab·i·tess, the wife of the dead man, in order to restore the name of the dead man to his inheritance.”
— Ruth 4:5 NWT Study Edition
To this the repurchaser said: “I am unable to repurchase it, for I may ruin my own inheritance. Repurchase it for yourself with my right of repurchase, because I am not able to repurchase it.”
— Ruth 4:6 NWT Study Edition
I am also acquiring Ruth the Moʹab·i·tess, the wife of Mahʹlon, as a wife to restore the name of the dead man to his inheritance, so that the name of the dead man will not be cut off from among his brothers and from the city gate of his home. You are witnesses today.”
— Ruth 4:10 NWT Study Edition

So-and-so was willing to buy the field, but when he realized it would also mean marrying Ruth and raising a child who would inherit in the dead man’s name, he backed out because he “may ruin my own inheritance.” Boaz put the interests of Elimelech’s family and the honor of the dead man’s name ahead of his own inheritance. See also Deut. 25:6.

Reflect on the Lessons

How might Ruth’s example of diligence and gratitude help us when we suffer financial hardship?
Ruth the Moʹab·i·tess said to Na·oʹmi: “Let me go out, please, to the fields and glean among the ears of grain behind whoever looks on me with favor.” So Na·oʹmi said to her: “Go, my daughter.”
— Ruth 2:2 NWT Study Edition
Then she took it and went into the city, and her mother-in-law saw what she had gleaned. Ruth also took out and gave to her the food that was left over after she had eaten her fill.
— Ruth 2:18 NWT Study Edition
May Jehovah reward you for what you have done, and may there be a perfect wage for you from Jehovah the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to seek refuge.”
— Ruth 2:12 NWT Study Edition

Ruth asked to work, worked from morning until just a short rest (Ruth 2:7), shared her leftover food with Naomi, and was grateful for Boaz’s kindness. She sought refuge in Jehovah.

Do not withhold good from those to whom you should give it If it is within your power to help.
— Prov. 3:27 NWT Study Edition
Comment prep

💬 Comment you could share: Ruth didn’t wait for food to come to her. She asked to glean and worked hard all day. Then Boaz noticed and blessed her.

📌 Personal application: If money is tight, I can combine hard work with trust in Jehovah, and be grateful for help.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah rewards those who take refuge under his wings.

When Naomi was brokenhearted, Ruth helped her cope. What do we learn from Naomi about humbly accepting help from others? What do we learn from Ruth about encouraging others?
She would say to the women: “Do not call me Na·oʹmi. Call me Maʹra, for the Almighty has made life very bitter for me.
— Ruth 1:20 NWT Study Edition
I was full when I went, but Jehovah made me return empty-handed. Why should you call me Na·oʹmi, when it is Jehovah who opposed me and the Almighty who caused me calamity?”
— Ruth 1:21 NWT Study Edition
At that Na·oʹmi said to her daughter-in-law: “May he be blessed by Jehovah, who has not failed in his loyal love toward the living and the dead.” Na·oʹmi continued: “The man is related to us. He is one of our repurchasers.”

Naomi first voiced her bitterness openly (above), but she later let Ruth support her and recognized Jehovah’s loyal love again (Ruth 2:20). Ruth stayed by Naomi’s side and provided for her, and the women later told Naomi that Ruth “is better to you than seven sons” (Ruth 4:15).

Comment prep

💬 Comment you could share: Naomi was honest about her pain but eventually let Ruth help her. Ruth didn’t argue with Naomi’s bitter words; she stayed loyal and kept working.

📌 Personal application: I can accept help humbly when I’m struggling, and I can encourage others by being present and practical rather than offering lots of words.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: Jehovah understands our grief and uses others to comfort us.

In what other ways might you imitate the courage of Naomi and Ruth?
Comment prep

💬 Comment you could share: Ruth’s courage was quiet: leaving her homeland, working hard, and following proper arrangements. I can imitate this by taking my stand for Jehovah even when others around me don’t.

📌 Personal application: Naomi’s courage was to be honest about her pain and still trust in Jehovah’s loyal love.

Meditate on the Bigger Picture (wrap-up questions)

1. What does this teach us about Jehovah?

Jehovah is a compassionate provider (gleaning law, Lev. 19:10), a protector of widows and foreigners, and a God of loyal love who rewards refuge-seeking (Ruth 2:12). He turned Naomi’s bitterness into joy (Ruth 4:14).

Then the women said to Na·oʹmi: “Praised be Jehovah, who has not left you without a repurchaser today. May his name be proclaimed in Israel!
— Ruth 4:14 NWT Study Edition
Comment prep

💬 Comment you could share: The women said Jehovah “has not left you without a repurchaser.” Jehovah was working behind the scenes even when Naomi felt he had opposed her.

🙏 Getting to know Jehovah: He sees our sorrow and acts in his own time.

2. Are there any points from this lesson we can use in the ministry or our daily life?

Ruth’s loyalty to Naomi’s God shows that people from any background can choose Jehovah. In the ministry we can welcome sincere newcomers who must make big changes. In daily life we can be diligent, grateful, and generous with those in need.

Comment prep

💬 Comment you could share: Ruth was a foreigner who sought refuge with Jehovah. I want to treat newcomers and those who have a difficult background with the warmth Boaz showed her.

📌 Personal application: I can look for one person this week to help in a practical way.
